A feasible point must satisfy how many inequalities in the system?	every inequality
The solution of a system of inequalities is what part of the graph?	the overlap of all solution regions
A point satisfying only one inequality is feasible?	no — both constraints are required
What is another name for the overlap of all constraints?	feasible region
If two shaded regions do not overlap, the system has what?	no feasible solution
To verify a point is feasible, what do you do?	substitute it into every inequality
For $x+y\le18$, what does 18 represent?	a maximum total
For $y\ge6$, what does 6 represent?	a minimum for y
What boundary is used for $y\lt x+3$?	dashed
What boundary is used for $y\ge-x+5$?	solid
A point on a solid boundary may be feasible?	yes
A point on a dashed boundary may be feasible?	no — dashed boundary excluded
What does nonnegativity $x\ge0$ mean in context?	x cannot be negative
What does nonnegativity $y\ge0$ mean in context?	y cannot be negative
When graphing two constraints, shade what at the end?	only the common overlap
A feasible region can contain how many points?	zero, one boundary case, or infinitely many depending on constraints
What must a contextual feasible point also satisfy?	the meanings and limits of the quantities
Why graph a system of inequalities?	to visualize all choices satisfying every constraint
