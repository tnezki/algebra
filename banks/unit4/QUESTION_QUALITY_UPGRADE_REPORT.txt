SYSTEM LESSONS
--------------
1. Variation metadata is not enough; quality must be judged from the actual
   instantiated prompt, values, representation, and expected solution.
2. Cross-form repetition can be pedagogically useful, but three- and four-form
   seed reuse can create fake abundance even when every individual form is clean.
3. Representation-rich items can often be repaired by changing the mathematical
   job supported by the existing figure, avoiding unnecessary visual churn.
4. Tarsia quality requires unique match destinations, not merely unique
   prompt-answer row pairs.
5. Small generator artifacts are worth repairing bank-wide when the correction is
   unambiguous, but secure summative architecture should remain conservative for
   the dedicated security/evidence pass.
