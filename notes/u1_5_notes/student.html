Equations as Models

Model the quantities, then interpret the solution.

What you'll learn about

...and why

Equations let you represent unknown quantities, solve for them, and explain what the result means.

Section Introduction

Turn a situation into an equation

An equation is a compact model of how quantities in a situation are related. A good model starts with a clearly defined unknown and ends with a solution that still has meaning in the original situation.

Symbols matter because they stand for real quantities in the model.

A bike rental costs $12 plus $4 per hour. A customer pays $32 total.

\(\text{Equation: }\_\_\_\_\)

Part (a)

Define a variable for the number of rental hours.

Part (b)

Use your variable from part (a) to write an equation for the total cost.

Part (c)

Solve your equation from part (b) and check the solution in the original equation.

Part (d)

Interpret the checked solution from part (c) in the situation.

I Can 1Define a variable.

A variable should name the unknown quantity clearly. Define it before writing an equation so the symbols have meaning.

Include units or a quantity description when useful: “Let h be the number of hours” is clearer than “Let x be x.”

MATH NOTESDefine the unknown
Situation: A club has a $25 setup fee plus $6 per person.
Variable: Let \(p\) = number of people.
Total: \(25+6p\)

Use the visible mathematics as evidence for the I Can statement above.

Example 1: Model it together

A streaming plan costs $9 per month plus a one-time fee. The total after 4 months is $49. Define a variable for the one-time fee.

You Try It 1: Fresh attempt

A school trip charges $18 per student plus a bus fee. The total for 20 students is $510. Define a variable for the bus fee.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What makes a variable definition useful?
  2. Why should the variable represent the requested unknown?

I Can 2Write an equation that matches a situation.

An equation states that two quantities are equal. Translate the situation by identifying the parts that make the total or balance.

Check that every number and operation in the equation has a meaning in the situation. Units and phrases such as “per,” “starts with,” and “total” can guide the structure.

MATH NOTESWrite the model

Start

$15

Per hour

$7h

Total

$50

Equation

\(15+7h=50\)

Use the visible mathematics as evidence for the I Can statement above.

Example 2: Model it together

A gym charges a $20 registration fee and $8 per visit. A member has paid $68. Write an equation for the number v of visits.

You Try It 2: Fresh attempt

A taxi fare is $5 plus $3 per mile. The fare is $29. Write an equation for miles m.

Stop and Discuss

  1. How does the word “per” appear in an equation?
  2. How can you check that an equation matches the quantities?

I Can 3Use equivalent steps to solve when needed.

Equivalent steps keep an equation balanced. When solving, undo operations in a sensible order while doing the same thing to both sides.

The goal is not to move symbols by a memorized trick; it is to preserve equality while isolating the variable.

MATH NOTESKeep the equation balanced

\(14+6x=50\)

Subtract 14 from both sides: \(6x=36\)

Divide both sides by 6: \(x=6\)

Use the visible mathematics as evidence for the I Can statement above.

Example 3: Model it together

Solve \(11+5n=41\) using equivalent steps.

You Try It 3: Fresh attempt

Solve \(7+4p=35\) using equivalent steps.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why must the same operation be applied to both sides?
  2. How can you tell which operation to undo first?

I Can 4Check and interpret a solution in context.

A solution is not finished until it is checked and interpreted. Substitute it into the original equation to verify the equality.

Then return to the variable definition. State what the number means in the situation, including units when appropriate.

MATH NOTESCheck and interpret

Solve

\(10+3h=28\) gives \(h=6\).

Check

\(10+3(6)=28\) ✓

Interpret

The rental lasted 6 hours.

Reasonableness

The result fits the context.

Use the visible mathematics as evidence for the I Can statement above.

Example 4: Model it together

A parking garage charges $6 plus $2 per hour. The total is $20. Solve \(6+2h=20\), check, and interpret h.

You Try It 4: Fresh attempt

A service charges $14 plus $5 per item. The total is $44. Solve \(14+5i=44\), check, and interpret i.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why is a correct number not a complete contextual answer?
  2. What does a failed substitution check tell you?
