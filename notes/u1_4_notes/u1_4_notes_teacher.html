Expressions and Structure

Equivalent expressions can look different and still have the same value.

What you'll learn about

Learning Target: Read, evaluate, and rewrite expressions without changing their value.

  • Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.
  • Substitute values into an expression.
  • Combine like terms.
  • Use the distributive property correctly.
...and why

Reading expression structure helps you simplify accurately, evaluate formulas, and recognize when two algebraic forms mean the same thing.

Section Introduction

See the pieces before changing the expression

An expression is built from numbers, variables, operations, and grouping symbols. Before simplifying, identify how the pieces are connected. Addition and subtraction separate terms; multiplication creates factors.

Rewriting is safe only when the new expression is equivalent—it has the same value for every allowed substitution. Combining like terms and distributing are two common ways to rewrite without changing the value.

Read the structure first; then choose a legal rewrite.

A robotics team orders 3 identical kits. Each kit contains \(x\) sensors and 2 cables, and the team also buys 4 extra sensors. The expression \(3(x+2)+4\) represents the total number of items. Use this same expression for Parts A–D.

Part A

Identify the factors in the product \(3(x+2)\) and identify the terms in the full expression.

Part B

Evaluate the original expression when \(x=5\).

Part C

Use the distributive property and combine like terms to rewrite the expression.

Part D

Use \(x=5\) to check that the original and rewritten expressions are equivalent.

Teacher support

Answer: A: factors 3 and (x+2); full terms are 3(x+2) and 4 before distributing. B: 25. C: 3x+10. D: both forms give 25 at x=5.

Reasoning: Distribution gives 3x+6, then 6 and 4 are like constant terms. Substitution provides a useful check, though one test value alone is not a proof for every expression.

Teaching move / likely issue: Students may call x and 2 “terms” of the whole expression despite the grouping; ask where the top-level addition occurs.

I Can 1Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.

A term is a part of an expression separated by addition or subtraction at the current level. A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable term. Factors are quantities being multiplied.

Structure depends on grouping. In \(4(x+3)\), 4 and \((x+3)\) are factors. Inside the parentheses, x and 3 are terms. Naming the level you are reading prevents you from mixing those roles.

MATH NOTESTerms are added; factors are multiplied

In \(5x-2y+7\): terms are \(5x\), \(-2y\), and \(7\); coefficients are 5 and -2.

In \(3(x+4)\): 3 and \(x+4\) are factors; inside the grouping, x and 4 are terms.

Example 1: Name the structure

For \(6x-3y+10\), list the terms and identify the coefficients of the variable terms.

Teacher support

Answer: Terms: 6x, -3y, 10. Coefficients: 6 and -3.

Reasoning: The subtraction sign belongs with the following term, so the second term is -3y.

Teaching move / likely issue: Do not let students drop the sign from a coefficient.

You Try It 1: Read grouping

In \(4(2x-5)\), identify the two factors in the outer product and the two terms inside the parentheses.

Teacher support

Answer: Outer factors: 4 and (2x-5). Inner terms: 2x and -5.

Reasoning: The outer operation is multiplication; the grouping contains a subtraction.

Teaching move / likely issue: Ask students to say which operation they are reading at each level.

Stop and Discuss

  1. How can parentheses change what counts as a factor or term at a particular level?
  2. Why should a negative sign stay attached when you name a term or coefficient?
Teacher support: Listen for explanations that cite the representation, operation, or pattern rather than only the final answer.

I Can 2Substitute values into an expression.

To substitute, replace a variable with a given value while preserving the expression’s operations. Parentheses make the replacement visible: if \(x=-3\), write \(2(-3)^2+1\) before calculating.

After substitution, the task becomes numerical evaluation. Follow order of operations: grouping and exponents before multiplication/division, then addition/subtraction.

MATH NOTESSubstitute first, then evaluate

Evaluate \(2x^2-3\) at \(x=-2\):

\(2(-2)^2-3=2(4)-3=5\)

Parentheses protect the substituted value and its sign.

Example 2: Evaluate with substitution

Evaluate \(3a^2-2a+4\) when \(a=-1\). Show the substituted expression before simplifying.

Teacher support

Answer: 9.

Reasoning: \(3(-1)^2-2(-1)+4=3+2+4=9\).

Teaching move / likely issue: Look for students treating (-1)^2 as -1.

You Try It 2: Evaluate another expression

Evaluate \(5-2m+3m^2\) when \(m=2\). Show the substituted expression first.

Teacher support

Answer: 13.

Reasoning: \(5-2(2)+3(2)^2=5-4+12=13\).

Teaching move / likely issue: Order of operations: exponent before the multiplication by 3.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why is it useful to write parentheses around a substituted negative value?
  2. At what point does an algebraic expression become an ordinary numerical expression?
Teacher support: Listen for explanations that cite the representation, operation, or pattern rather than only the final answer.

I Can 3Combine like terms.

Like terms have the same variable part, including the same exponents. Their coefficients can be combined because they count the same kind of quantity.

Terms such as \(3x\) and \(-5x\) are like terms, but \(3x\) and \(3x^2\) are not. Constants are like other constants. Combining like terms changes the form, not the value.

MATH NOTESCombine only matching variable parts

Like

\(4x-7x=-3x\)

\(5+9=14\)

Not like

\(x\) and \(x^2\)

\(2a\) and \(2b\)

Example 3: Combine like terms

Simplify \(7x+3-2x+5\). Show which terms you combine.

Teacher support

Answer: 5x+8.

Reasoning: Combine 7x-2x and 3+5 separately.

Teaching move / likely issue: Students may incorrectly combine coefficients and constants into 13x.

You Try It 3: Simplify with negatives

Simplify \(4y-6+9y-2\).

Teacher support

Answer: 13y-8.

Reasoning: 4y+9y=13y and -6-2=-8.

Teaching move / likely issue: Check that both negative constants are preserved.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What must match before two variable terms can be combined?
  2. Why does combining like terms preserve the value of the expression?
Teacher support: Listen for explanations that cite the representation, operation, or pattern rather than only the final answer.

I Can 4Use the distributive property correctly.

The distributive property connects multiplication over addition or subtraction: \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\). The outside factor must multiply every term inside the grouping.

A negative outside factor changes signs as it multiplies. After distributing, look again for like terms. Distribution and combining like terms often work together to create an equivalent expression.

MATH NOTESThe outside factor reaches every term

\(3(2x-5)=3(2x)+3(-5)=6x-15\)

\(-2(x+4)=-2x-8\)

Multiply every term inside the grouping—not just the first one.

Example 4: Distribute correctly

Rewrite \(5(2x-3)+4x\) without parentheses and simplify.

Teacher support

Answer: 14x-15.

Reasoning: Distribute: 10x-15+4x, then combine like x-terms.

Teaching move / likely issue: A common error is 10x-3+4x; require the 5 to multiply -3.

You Try It 4: Distribute a negative factor

Rewrite \(-3(x-4)+2x\) without parentheses and simplify.

Teacher support

Answer: -x+12.

Reasoning: Distribute -3 to both terms: -3x+12+2x, then combine.

Teaching move / likely issue: Watch the sign: (-3)(-4)=+12.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What visual or verbal reminder helps you distribute to every term?
  2. Why can a negative factor change more than one sign inside a grouping?
Teacher support: Listen for explanations that cite the representation, operation, or pattern rather than only the final answer.

Vocabulary Reference

TermMeaning in this section
termA part of an expression separated by addition or subtraction at the level being considered.
coefficientA numerical factor multiplying a variable term.
factorA quantity multiplied by another quantity.
equivalent expressionAn expression with the same value as another for every allowed input.
distributive propertyThe rule that multiplies a factor across each term in a sum or difference, such as \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\).

Section Summary

  • Read grouping and operations before changing an expression.
  • Substitute values with parentheses and then use order of operations.
  • Combine only like terms.
  • Distribute an outside factor to every term inside the grouping.

MistakeWhat to remember

Dropping a negative coefficientKeep the sign attached to the term.
