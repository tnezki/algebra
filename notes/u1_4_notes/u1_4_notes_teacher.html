Expressions and Structure

See the structure before changing the expression.

What you'll learn about

Learning Target: Read, evaluate, and rewrite expressions without changing their value.

  • Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.
  • Substitute values into an expression.
  • Combine like terms.
  • Use the distributive property correctly.
...and why

Structure tells you how an expression is built. Seeing that structure makes evaluation and rewriting more reliable and prepares you to solve more complicated equations later.

Section Introduction

Expressions have parts and structure

An expression is built from terms, factors, numbers, variables, and operations. Reading that structure correctly matters: a coefficient multiplies a variable term, while factors are quantities multiplied together inside a product.

Rewriting is useful only when the value stays the same. Substitution can check a value, like terms can be combined, and the distributive property can remove grouping while preserving equivalence.

Every rewrite should preserve the value of the original expression.

I Can 1Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.

Terms are the pieces of an expression separated by addition or subtraction at the outer level. In 6x² - 3x + 8, the terms are 6x², -3x, and 8.

A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable part. Factors are quantities multiplied together, so in 4(x+2), the factors are 4 and (x+2). Reading these parts first helps you decide which algebra move is valid.

MATH NOTESRead the parts before operating

Terms

6x² | -3x | +8

Coefficients

6 is the coefficient of x²; -3 is the coefficient of x.

Factors

In 4(x+2), 4 and (x+2) are factors.

Structure clue

Parentheses group terms before distribution.

Identify the operation that connects the parts before deciding what each part is called.

Example 1: Name the structure

In 9a - 4b + 7, list the terms and identify the coefficients of a and b.

Answer / reasoning: Terms: 9a, -4b, 7. Coefficients: 9 and -4.
Teacher move: Ask the student to point to the exact step or representation that justifies the answer.

You Try It 1: Find factors

In -3(2m + 5), identify the two main factors and the terms inside the parentheses.

Answer / reasoning: Main factors: -3 and (2m+5). Terms inside: 2m and 5.

I Can 2Substitute values into an expression.

Substitution replaces a variable with a given value. Parentheses are especially important when the substituted value is negative because they preserve the intended operation.

After substituting, evaluate using the order of operations. A substitution check can also test whether two expressions are equivalent at a chosen value, although checking one value alone does not prove equivalence for every value.

MATH NOTESSubstitute, then evaluate

Expression: 3a² - 2b

Let a = 2 and b = -1.

3(2)² - 2(-1) = 3·4 + 2 = 14

Put substituted negative values in parentheses before applying operations.

Example 2: Evaluate with two variables

Evaluate 2p² - 3q for p = -2 and q = 4.

Answer / reasoning: 2(-2)^2 - 3(4) = 8 - 12 = -4.
Teacher move: Ask the student to point to the exact step or representation that justifies the answer.

You Try It 2: Check an expression value

Evaluate 5 - 2r + s for r = -3 and s = 4.

Answer / reasoning: 5 - 2(-3) + 4 = 5+6+4=15.
Teacher move: If work stalls, ask what the input/structure/evidence tells them to do first rather than supplying the next operation.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why are parentheses useful when substituting a negative number?
  2. What part of order of operations becomes especially important when exponents are present?
Discussion move: Ask a second student to restate the reasoning using input/output language.

I Can 3Combine like terms.

Like terms have the same variable part, including the same exponents. Their coefficients can be added or subtracted because the terms represent the same kind of quantity.

Constants are like terms with other constants. Terms such as 3x and 3x² are not like because their variable parts differ. Keep unlike terms separate.

MATH NOTESCombine only like terms

7x - 3 + 2x + 8

7x + 2x + (-3 + 8)

= 9x + 5

Group by matching variable part, then combine coefficients.

Example 3: Combine two groups

Simplify 7x - 3 + 2x + 8.

Answer / reasoning: 7x+2x=9x and -3+8=5, so 9x+5.
Teacher move: Ask the student to point to the exact step or representation that justifies the answer.

You Try It 3: Combine with negatives

Simplify -4p + 6 + 9p - 2.

Answer / reasoning: -4p+9p=5p and 6-2=4, so 5p+4.
Teacher move: If work stalls, ask what the input/structure/evidence tells them to do first rather than supplying the next operation.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why can 4x and 6x be combined but 4x and 6x² cannot?
  2. How can rearranging terms help you simplify without changing value?
Discussion move: Have students compare two methods and identify what information stayed unchanged.

I Can 4Use the distributive property correctly.

The distributive property connects multiplication and addition: a(b+c)=ab+ac. The outside factor multiplies every term inside the parentheses.

Distribution can be followed by combining like terms. Keep the two moves separate in your written work so you can see whether every inside term was multiplied and whether only like terms were combined.

MATH NOTESDistribute to every term

5(2x - 3) - 4x

→ 10x - 15 - 4x

→ 6x - 15

Check at x=2: original = -3; simplified = -3.

Distribute first; then combine like terms if any remain.

Example 4: Distribute and simplify

Rewrite 5(2x - 3) - 4x as an equivalent expression.

Answer / reasoning: 5(2x-3)=10x-15; then 10x-4x=6x, so 6x-15.
Teacher move: Ask the student to point to the exact step or representation that justifies the answer.

You Try It 4: Distribute a negative factor

Rewrite -3(2m + 5) + m as an equivalent expression.

Answer / reasoning: -6m-15+m=-5m-15.
Teacher move: If work stalls, ask what the input/structure/evidence tells them to do first rather than supplying the next operation.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What common error happens when a negative factor is distributed?
  2. How can substitution be used as a quick check after a rewrite?
Discussion move: Use one incorrect example to surface the misconception before moving on.

Section Summary

  • Terms are separated by addition or subtraction; coefficients multiply variable parts; factors are multiplied quantities.
  • Substitution replaces a variable with a given value before evaluating.
  • Like terms can be combined because they have the same variable part.
  • The distributive property multiplies an outside factor by every term inside the group.

Common Mistakes to Watch

MistakeWhat to remember
Combining unlike terms.Only terms with the same variable part can be combined.
Distributing to only one term.Multiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses.
Dropping a negative sign during substitution.Use parentheses around a substituted negative value before evaluating.
Confusing coefficients and exponents.A coefficient multiplies a term; an exponent describes repeated multiplication of a factor.
