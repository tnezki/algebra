Read the pieces, preserve value, and rewrite with purpose.

What you'll learn about

  • Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.

...and why

Expression structure lets you calculate accurately and rewrite algebra into forms that are easier to understand, compare, or use in equations.

Section Introduction

See the structure before you simplify

An expression is built from terms and factors. Reading those pieces correctly prevents many common algebra mistakes.

Substitution tests what an expression is worth for a particular variable value. Combining like terms and distributing create equivalent expressions without changing the value.

Keep operations visible and use equivalence as the reason for every rewrite.

I Can 1Identify terms, coefficients, and factors.

Terms are the major pieces of an expression separated by addition or subtraction. In \(7x-3+2y\), the terms are \(7x\), \(-3\), and \(2y\).

A coefficient is the numerical factor attached to a variable term. Factors are quantities being multiplied, so in \(5(2x+1)\), 5 and \((2x+1)\) are factors of the product.

Reading structure before calculating helps you decide which operations are actually present and which pieces may be combined later.

MATH NOTESName the pieces of an expression

Expression: \(6a-4+3b\)

Terms: \(6a, -4, 3b\)

Coefficients: 6 and 3

In \(2(3a-5)\), the outside factor is 2 and the grouped factor is \((3a-5)\).

Connect the operation or feature back to the relationship you are describing.

Example 1: Read expression structure

For \(9x+4-2y\), list the terms and identify the coefficients of the variable terms.

I Can 2Substitute values into an expression.

Substitution replaces a variable with a specified value while keeping the rest of the expression unchanged. Write the replacement in parentheses so the original operation is visible.

After substitution, evaluate using the usual order of operations. If more than one variable is present, replace every named variable before calculating.

A substitution check is also useful for testing whether two expressions are equivalent at a particular value, though one matching value alone does not prove equivalence for all values.

MATH NOTESSubstitute before simplifying

Evaluate \(3x^2-2x+1\) at \(x=-2\).

\(3(-2)^2-2(-2)+1=3(4)+4+1=17\).

Connect the operation or feature back to the relationship you are describing.

Example 2: Substitute and evaluate

Evaluate \(2n^2+5\) when \(n=-3\).

You Try It 2: Substitute and evaluate

Evaluate \(4a-3b\) when \(a=5\) and \(b=-2\).

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why should negative substituted values be placed in parentheses?
  2. What must happen before applying order of operations?
  3. Can one matching substitution prove two expressions are equivalent? Why not?

I Can 3Combine like terms.

Like terms have exactly the same variable part. Their coefficients may differ, but the variables and exponents must match.

Combining like terms adds or subtracts the coefficients while preserving the common variable part. Constants combine with constants.

Unlike terms stay separate. Rewriting an expression by combining only like terms preserves its value and creates an equivalent expression.

MATH NOTESCombine coefficients, preserve variable parts

\(8x+3-5x+7\)

\((8x-5x)+(3+7)=3x+10\)

But \(3x+10\) cannot be combined further because \(3x\) and 10 are unlike terms.

Connect the operation or feature back to the relationship you are describing.

Example 3: Combine like terms

Simplify \(11p-4+3p+9\).

You Try It 3: Combine like terms

Simplify \(6a+2b-4a+5b\).

Stop and Discuss

  1. What has to match for two terms to be like terms?
  2. Why can constants combine with each other?
  3. What error occurs if you combine \(x\)-terms with \(x^2\)-terms?

I Can 4Use the distributive property correctly.

The distributive property replaces a product of a factor and a sum or difference with the sum or difference of products. The outside factor multiplies every term inside the parentheses.

Keep signs attached to terms. If the outside factor is negative, each inside term changes accordingly after multiplication.

After distributing, look again for like terms. Distribution and combining like terms are separate moves, and doing them in that order keeps the structure clear.

MATH NOTESMultiply every term in the group

\(5(3x+2)-2x\)

\(=15x+10-2x\)

\(=13x+10\)

The factor 5 multiplies both \(3x\) and 2.

Connect the operation or feature back to the relationship you are describing.

Example 4: Distribute to every term

Simplify \(4(2x-3)+x\).

You Try It 4: Distribute to every term

Simplify \(-3(2m+5)+7m\).

Stop and Discuss

  1. What does the outside factor multiply?
  2. Why should signs be kept with their terms?
  3. How can substitution be used to check a distributed expression?

Section Summary

  • Terms are separated by addition or subtraction; coefficients are numerical factors.
  • Substitution replaces variables with given values before evaluating.
  • Like terms share the same variable part and can be combined.
  • Distribution multiplies the outside factor by every term in the group.
