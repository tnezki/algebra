Reading: Read the parts of an expression before rewriting

An algebraic expression has parts that signal its structure. A term is a piece separated by addition or subtraction at the outer level. A coefficient is a numerical multiplier attached to a variable term, and a factor is a quantity being multiplied by another quantity.

An equivalent expression may look different but has the same value for every allowed substitution. Rewriting is useful because one form may make a pattern, total, factor, or calculation easier to see than another form.

The distributive property connects multiplication and addition: $a(b+c)=ab+ac$. The outside factor multiplies every term inside the grouping symbols. After distributing, like terms can be combined only when their variable parts match.

Stop and Discuss

  1. In $3(x+4)+2x$, identify a factor and two like terms that appear after distributing.
  2. Why must the outside factor multiply every term inside parentheses?

Reading: Use an area model to organize partial products

A rectangle model makes distribution visible as area. If one side is split into $x$ and $3$ while the other is split into $x$ and $2$, the large rectangle is partitioned into four smaller rectangles. Each small area is a partial product.

Those areas are $x^2$, $2x$, $3x$, and $6$. Adding them gives $x^2+2x+3x+6=x^2+5x+6$. The area model and symbolic distribution describe the same multiplication structure.

A model is most useful when labels are connected to the algebra. Read the side lengths first, multiply each row-column pair, then combine like terms only after all partial products have been recorded.

Stop and Discuss

Math NotesArea model as distribution

Each cell represents one product. The sum of all cell areas equals the product represented by the entire rectangle.

Reading: Verify equivalence instead of trusting appearance

Algebraic properties can prove that two expressions are equivalent. Distribute, regroup, and combine like terms until both expressions reach the same form. This checks equivalence structurally rather than from a few examples.

Substitution is also a useful check. Choose a convenient value for the variable and evaluate both expressions. If the values differ, the expressions are definitely not equivalent. Matching at one value is evidence, but it is not a proof that they match for every value.

Error analysis focuses on the exact step where value changed. Common errors include distributing to only one term, losing a negative sign, or combining unlike terms. Writing one algebraic step per line makes those changes easier to inspect.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What can one failed substitution prove about two expressions?
  2. Why does one successful substitution not prove equivalence for every value?

Vocabulary / Key Terms — Definitions

TermMeaning

equivalent expressionan expression with the same value as another for every allowed substitution
distributive propertythe rule that multiplies a factor across every term in a sum or difference

Summary

  • Expression structure tells you which operations and groupings matter.
  • The distributive property multiplies the outside factor by every term inside the group.
  • Area models organize partial products and connect visual structure to symbolic expansion.
  • Equivalence can be established by valid algebraic properties and checked with substitution.
