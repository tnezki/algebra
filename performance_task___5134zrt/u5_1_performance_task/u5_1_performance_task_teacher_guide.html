Evidence Focus
  1. I can construct equivalent polynomial expressions by factoring out a greatest common factor.
  2. I can apply structure to rewrite polynomial expressions in a more useful form.
  3. I can determine whether a rewritten expression is reasonable by checking common factors and equivalent products.

A rectangular display has total area 12x^2 + 18x. Find a shared dimension and use it to rewrite the area structurally. Work so another person can follow the mathematical evidence you used.

Situation / Starting Evidence

Total area: 12x^2 + 18x
Pencil, calculator if normally allowed

Find the shared structure

Identify the greatest common factor of both terms, including the variable factor.

Teacher evidence / sample: GCF is 6x.

Build dimensions

Write the area as a product and interpret the two factors as possible side lengths.

Teacher evidence / sample: 6x(2x+3). Sides can be 6x and 2x+3.

Stress-test an incorrect factor

A student writes 3x(4x+6). Explain why it is equivalent but does not factor out the greatest common factor.

Teacher evidence / sample: It expands correctly, but 4x+6 still has common factor 2; 6x is the GCF and gives a more fully factored form.
