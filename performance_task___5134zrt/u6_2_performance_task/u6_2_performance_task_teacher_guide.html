Evidence Focus
  1. I can build perfect-square trinomials by adding the needed constant term.
  2. I can use completing the square to solve quadratic equations and write equivalent vertex-form expressions.
  3. I can predict how completing the square reveals the vertex and symmetry of a quadratic function.

A student tries to rewrite x^2+6x-7=0 by completing the square but loses equivalence. Work so another person can follow the mathematical evidence you used.

Situation / Starting Evidence

Student work: x^2+6x=7 -> x^2+6x+9=7 -> (x+3)^2=7
Pencil, calculator if normally allowed

Locate the broken step

Identify exactly what must happen when 9 is added to the left side.

Teacher evidence / sample: Add 9 to both sides: x^2+6x+9=16.

Repair and solve

Complete the square correctly and solve the equation.

Teacher evidence / sample: (x+3)^2=16 -> x+3=+/-4 -> x=1 or x=-7.

Connect to vertex form

Rewrite y=x^2+6x-7 in vertex form and identify the vertex.

Teacher evidence / sample: y=(x+3)^2-16; vertex (-3,-16).

Verify the solutions

Check both solutions in the original equation or connect them to intercept symmetry.

Teacher evidence / sample: 1 and -7 both make x^2+6x-7=0; they are symmetric about x=-3.
