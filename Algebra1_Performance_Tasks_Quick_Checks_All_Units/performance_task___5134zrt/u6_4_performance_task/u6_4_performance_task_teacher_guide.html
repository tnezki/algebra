Section 6.4

Evidence Focus
  1. I can formulate a solution-method plan based on the structure of a quadratic equation.
  2. I can use factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula when the equation structure supports the method.
  3. I can critique a selected solution method for efficiency, accuracy, and fit to the equation.

You are the triage lead for four quadratic equations. Choose a method for each, solve selected cases, and defend efficiency. Work so another person can follow the mathematical evidence you used.

Situation / Starting Evidence

A: x^2-9=0   B: x^2+7x+10=0   C: x^2+6x+1=0   D: 3x^2+2x-7=0
Pencil, calculator if normally allowed

Assign methods

Choose an efficient method for each equation: factoring/square-root structure, completing the square, or quadratic formula.

Teacher evidence / sample: A square roots/difference squares; B factoring; C completing square is natural; D quadratic formula is efficient. Other defensible choices accepted.

Solve two contrasting cases

Solve B and D with your selected methods.

Teacher evidence / sample: B=(x+5)(x+2)=0 -> -5,-2. D: x=(-2+/-sqrt(88))/6=(-1+/-sqrt22)/3.

Critique a method claim

A student says the quadratic formula is always the best method because it always works. Evaluate the claim.

Teacher evidence / sample: It works broadly but may be inefficient; structure can make factoring or square roots faster and clearer.

Write a decision rule

Create a short method-selection rule based on visible equation structure, not on memorizing one universal method.

Teacher evidence / sample: Strong rule checks zero-product/factorability or square structure first, then completing square/vertex need, otherwise formula; exact wording varies.
