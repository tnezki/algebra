Section 5.4

Evidence Focus
  1. I can formulate quadratic functions in factored form from zeros or intercepts.
  2. I can use factored form to predict zeros and x-intercepts of quadratic graphs.
  3. I can extend connections among factors, zeros, intercepts, and graph behavior.

A quadratic graph crosses the x-axis at x=-2 and x=5 and passes through (0,-20). Reconstruct a factored equation and use it to answer graph questions. Work so another person can follow the mathematical evidence you used.

Situation / Starting Evidence

Zeros: -2 and 5. Point on graph: (0,-20).
Pencil, calculator if normally allowed

Build the factors

Write the factor structure implied by the zeros, including an unknown scale factor a.

Teacher evidence / sample: f(x)=a(x+2)(x-5).

Determine the scale

Use (0,-20) to find a and write the final equation.

Teacher evidence / sample: -20=a(2)(-5)=-10a, so a=2. f(x)=2(x+2)(x-5).

State the x-intercepts and opening direction. Find the y-intercept from your equation.

Teacher evidence / sample: x-intercepts (-2,0),(5,0); opens up because a=2>0; y-intercept -20.

Audit a reverse claim

A student says f(x)=2(x-2)(x+5) has the same zeros. Critique the claim.

Teacher evidence / sample: That equation has zeros 2 and -5, not -2 and 5; sign inside factor reverses the zero value.
