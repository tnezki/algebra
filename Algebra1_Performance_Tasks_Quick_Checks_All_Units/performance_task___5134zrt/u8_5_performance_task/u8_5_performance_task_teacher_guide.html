A community project compares three donation-growth models over weeks: A linear, B quadratic, and C exponential. Choose representations strategically to answer different questions. Work so another person can follow the mathematical evidence you used.

Situation / Starting Evidence

A(w)=200+80w    B(w)=200+20w^2    C(w)=200(1.18)^w
Pencil, calculator if normally allowed

Choose efficient representations

For comparing early weeks, finding a turning point, and estimating long-term growth, state which representation (table, graph, equation) you would prioritize and why.

Teacher evidence / sample: Answers vary; must tie representation to information needed. Equations reveal structure; tables compare common inputs; graphs show overall intersections/trends.

Build a common-input comparison

Compute all three models at w=0,2,4,6.

Teacher evidence / sample: A:200,360,520,680. B:200,280,520,920. C:200,~278.5,~387.8,~539.8.

Resolve competing claims

Claim 1: “Quadratic is best because it is largest at week 6.” Claim 2: “Exponential must always win.” Evaluate both claims using domain and evidence.

Teacher evidence / sample: Claim 1 depends on goal/time horizon; week 6 B is largest. Claim 2 is not automatically true at every finite input; exponential may eventually dominate but parameters and domain matter.

Recommend and revise

Recommend a model if the project only lasts 6 weeks. Then state what new evidence would make you revisit your choice.

Teacher evidence / sample: For maximizing week-6 total with given formulas, B. Revisit if constraints, data fit, or time horizon changes; recommendation must match stated objective.
