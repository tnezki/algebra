Algebra 1 · Section 7.2 Demo Teacher Guide

A quantity grows 8% each year. Is the growth factor 0.08, 1.08, or 8?

Writing Exponential Equations

Why This Fits

This error or contradiction reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will write exponential equations from tables, graphs, and situations.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Table: a=120, b=1.25, so y=120(1.25)^x.
  • Growth: y=900(1.06)^t.
  • Decay: y=1400(0.82)^t.

Watch For

  • Using the percent change itself as the multiplicative factor.
  • Switching the roles of initial value and growth/decay factor.

Strong Synthesis

a is the value when x=0; b is the constant multiplicative factor for each one-unit increase in x.
Choose

A quantity grows 8% each year. Is the growth factor 0.08, 1.08, or 8?

Apply each proposed factor to an initial value of 100 for one year.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Table: a=120, b=1.25, so y=120(1.25)^x.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Reveal

Test the claim

8% growth means keep 100% and add 8%, so multiply by 1.08.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. What does a represent in y=a(b)^x?

2. Why is 0.92 a factor for 8% decay?

3. How can a table reveal b?
