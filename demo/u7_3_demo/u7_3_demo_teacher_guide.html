Which grows faster: 1.2^x or 2^x? What about (0.5)^x?

Graphing and Analyzing Exponential Functions

Why This Fits

This graph or data reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will graph exponential functions and analyze key features.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • All equal 1 at x=0.
  • 2^x grows faster than 1.5^x for positive x.
  • (0.5)^x decays toward 0 as x increases and is always positive.

Watch For

  • Drawing an exponential graph through its horizontal asymptote.
  • Treating end behavior as linear or ignoring the effect of the growth/decay factor.

Strong Synthesis

For b>1 the function grows; for 0<b<1 it decays. Values closer to 1 change more slowly per step.

Reveal

Compare with the mathematical model

Generated graphs confirm faster growth for larger bases above 1 and decay for bases between 0 and 1.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why do all three basic graphs pass through (0,1)?

2. What makes a base represent decay?

3. What does the horizontal asymptote mean?
