Who is farther from x=3: a student at x=-1 or a student at x=7?

Absolute Value Functions and Piecewise Thinking

Why This Fits

This human movement or position model creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will analyze and model situations involving absolute value functions.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Outputs: 4,3,2,1,0,1,2,3,4.
  • Vertex is (3,0); graph is symmetric about x=3.
  • Left piece decreases with slope -1 as x approaches 3; right piece increases with slope +1.

Watch For

  • Treating absolute value as ordinary linear behavior across the vertex.
  • Ignoring interval conditions in a piecewise rule.

Strong Synthesis

The vertex marks zero distance at the target; equal horizontal offsets left/right produce equal nonnegative distances, creating two linear pieces with opposite slopes.
