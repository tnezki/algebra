Algebra 1 · Section 7.1 Demo Teacher Guide

Which account grows faster in the long run: +\$50 each month or ×1.10 each month?

Recognizing Growth and Decay

Why This Fits

This quick class vote or prediction creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will identify exponential relationships from patterns, tables, graphs, and situations.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Additive pattern: 20,30,40,50,60,70 with constant difference 10.
  • Multiplicative: 20,30,45,67.5,101.25,151.875 with constant ratio 1.5.
  • A decay example with factor 0.8 decreases by 20% each step.

Watch For

  • Using constant differences to justify an exponential relationship.
  • Confusing a decay factor with the percent decrease.

Strong Synthesis

The multiplicative ratio/factor stays constant for equal input intervals; in a linear relationship the additive difference stays constant.
Choose

Which account grows faster in the long run: +\$50 each month or ×1.10 each month?

Both start at \$500; students predict values after 1, 3, and 10 months qualitatively.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. What stays constant in linear growth?

2. What stays constant in exponential growth?

3. Why is a decay factor like 0.8 not an 80% decrease?

We will identify exponential growth and decay from constant multiplicative change.
