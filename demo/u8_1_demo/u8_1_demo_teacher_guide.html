Selecting Appropriate Models

Why This Fits

This error or contradiction reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will determine whether a situation is best modeled by a linear, quadratic, or exponential function.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A linear: first differences +4.
  • B quadratic: first differences 3,5,7; second differences +2.
  • C exponential: ratio ×1.5.

Watch For

  • Choosing a model from one or two points without checking the pattern of change.
  • Using additive evidence to justify an exponential model or ratio evidence to justify a linear model.

Strong Synthesis

Check constant first differences for linear, constant second differences for quadratic, and constant ratios for exponential; then validate with graph/context behavior and domain.
