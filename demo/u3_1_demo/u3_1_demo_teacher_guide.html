Do two linear equations always meet exactly once?

Graphing and Elimination

Why This Fits

This quick class vote or prediction creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will solve systems graphically and by elimination while interpreting the meaning of different solution types.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A has one solution; solving gives (2,3).
  • B has no solution because equal slopes and different intercepts are parallel.
  • C has infinitely many solutions because the second equation is twice the first.

Watch For

  • Assuming every system has exactly one solution.
  • Eliminating terms without applying equivalent operations to an entire equation.

Strong Synthesis

A unique ordered pair corresponds to one intersection; a false statement corresponds to parallel distinct lines; an identity corresponds to equivalent equations/same line.
Choose

Do two linear equations always meet exactly once?

Three pairs of lines are described: different slopes, same slope/different intercepts, and equivalent equations.
