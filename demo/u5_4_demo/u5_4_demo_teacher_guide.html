Factored Form and Zeros

Why This Fits

This graph or data reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will connect factors, zeros, intercepts, and graphs of quadratic functions.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A zeros are 2 and -3.
  • B has repeated zero -1; the graph touches/turns at the x-axis rather than crossing in the basic case.
  • A function with zeros -4 and 5 can be y=(x+4)(x-5) times any nonzero scale factor.

Watch For

  • Treating factors themselves as zeros instead of setting each factor equal to zero.
  • Confusing x-intercepts with y-intercepts.

Strong Synthesis

At an x-intercept y=0, so the product of factors is zero. The zero-product property says at least one factor must equal zero.
