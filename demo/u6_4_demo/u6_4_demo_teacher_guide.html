Which method would you choose first for each: x²-9=0, x²+6x+2=0, 2x²+7x+3=0?

Choosing and Defending Solution Methods

Why This Fits

This quick class vote or prediction creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will compare factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula and select efficient solution methods.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A: square roots or factoring difference of squares are efficient.
  • B: completing square or formula; factoring is not obvious over integers.
  • C: factoring works cleanly: (2x+1)(x+3)=0.

Watch For

  • Choosing a familiar method without considering equation structure or efficiency.
  • Claiming a method is best without comparing accuracy, complexity, or available structure.

Strong Synthesis

It uses the equation’s visible structure, reduces unnecessary algebra and error risk, and produces exact/useful results efficiently.
Choose

Which method would you choose first for each: x²-9=0, x²+6x+2=0, 2x²+7x+3=0?

Students vote factoring, completing square/square roots, or formula for each equation.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: A: square roots or factoring difference of squares are efficient.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Reveal

Test the claim

Different structures favor different methods; more than one method can be valid, but efficiency and error risk differ.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. What clue makes x²-9=0 especially simple?

2. When is factoring uncertain or inefficient?

3. What makes the quadratic formula a reliable fallback?
