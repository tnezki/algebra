Recognizing Function Families

Why This Fits

This four view mystery creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will identify common function families from graphs, equations, tables, and contexts.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A linear, constant first difference +3.
  • B quadratic due to squared input and vertex structure.
  • C exponential decay with factor .75.

Watch For

  • Choosing a family from one surface feature while ignoring conflicting table or equation evidence.
  • Treating all curved graphs as quadratic.

Strong Synthesis

Combine structural evidence from a table, equation, or context with graph features; use constant differences/second differences/ratios or defining equation structure.
Mystery

One family is hiding behind four partial clues. Can you identify it before the graph appears?

Do not guess by appearance. Decide what evidence would have to agree.

Discussion

What carries forward?
