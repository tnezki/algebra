Two students move from (1,2) to (5,6). What is the rate of vertical change per horizontal step?

Rate of Change and Slope

Why This Fits

This human movement or position model creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will interpret slope as a measure of constant rate of change.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Case 1 slope 4/4=1.
  • Case 2 slope -4/4=-1.
  • Case 3 slope 0.

Watch For

  • Reversing vertical and horizontal change or using inconsistent point order.
  • Interpreting slope as a starting value rather than a rate.

Strong Synthesis

All slope triangles on the same nonvertical line are similar, so rise and run scale by the same factor and preserve their ratio.
Predict

Two students move from (1,2) to (5,6). What is the rate of vertical change per horizontal step?

Make a visible prediction, sketch, or physical arrangement before the reveal.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Case 1 slope 4/4=1.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Evidence

Use what you can observe

A volunteer walks 4 units right and 4 units up, then another volunteer uses 2 right and 2 up on the same line.

Record one rise/run pair and its ratio.

Teacher notes

If later slides depend on this evidence, verify students have recorded the needed information before advancing.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Case 2 slope -4/4=-1.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Reveal

Compare with the mathematical model

Both triangles give slope 1 because the ratio is preserved.

Teacher notes

Expected/acceptable evidence: Case 3 slope 0.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why do different slope triangles give the same slope?

2. What does a negative slope require about direction?

3. How do units change the meaning of slope in a context?

We will interpret slope as a constant rate, not just a fraction formula.
