What area does a rectangle with side lengths x+2 and x+3 have?

Polynomial Operations

Why This Fits

This physical object or manipulative reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will add, subtract, and multiply polynomial expressions.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A expands to x²+5x+6.
  • B expands to 2x²+7x-4.
  • Every row/column pairing contributes one partial product; like terms combine only after all products are represented.

Watch For

  • Combining unlike polynomial terms.
  • Distributing a negative sign or multiplying binomials incompletely.

Strong Synthesis

Each cell is forced by one row factor times one column factor, so the complete rectangle accounts for every term-to-term multiplication.

Evidence

Use what you can observe

Four subrectangles appear as the sides are partitioned into x, 2 and x, 3.

Reveal

Compare with the mathematical model

The parts are x², 3x, 2x, and 6, which combine to x²+5x+6.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why are there four partial products?

2. Where does the middle coefficient 5 come from?

3. How would a missing partial product show up in the model?

We will use structure to multiply polynomials reliably and interpret the resulting terms.
