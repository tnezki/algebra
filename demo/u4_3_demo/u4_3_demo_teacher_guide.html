Algebra 1 · Section 4.3 Demo Teacher Guide

A student claims multiplying f(x) by 2 moves every point up 2 units. What will the graph actually do?

Transformations

Why This Fits

This error or contradiction reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will analyze and graph transformations involving stretches, compressions, and reflections.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • +2 changes every y-value by +2: vertical translation.
  • 2f(x) doubles y-values: vertical stretch; points on the x-axis remain fixed.
  • f(x-2) shifts right 2.

Watch For

  • Confusing vertical stretch/compression with horizontal movement.
  • Reflecting or scaling points inconsistently across the graph.

Strong Synthesis

Vertical shift: (x,y)→(x,y+k); horizontal shift: (x,y)→(x+h,y) with notation interpreted carefully; vertical scale/reflection: (x,y)→(x,ay).
Choose

A student claims multiplying f(x) by 2 moves every point up 2 units. What will the graph actually do?

Compare y=f(x)+2 with y=2f(x) at points where f(x)=0, 1, and 3.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Reveal

Test the claim

Adding 2 creates a vertical translation; multiplying by 2 scales y-values away from the x-axis.

Teacher notes

Expected/acceptable evidence: 2f(x) doubles y-values: vertical stretch; points on the x-axis remain fixed.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. Which point stays fixed under vertical scaling?

2. How can a table of key points distinguish a shift from a stretch?

3. What does a negative multiplier add to the transformation?
