Algebra 1 · Section 8.5 Demo Teacher Guide

Two students use the same data but make opposite claims about which model is best. What evidence would settle the disagreement?

Function Synthesis and Capstone Analysis

Why This Fits

This competing claims or models creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will synthesize understanding of function families and representations to solve complex problems.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Claim 1 is false: B overtakes A between x=2 and x=3 in the table.
  • Claim 2 is supported for long-run positive x in these models; B is exponential with factor 2.
  • C is quadratic with minimum at x=1, value 9.

Watch For

  • Choosing a representation by habit rather than by the information needed.
  • Combining results from different representations without checking that they describe the same relationship.

Strong Synthesis

Select the representation that exposes the needed information most directly, then cross-check with another view when the conclusion depends on equivalence, scale, or model validity.
Choose

Two students use the same data but make opposite claims about which model is best. What evidence would settle the disagreement?

One claim cites graph appearance; the other cites a table pattern and equation structure.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Claim 1 is false: B overtakes A between x=2 and x=3 in the table.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Test the claim

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. Which representation answers model-family questions most efficiently here?

2. What evidence should cause a conclusion to be revised?

3. How do we check that multiple representations really describe the same relationship?
