Algebra 1 · Section 1.1 Demo Teacher Guide

Three views agree. Which graph is the fourth view?

Multiple Representations of Relationships

Why This Fits

This four view mystery creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will connect tables, graphs, equations, and contexts that represent the same relationship.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Rate 2 and starting value 3 must appear in every view.
  • The graph must contain (0,3), (1,5), (2,7), and (4,11).
  • A valid evidence statement names specific values/rate, not visual similarity.

Watch For

  • Matching representations by visual appearance while ignoring scale or values.
  • Changing a table or graph without preserving the same ordered pairs or relationship.

Strong Synthesis

Check invariant evidence such as matching ordered pairs, the same rate/pattern of change, and the same starting value or other defining feature.
Mystery

Three views agree. Which graph is the fourth view?

Do not guess by appearance. Decide what evidence would have to agree.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Rate 2 and starting value 3 must appear in every view.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Clues

Record the evidence

A table with (0,3), (1,5), (2,7), (4,11), the rule y=2x+3, and a taxi context with a \$3 start fee plus \$2 per mile.

Record the table and rule; you will need them after the graph choices disappear.

Reveal

Discussion

What carries forward?

1. What evidence rules out a graph fastest?

2. Why is matching by shape alone unsafe?

3. Which representation makes the starting value easiest to see?
