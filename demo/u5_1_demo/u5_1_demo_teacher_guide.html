What is the largest common “bundle” hidden in 6x²+9x?

GCF and Structure

Why This Fits

This physical object or manipulative reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will identify common factors and use structure to rewrite polynomial expressions.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A factors as 3x(2x+3).
  • B factors as 4x²(2x-3).
  • A factor can be common without being greatest; multiplying factors back must reproduce the original expression.

Watch For

  • Factoring out a quantity that is not common to every term.
  • Failing to verify the factored form by multiplication.

Strong Synthesis

Its numerical part is the greatest common divisor and its variable part uses the smallest exponent present in every term; no larger factor divides all terms.

Reveal

Compare with the mathematical model

Three x is common to both terms, giving 3x(2x+3).

Teacher notes

Expected/acceptable evidence: A factor can be common without being greatest; multiplying factors back must reproduce the original expression.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why is 3 also common but not greatest?

2. How does factoring undo distribution?

3. What should multiplying the factors reproduce?

We will use structure to extract the greatest common factor and check by distribution.
