Algebra 1 · Section 8.4 Demo Teacher Guide

Three models can all pass near the first two ball-height measurements. Which one should describe the whole flight?

Modeling Investigation

Why This Fits

This competing claims or models creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will create and analyze mathematical models using real-world data.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • The data rise then fall, strongly favoring a downward-opening quadratic.
  • Candidate Q captures a single maximum and decline; linear/exponential candidates cannot represent the full rise-and-fall pattern.
  • The model domain should be limited roughly to the observed flight interval; predictions are estimates because data are measured/noisy.

Watch For

  • Reporting a model without stating assumptions or a meaningful domain.
  • Treating a model prediction as exact even when data or constraints show limitations.

Strong Synthesis

It must match the structural behavior, respect context/domain, have interpretable parameters/features, and remain reasonably consistent across the data—not just fit selected points.
Choose

Three models can all pass near the first two ball-height measurements. Which one should describe the whole flight?

Candidate models are linear, exponential, and downward-opening quadratic; later time points show rise then fall.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: The data rise then fall, strongly favoring a downward-opening quadratic.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Reveal

Test the claim

Only the quadratic family can represent a single smooth rise to a maximum followed by a fall in this simplified setting.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. What evidence rules out the linear model?

2. What evidence rules out exponential growth/decay?

3. What makes a model useful even when it is not exact?

We will carry out a modeling cycle and defend our model with multiple kinds of evidence.
