Where can you stand if you must satisfy two rules at once?

Linear Systems of Inequalities

Why This Fits

This human movement or position model creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will analyze and interpret systems of linear inequalities.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • The feasible region is the overlap above/on y=x-1 and below/on y=-x+5.
  • The boundaries intersect at (3,2).
  • Points such as (0,1) satisfy both; points above the second line or below the first fail one constraint.

Watch For

  • Treating any point satisfying one inequality as feasible for the system.
  • Overlooking that the feasible region is the overlap of all constraints.

Strong Synthesis

It must satisfy every inequality in the system simultaneously, including any boundary conditions represented by solid or dashed lines.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why is satisfying just one inequality not enough?

2. What does a point on a solid boundary mean?

3. How could a feasible region represent real constraints?
