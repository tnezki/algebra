Algebra 1 · Section 1.5 Demo Teacher Guide

A game night costs \$5 to enter and \$3 per game. You spent \$26. Which equation finds the number of games?

Equations as Models

Why This Fits

This competing claims or models creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will write, solve, and interpret equations that model situations.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Story A: $6+4r=30$, so $r=6$ rentals.
  • Story B: $18+7w=60$, so $w=6$ weeks.
  • For $5+3x=26$, any context with fixed 5, repeated 3 per unit, total 26 is valid; x=7 must make contextual sense.

Watch For

  • Performing an operation on only one side of an equation.
  • Reporting a numerical solution without interpreting units or context.

Strong Synthesis

Return to the defined quantity, attach units/meaning, and check the value is feasible and actually answers the situation.
Choose

A game night costs \$5 to enter and \$3 per game. You spent \$26. Which equation finds the number of games?

Claim A: 5+3g=26. Claim B: 3+5g=26. Claim C: 5(g+3)=26.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Story A: $6+4r=30$, so $r=6$ rentals.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Reveal

Test the claim

Only 5+3g=26 matches the fixed fee plus per-game cost; g=7 games.

Teacher notes

Expected/acceptable evidence: Story B: $18+7w=60$, so $w=6$ weeks.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Discussion

What changed your mind?

1. What does each number mean in the valid equation?

2. Why can an equation be solved correctly but still model the wrong story?

3. What makes 7 a reasonable answer?

We will build equations from quantities, solve them, and return the solution to the original situation.
