Algebra 1 · Section 3.3 Demo Teacher Guide

Two snack bundles cost different totals. Can we determine each item price?

Writing and Solving Systems from Situations

Why This Fits

This physical object or manipulative reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will create and solve systems that model real-world situations.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Equations: 2d+s=13 and d+2s=17.
  • Solution d=3, s=7.
  • If only Bundle B total rises while A stays fixed, at least one price must change; solving new system gives d=2, s=9.

Watch For

  • Writing two equations that describe the same constraint instead of two distinct constraints.
  • Defining variables inconsistently with the equations or final interpretation.

Strong Synthesis

One equation describes infinitely many possible pairs. A second non-equivalent constraint can narrow the possibilities to a unique pair when the lines intersect once.
Predict

Two snack bundles cost different totals. Can we determine each item price?

Make a visible prediction, sketch, or physical arrangement before the reveal.

Teacher notes

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Evidence

Use what you can observe

Bundle A: 2 drinks + 1 sandwich = $13. Bundle B: 1 drink + 2 sandwiches = $17.

Record both bundle equations before the token arrangement changes.

Teacher notes

If later slides depend on this evidence, verify students have recorded the needed information before advancing.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why is one bundle alone insufficient?

2. What does the ordered pair represent?

3. Which algebra method mirrors combining or removing token groups?
