Comparing Quadratic Forms

Why This Fits

This four view mystery creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will compare standard, vertex, and factored forms of quadratic functions and determine which form best reveals key features.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • All three are equivalent.
  • Factored form: zeros 1 and 5.
  • Vertex form: vertex (3,-4), axis x=3.

Watch For

  • Assuming one quadratic form is always best for every question.
  • Reading zeros, vertex, or intercepts from the wrong structural feature.

Strong Synthesis

Use the form that exposes the needed feature with the least extra work, while recognizing all forms describe the same function.
Mystery

Three equations describe the same parabola. Which one would you choose to find its zeros?

Do not guess by appearance. Decide what evidence would have to agree.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.

Expected/acceptable evidence: All three are equivalent.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Clues

Record the evidence

Standard: y=x²-6x+5. Factored: y=(x-1)(x-5). Vertex: y=(x-3)²-4.

Record one feature you can read quickly from each form.

Teacher notes

If later slides depend on this evidence, verify students have recorded the needed information before advancing.

Expected/acceptable evidence: Factored form: zeros 1 and 5.

Commit

Choose and defend

Make one choice. Be ready to name two pieces of evidence that support it.

Reveal

Compare your evidence

Each form foregrounds a different feature while the graph confirms they describe the same relationship.

Teacher notes

Expected/acceptable evidence: Standard form: y-intercept 5 and leading coefficient 1.

Move on when students can state a reason connected to a value, feature, structure, or representation—not merely a guess.

Discussion

What carries forward?

1. Which form makes zeros obvious?

2. Which form makes the vertex obvious?

3. Why can “best form” depend on the question?

We will compare quadratic forms as different views of the same function.
