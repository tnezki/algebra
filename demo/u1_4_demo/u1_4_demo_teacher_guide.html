Is 3(x+4) the same as 3x+4?

Expressions and Structure

Why This Fits

This physical object or manipulative reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will interpret and rewrite algebraic expressions by identifying structure, meaning, and equivalent forms.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • $3(x+4)=3x+12$ because 3 multiplies both terms.
  • $2(2x+5)=4x+10$.
  • $4x+5$ omits distribution to the 5 and changes the area/value.

Watch For

  • Combining unlike terms as though they were like terms.
  • Distributing a factor to only one term or changing the value while rewriting.

Strong Synthesis

Both expressions describe the same total area assembled from the same subareas for every admissible x, so the equivalence is structural, not a one-value coincidence.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Where did the 12 come from?

2. What would a model for 3x+4 actually look like?

3. How can structure tell us two expressions are equivalent?
