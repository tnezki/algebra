If the ratios are close to, but not exactly, 1.2, can an exponential model still be reasonable?

Exponential Modeling with Data

Why This Fits

This graph or data reveal creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will create and analyze exponential models using real-world data.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Ratios are about 1.21, 1.18, 1.22, 1.18; not identical but clustered near 1.2.
  • A model like y=100(1.2)^t is reasonable for the trend.
  • Predictions should be compared with observed values and described as estimates.

Watch For

  • Assuming noisy data have a perfect constant ratio.
  • Making predictions without considering the meaningful domain or model limitations.

Strong Synthesis

Models summarize systematic structure while measurements/processes vary. Judge the overall pattern, residual size/direction, interpretability, and domain.

Discussion

What should we carry forward?

1. Why do real data ratios rarely match perfectly?

2. What does the growth factor mean?

3. How should a prediction outside the data range be described?

We will fit and critique exponential models rather than demand perfect data.
