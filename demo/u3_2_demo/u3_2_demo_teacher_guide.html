For y=3x-2 and 2x+y=13, which first move is most efficient?

Solving Systems by Substitution

Why This Fits

This competing claims or models creates a short point of tension that surfaces the section misconception without trying to teach the whole lesson.

Time5–8 minutes
GroupingWhole class / projector
Core ideaStudents will solve systems using substitution and choose efficient forms for solving.

Materials

Projector/screen; student notebook or mini whiteboard when the slide asks students to record or commit.

Before Class

Open the deck in presentation view. If a reveal figure is used, confirm the local image loads. Do not reveal later slides early.

Suggested Flow

Use the slide-specific notes below. Keep the pace brisk; the goal is noticing and a productive question, not complete instruction.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • System 1: substitute y=3x-2 to get 5x-2=13, x=3, y=7.
  • System 2: substitute x=4y+1 into the second equation.
  • A substitution replaces the variable with the entire equivalent expression everywhere that variable occurs; partial replacement is invalid.

Watch For

  • Substituting into only part of an expression or equation.
  • Failing to interpret the ordered pair in the original system.

Strong Synthesis

Use an already-isolated variable or a variable with coefficient ±1 when possible, minimizing extra algebra while preserving equivalence.
Choose

For y=3x-2 and 2x+y=13, which first move is most efficient?

Three proposals: solve the second equation for y; substitute 3x-2 for y immediately; solve the first equation for x.

Commit to a claim, vote, or prediction and prepare one reason.

Teacher notes

Purpose: create a quick commitment before formal instruction. Ask for reasons, not just answers.
