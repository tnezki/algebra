Algebra 1 - Unit 3: Systems and Constraints

Essential Standard

Students will solve, represent, interpret, and justify systems and constraints using graphs, algebraic methods, solution types, and contextual meaning.

Section Outline

Section 3.1: Graphing and Elimination

Learning Target: Students will solve systems graphically and by elimination while interpreting the meaning of different solution types.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct graphical representations of systems and connect intersection points to solutions.
  • I can use elimination to solve systems and interpret the solution type.
  • I can determine whether a system has one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions from algebraic and graphical evidence.

Key Representations

  • Graphs of two linear equations
  • Tables of paired values
  • Standard form equations
  • Intersection points
  • Solution-type statements

Modeling Opportunities

  • Represent two competing plans as a system of equations
  • Interpret an intersection as a break-even point
  • Connect elimination steps to equivalent constraints
  • Use solution types to interpret whether a situation is possible, impossible, or always true
Section 3.2: Solving Systems by Substitution

Learning Target: Students will solve systems using substitution and choose efficient forms for solving.

I Can Statements

  • I can formulate a substitution plan when one equation is already written in an efficient form.
  • I can apply substitution to solve systems and interpret the ordered-pair solution.
  • I can assess which algebraic method is more efficient for a given system.

Key Representations

  • Slope-intercept form equations
  • Equations solved for one variable
  • Substitution work
  • Ordered-pair solutions
  • Contextual solution statements

Modeling Opportunities

  • Represent one quantity in terms of another before solving
  • Use substitution to connect two related constraints
  • Interpret a solution in a two-variable situation
  • Select an efficient solving pathway based on equation structure
Section 3.3: Writing and Solving Systems from Situations

Learning Target: Students will create and solve systems that model real-world situations.

I Can Statements

  • I can build a system of equations that represents two constraints in a situation.
  • I can predict the meaning of a solution before solving from the context and variables.
  • I can design a solution pathway that connects equations, calculations, and a contextual conclusion.

Key Representations

  • Verbal situations
  • Variable definitions
  • Systems of equations
  • Graphs or tables for checking
  • Contextual conclusions

Modeling Opportunities

  • Create a system from ticket, mixture, cost, or comparison situations
  • Choose variables that make the constraints clear
  • Interpret both coordinates of the solution in context
  • Check whether a solution is reasonable for the situation
Section 3.4: Graphing Linear Inequalities

Learning Target: Students will graph and interpret linear inequalities.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct a boundary line and shaded region for a linear inequality.
  • I can use test points to interpret which region satisfies an inequality.
  • I can critique a graph of a linear inequality using the boundary, shading, and solution region.

Key Representations

  • Linear inequalities
  • Solid and dashed boundary lines
  • Shaded half-planes
  • Test points
  • Solution-region statements

Modeling Opportunities

  • Represent limits such as budgets, minimums, and maximums
  • Use a test point to check a feasible region
  • Interpret boundary inclusion in context
  • Connect inequality symbols to shaded solution regions
Section 3.5: Linear Systems of Inequalities

Learning Target: Students will analyze and interpret systems of linear inequalities.

I Can Statements

  • I can formulate a system of inequalities that represents multiple constraints.
  • I can extend graphing strategies to represent overlapping solution regions.
  • I can apply feasible-region reasoning to interpret possible solutions in context.

Key Representations

  • Systems of inequalities
  • Multiple boundary lines
  • Overlapping shaded regions
  • Feasible points
  • Constraint-based contexts

Modeling Opportunities

  • Represent real-world restrictions with more than one inequality
  • Interpret feasible and non-feasible points in context
  • Use overlapping regions to model choices under constraints
  • Connect graphical solutions to decision-making situations
Unit Modeling Themes
  • Systems model two or more conditions that must be true at the same time
  • Solution points and solution regions represent meaningful choices or outcomes
  • Different algebraic methods can solve the same relationship efficiently depending on structure
  • Inequalities model constraints, limits, and feasible options
  • Graphs, equations, and contexts work together to support interpretation and reasonableness checks

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 - Rungs 1-2

Format: 3-5 minute warm-up, vocabulary check, representation recognition, or exit ticket

Purpose: DOK 1 reveals whether students can read systems, equations, inequality symbols, boundary types, intersections, and solution regions before choosing a solving method.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Solution-Type Sort Can Be Assessed After Section 3.1 - use graph and equation clues to classify one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.
  2. Intersection Meaning Check Can Be Assessed After Section 3.1 - interpret an intersection point as a solution to both equations in a brief context.
  3. Boundary and Shading Read Can Be Assessed After Section 3.4 - represent whether a boundary should be solid or dashed and which side of the line is shaded.
  4. Constraint Vocabulary Check Can Be Assessed After Section 3.5 - classify points as feasible or not feasible using the language of constraints and solution regions.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 1 performance reveals whether students can read the language and features of systems and inequalities before being asked to solve or model with them.
DOK 2 - Rungs 3-4

Format: Mini-quiz, graph/table task, matching task, partner check, or short written task

Purpose: DOK 2 reveals whether students can carry out solving and graphing procedures while connecting their work to solution meaning.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Elimination Mini-Solve Can Be Assessed After Section 3.1 - construct an elimination pathway and state the solution type that results.
  2. Substitution Pathway Can Be Assessed After Section 3.2 - solve a system by substitution when one equation is already isolated.
  3. Situation to System Can Be Assessed After Section 3.3 - translate a short context into two equations and solve for the shared outcome.
  4. Inequality Graph Check Can Be Assessed After Section 3.4 - verify whether a test point and shaded region correctly represent a given inequality.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 2 performance reveals whether students can execute system-solving and inequality-graphing routines accurately while maintaining the meaning of the solution.
DOK 3 - Rungs 5-6

Format: Constructed response, model critique, strategy comparison, representation analysis, or discourse prompt

Purpose: DOK 3 reveals whether students can choose methods, critique representations, and reason about constraints rather than only completing procedures.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Method Efficiency Argument Can Be Assessed After Section 3.2 - assess whether graphing, substitution, or elimination is most efficient for a given system.
  2. Constraint Model Build Can Be Assessed After Section 3.3 - formulate a system from a context and connect each equation to a specific constraint.
  3. Feasible Region Reasoning Can Be Assessed After Section 3.5 - determine whether several points satisfy all constraints and interpret what each point means.
  4. Representation Consistency Check Can Be Assessed After Section 3.5 - analyze whether a graph, system, and context agree about the same solution region.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 3 performance reveals whether students can reason across solving methods, representations, and contextual constraints with enough precision to catch inconsistencies.
DOK 4 - Rungs 7-8

Format: Brief performance task, modeling task, critique-and-revise task, design task, or capstone synthesis task

Purpose: DOK 4 reveals whether students can synthesize systems, inequalities, and constraints into a coherent model in a short performance setting.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Design a Break-Even System Can Be Assessed After Section 3.3 - design a two-option context, write the system, solve it, and interpret the break-even point.
  2. Revise the Broken Model Can Be Assessed After Section 3.4 - create a corrected graph for a flawed linear inequality model and state what changed.
  3. Feasible Choice Snapshot Can Be Assessed After Section 3.5 - model a short decision situation with inequalities and interpret two possible feasible choices.
  4. Constraint Adaptation Task Can Be Assessed After Section 3.5 - adapt one constraint in a system and predict how the solution region changes.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 4 tasks reveal whether students can synthesize equations, inequalities, graphs, and contexts to model and interpret systems under constraints. These should remain short performance tasks rather than large projects.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 3 Summative Assessment Assessed during Unit 3 with later spiral review

Assessment is designed for a 45-minute paper-only time limit and is built around balanced evidence rather than a fixed question-count breakdown. Each version includes a mix of graphical system solving, elimination, substitution, context modeling, linear inequality graphing, feasible-region interpretation, and solution-type reasoning aligned to the unit learning targets and Depth of Knowledge expectations.

Assessments emphasize interpreting solution meaning, choosing and carrying out efficient solving methods, representing constraints with equations or inequalities, and connecting algebraic and graphical evidence to contextual conclusions.

Summative assessments are intentionally focused on DoK 1-3 evidence within a paper-only environment. Extended DoK 4 modeling, critique, design, and performance tasks are assessed separately through formative assessments, collaborative activities, investigations, discourse tasks, and performance assessments rather than embedded directly into the summative assessment.

Because of the spiraling course structure, Unit 3 content is revisited through subsequent units via retrieval practice, warm-ups, and formative assessments.

Holistic Rubric

A - Highly Proficient

The student demonstrates strong, flexible understanding of systems, solution types, algebraic methods, inequalities, and constraints across equations, graphs, and contexts. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student consistently connects equations, graphs, tables, and contexts while justifying conclusions using precise mathematical language.

B - Proficient

The student demonstrates solid understanding of systems, solution meaning, solving methods, and linear constraints. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Explanations are generally clear, though they may lack some precision or depth.

C - Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates partial but passable understanding of solving systems, graphing inequalities, and interpreting basic solution meaning. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with notation, reasoning, restrictions, representations, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is enough evidence that the student understands the main ideas.

Not Yet - Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate sufficient understanding of system solving, solution types, inequalities, or constraint-based reasoning. Work shows major errors, missing reasoning, weak conceptual understanding, or inability to connect representations. The student may need reteaching, additional practice, or reassessment to demonstrate proficiency.
