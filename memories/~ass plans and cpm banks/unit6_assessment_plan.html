Algebra 1 - Unit 6: Solving and Modeling with Quadratics

Essential Standard

Students will solve quadratic equations, analyze solution methods, model quadratic situations, and interpret solutions in equations, graphs, and contexts.

Section Outline

Section 6.1: Solving Quadratics by Factoring

Learning Target: Students will solve quadratic equations using factored form and interpret solutions.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct factored quadratic equations from given zeros, intercepts, or contextual constraints.
  • I can apply the zero product property to solve quadratic equations in factored form.
  • I can determine whether solutions make sense in relation to a graph or context.

Key Representations

  • Factored quadratic equations
  • Zeros and $x$-intercepts
  • Tables of values
  • Graphs of parabolas
  • Contextual solution statements

Modeling Opportunities

  • Model break-even points using factored quadratic equations
  • Connect ground-contact times to zeros in projectile contexts
  • Represent rectangular area relationships with products of linear factors
  • Interpret when negative or repeated solutions are meaningful
Section 6.2: Completing the Square

Learning Target: Students will solve quadratic equations by completing the square and connect the process to vertex form.

I Can Statements

  • I can build perfect-square trinomials by adding the needed constant term.
  • I can use completing the square to solve quadratic equations and write equivalent vertex-form expressions.
  • I can predict how completing the square reveals the vertex and symmetry of a quadratic function.

Key Representations

  • Standard form equations
  • Perfect-square trinomials
  • Vertex form expressions
  • Algebra tiles or area models
  • Parabola vertices and axes of symmetry

Modeling Opportunities

  • Model height or profit situations where the maximum or minimum is most useful
  • Connect area diagrams to completing-square steps
  • Rewrite quadratic models to reveal optimal input values
  • Interpret constants introduced during equivalent transformations
Section 6.3: Quadratic Formula

Learning Target: Students will solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula and interpret solutions.

I Can Statements

  • I can design a coefficient map that organizes $a$, $b$, and $c$ before using the quadratic formula.
  • I can apply the quadratic formula to solve equations with rational, irrational, or no real solutions.
  • I can assess how the discriminant predicts the number and type of real solutions.

Key Representations

  • Standard form equations
  • Quadratic formula
  • Discriminant values
  • Exact and approximate solutions
  • Graphs with two, one, or no $x$-intercepts

Modeling Opportunities

  • Model solution type using the discriminant before calculating roots
  • Connect irrational solutions to graphing-window estimates
  • Interpret no-real-solution cases in physical contexts
  • Use formula outputs to answer context-specific questions
Section 6.4: Choosing and Defending Solution Methods

Learning Target: Students will compare factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula and select efficient solution methods.

I Can Statements

  • I can formulate a solution-method plan based on the structure of a quadratic equation.
  • I can use factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula when the equation structure supports the method.
  • I can critique a selected solution method for efficiency, accuracy, and fit to the equation.

Key Representations

  • Standard form equations
  • Factored form equations
  • Vertex form equations
  • Method-selection flowcharts
  • Written solution-method comparisons

Modeling Opportunities

  • Model decision-making with method-selection criteria
  • Revise inefficient solution plans for the same equation
  • Connect equation structure to the most efficient solving strategy
  • Interpret whether exact or approximate solutions are more useful
Section 6.5: Quadratic Modeling and Optimization

Learning Target: Students will use quadratic functions to model real-world situations and interpret maximums, minimums, and intercepts.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct quadratic models from real-world constraints, data, or key features.
  • I can extend quadratic models to predict maximums, minimums, intercepts, and meaningful solution values.
  • I can assess whether a quadratic model and its solutions are reasonable for the situation.

Key Representations

  • Contextual quadratic models
  • Graphs with vertices and intercepts
  • Tables of input-output values
  • Vertex, standard, and factored forms
  • Domain restrictions and solution interpretations

Modeling Opportunities

  • Model projectile height over time
  • Optimize area or revenue using quadratic functions
  • Interpret maximums and minimums in context
  • Restrict domains to meaningful values in real-world situations
Unit Modeling Themes
  • Choosing a quadratic form based on the feature needed
  • Connecting symbolic solutions to graph intercepts and contextual meaning
  • Using structure to select efficient solving methods
  • Interpreting maximums, minimums, zeros, and restrictions in real-world models
  • Checking reasonableness of solutions against graphs, tables, and contexts

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 - Rungs 1-2

Format: 3-5 minute warm-up, vocabulary check, representation recognition, or exit ticket

Purpose: DOK 1 reveals whether students can access the language, notation, and basic graph features needed before selecting and using quadratic solution methods.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Quadratic Feature Quick Read Can Be Assessed After Section 6.1 - Build a feature map from a factored equation and graph showing zeros, intercepts, and solution values.
  2. Coefficient Sort Can Be Assessed After Section 6.2 - Apply vocabulary for $a$, $b$, $c$, vertex, and axis of symmetry to short equation cards.
  3. Discriminant Snapshot Can Be Assessed After Section 6.3 - Classify equations by the number of real solutions using the discriminant value.
  4. Method Vocabulary Check Can Be Assessed After Section 6.4 - Interpret method names, equation forms, and solution features in a short exit ticket.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 1 performance shows whether students can read quadratic notation, use key vocabulary, and access enough structure to begin solving and interpreting equations.
DOK 2 - Rungs 3-4

Format: Mini-quiz, graph/table task, matching task, partner check, or short written task

Purpose: DOK 2 reveals whether students can carry out solution procedures and connect each procedure to visible features of equations and graphs.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Factored Form Solver Can Be Assessed After Section 6.1 - Solve quadratic equations in factored form and connect each solution to an intercept.
  2. Complete and Convert Can Be Assessed After Section 6.2 - Construct a perfect square trinomial and rewrite a quadratic expression in vertex form.
  3. Formula to Features Can Be Assessed After Section 6.3 - Apply the quadratic formula and interpret exact or approximate solutions from the result.
  4. Model Feature Table Can Be Assessed After Section 6.5 - Use a quadratic model to connect a table, graph, vertex, and intercepts to context statements.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 2 performance shows whether students can solve with appropriate procedures and translate between algebraic results, graph features, and context meaning.
DOK 3 - Rungs 5-6

Format: Constructed response, model critique, strategy comparison, representation analysis, or discourse prompt

Purpose: DOK 3 reveals whether students can choose methods intentionally, reason about solution meaning, and check whether answers fit the structure and context.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Method Fit Check Can Be Assessed After Section 6.4 - Determine which solving method is most efficient for several equations and give structure-based reasons.
  2. Reasonable Solutions Prompt Can Be Assessed After Section 6.5 - Assess whether calculated quadratic solutions are reasonable after considering domain and context restrictions.
  3. Discriminant and Graph Argument Can Be Assessed After Section 6.3 - Analyze how the discriminant, graph, and solution count support the same conclusion.
  4. Strategy Revision Can Be Assessed After Section 6.4 - Build a revised solution plan after recognizing when an original method is inefficient or incomplete.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 3 performance shows whether students can reason beyond procedure, select methods strategically, and connect solution values to meaningful mathematical and contextual evidence.
DOK 4 - Rungs 7-8

Format: Brief performance task, modeling task, critique-and-revise task, design task, or capstone synthesis task

Purpose: DOK 4 reveals whether students can synthesize solving methods, forms, graph features, and contextual interpretations in a short modeling or design situation.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Optimization Mini-Model Can Be Assessed After Section 6.5 - Design a short quadratic model from constraints and interpret the maximum or minimum in context.
  2. Equation Form Design Can Be Assessed After Section 6.5 - Generate equivalent quadratic forms that reveal zeros, vertex, and standard-form coefficients for the same situation.
  3. Model Critique and Revise Can Be Assessed After Section 6.5 - Validate a proposed quadratic model by checking constraints, solution meaning, and graph features, then revise one part.
  4. Method Synthesis Challenge Can Be Assessed After Section 6.4 - Create a compact strategy guide that maps equation structure to an efficient solving method and expected solution type.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 4 tasks reveal whether students can synthesize quadratic solving, modeling, method selection, and feature interpretation across equations, graphs, and contexts. These should remain short performance tasks rather than large projects.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 6 Summative Assessment Mid-to-Late Course Spiral Assessment

Assessment is designed for a 45-minute paper-only time limit and is built around balanced evidence rather than a fixed question-count breakdown. Each version includes a mix of solving by factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula tasks, method-selection prompts, graph-feature interpretation, and short quadratic modeling items aligned to the unit learning targets and Depth of Knowledge expectations.

Assessments emphasize efficient method selection, accurate solving, interpretation of zeros and intercepts, connections among forms, discriminant reasoning, and contextual interpretation of maximums, minimums, and solution values.

Summative assessments are intentionally focused on DoK 1-3 evidence within a paper-only environment. Extended DoK 4 modeling, critique, design, and performance tasks are assessed separately through formative assessments, collaborative activities, investigations, discourse tasks, and performance assessments rather than embedded directly into the summative assessment.

Because of the spiraling course structure, Unit 6 content is revisited through subsequent units via retrieval practice, warm-ups, and formative assessments.

Holistic Rubric

A - Highly Proficient

The student demonstrates strong, flexible understanding of quadratic solving methods, equation structure, graph features, and real-world solution interpretation. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student consistently connects equations, graphs, tables, and contexts while justifying conclusions using precise mathematical language.

B - Proficient

The student demonstrates solid understanding of solving quadratic equations and interpreting solutions using appropriate methods and representations. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Explanations are generally clear, though they may lack some precision or depth.

C - Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates partial but passable understanding of quadratic solving, method selection, and feature interpretation. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with notation, reasoning, restrictions, representations, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is enough evidence that the student understands the main ideas.

Not Yet - Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate sufficient understanding of quadratic solving methods, model interpretation, or connections among equations, graphs, and contexts. Work shows major errors, missing reasoning, weak conceptual understanding, or inability to connect representations. The student may need reteaching, additional practice, or reassessment to demonstrate proficiency.
