Algebra 1 · Section 5.4 Investigation Teacher Guide

Zeros Become Crossings

Factored Form and Zeros

Why This Fits

The investigation turns the zero-product property into a visible bridge between algebraic factors and graph intercepts.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will connect factors, zeros, intercepts, and graphs of quadratic functions.

Materials

factored-form cards, graph panels, root record sheet

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. From (x-2)(x+3), predict the x-values that make the product zero.
  2. Match the expression to a graph using the predicted intercepts.
  3. Repeat with a repeated factor and describe the different graph behavior at that zero.
  4. Write a factored quadratic for a graph with assigned integer zeros.
  5. Explain why a zero of a factor becomes an x-intercept of the function.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A zeros are 2 and -3.
  • B has repeated zero -1; the graph touches/turns at the x-axis rather than crossing in the basic case.
  • A function with zeros -4 and 5 can be y=(x+4)(x-5) times any nonzero scale factor.

Watch For

  • Treating factors themselves as zeros instead of setting each factor equal to zero.
  • Confusing x-intercepts with y-intercepts.

Strong Synthesis

Target: At an x-intercept y=0, so the product of factors is zero. The zero-product property says at least one factor must equal zero.

Synthesis — student prompt

  • What information would a scale factor change or preserve?
