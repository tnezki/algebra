Function Family Gallery Walk

Recognizing Function Families

Why This Fits

A gallery allows many families and representations while requiring evidence strong enough to survive a disagreement.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 4
Core ideaStudents will identify common function families from graphs, equations, tables, and contexts.

Materials

gallery station sheets, evidence tags, calculator optional

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Rotate through stations showing graphs, tables, equations, or contexts.
  2. Classify each as linear, quadratic, exponential, absolute value, or neither.
  3. Attach an evidence tag naming a structural reason, not just appearance.
  4. At two stations, add a second representation that would strengthen the classification.
  5. Revisit one disputed station and resolve it using differences, ratios, or equation structure.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A linear, constant first difference +3.
  • B quadratic due to squared input and vertex structure.
  • C exponential decay with factor .75.
  • D absolute value with V-shape/vertex when graphed.

Watch For

  • Choosing a family from one surface feature while ignoring conflicting table or equation evidence.
  • Treating all curved graphs as quadratic.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Combine structural evidence from a table, equation, or context with graph features; use constant differences/second differences/ratios or defining equation structure.
