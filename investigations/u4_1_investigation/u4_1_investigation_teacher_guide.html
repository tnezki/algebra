Algebra 1 · Unit 4 · Section 4.1 · Investigation Teacher Guide

Function Family Fingerprints

Graph/table/equation classification · designed for 20–25 minutes

Time20–25 minutes
GroupsPairs / groups of 3
Core toolGraph/table/equation classification

Why This Investigation Fits Section 4.1

The investigation directly follows the current learning target: Students will identify common function families from graphs, equations, tables, and contexts.

Primary representations from the assessment plan: Graphs of parent and familiar function families; Tables showing constant differences, second differences, or ratios; Equations in recognizable family forms; Contexts involving change patterns.

Materials Per Group

  • One student investigation per student (Notability or print)
  • Pencil; calculator when appropriate
  • Optional mini-whiteboard for group claims

Before Class

  1. Students should have completed the Section 4.1 notes.
  2. Open the student file and confirm any local figures render.
  3. Decide whether students work in pairs or groups of three.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus
0–4Directions / first evidenceCheck that students understand the representation and quantities.
4–12Core investigationPress for calculations, labels, and evidence.
12–18Analysis / claim checkAsk groups to revise unsupported claims.
18–25SynthesisConnect the evidence back to the learning target.

Expected Evidence / Strong Responses

Students should use constant first differences for linear, constant second differences for quadratic, constant ratios for exponential, and symmetry/V-shape or distance structure for absolute value.

Watch for: Students may classify by isolated values or surface context words instead of repeated-change evidence.

Strong Response Indicators

Look forEvidence of understanding
Representation useStudent uses the graph/table/equation/context for a mathematical reason, not decoration.
ReasoningStudent shows enough work to make the claim checkable.
InterpretationStudent connects numerical or graphical results to the original quantities when a context is present.

Connection Forward

Close by asking students which feature or representation from today will be useful in the next section. Keep the discussion brief; do not front-load later section procedures.
