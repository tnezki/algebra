Teacher Instructions · Algebra 1 · Section 1.3

Function Family Card Sort

Linear · Quadratic · Exponential · Absolute Value

Purpose: Students classify functions by structure across four representations: table, graph, equation, and context.
Print: Print this file double-sided. Blank back pages are inserted so every card sheet prints on only one side. Pages with cards contain exactly 8 cards each.
Setup
  1. Give each group one complete set of 32 cut cards: 8 tables, 8 graphs, 8 equations, and 8 contexts.
  2. Label four areas of the table: Linear, Quadratic, Exponential, Absolute Value.
  3. Students sort every card into one of the four families. Each family should finish with exactly 8 cards: 2 tables, 2 graphs, 2 equations, and 2 contexts.
  4. Require students to justify at least one card in each family using evidence such as constant first differences, constant second differences, constant ratios, graph shape, equation structure, or distance-from-a-point structure.
Teacher move: Do not confirm categories too early. Ask, “What evidence proves that family?” and “Would another representation tell the same story?”

Context Cards

8 scrambled situations · 2 of each function family

Cut apart all 8 cards. Do not write the function family on the front.
Context C1

A bacteria culture begins with 200 bacteria. The number of bacteria doubles every hour.

Context C2

A taxi charges a $4 starting fee plus $2.50 for every mile traveled.

Context C3

A thermostat is set to 72°F. A display reports how many degrees the room temperature is away from 72°F.

Context C4

A square garden has side length x + 2 feet. Its area changes as x changes.

Context C5

A car is worth $24,000 today and loses 15% of its value each year.

Context C6

A hiker walks along a straight trail. A marker reports the hiker’s distance from a campsite at mile 5, no matter which side of the campsite the hiker is on.

Context C7

A water tank contains 900 gallons and drains at a constant rate of 30 gallons per minute.

Context C8

A ball is thrown upward. Its height increases, reaches a highest point, and then decreases as time continues.
