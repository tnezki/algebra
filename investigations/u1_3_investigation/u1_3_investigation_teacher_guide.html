Algebra 1 · Section 1.3 Investigation Teacher Guide

Family Evidence Lab

Function Families

Why This Fits

The section requires family recognition across views, so the investigation makes students triangulate multiple kinds of evidence.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 4
Core ideaStudents will recognize common function families from graphs, tables, equations, and contexts.

Materials

family evidence sheet, calculator optional, graph comparison panel

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Analyze four tables for first differences, second differences, or ratios.
  2. Pair each table with one equation and one graph panel.
  3. Label the strongest piece of evidence for linear, quadratic, exponential, and absolute value families.
  4. Challenge a classification that uses graph shape only.
  5. Create one new small table that belongs to an assigned family.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A is linear: first differences are +3.
  • B is quadratic: first differences 3,5,7 and second differences are constant.
  • C is exponential: ratios are ×2.
  • D is absolute value: symmetric outputs about x=0 and a V pattern when graphed.

Watch For

  • Classifying a family from graph appearance alone without checking differences, ratios, or equation structure.
  • Confusing quadratic growth with exponential growth.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Several families are curved. Strong evidence comes from stable difference/ratio structure, equation form, and family-specific features across more than one representation.
