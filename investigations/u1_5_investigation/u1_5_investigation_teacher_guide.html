Algebra 1 · Section 1.5 Investigation Teacher Guide

Equation Story Swap

Equations as Models

Why This Fits

Reverse-engineering contexts forces variables and operations to carry meaning instead of becoming detached procedures.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupingpairs, then trade with another pair
Core ideaStudents will write, solve, and interpret equations that model situations.

Materials

context cards, blank index cards, pencils

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Choose a situation card and identify the unknown quantity and units.
  2. Write an equation before solving; annotate what each term means.
  3. Solve and decide whether the result is reasonable in context.
  4. Write a new plausible story for the same equation and trade it.
  5. Solve the traded story and check whether the interpretation still fits the equation.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Story A: $6+4r=30$, so $r=6$ rentals.
  • Story B: $18+7w=60$, so $w=6$ weeks.
  • For $5+3x=26$, any context with fixed 5, repeated 3 per unit, total 26 is valid; x=7 must make contextual sense.

Watch For

  • Performing an operation on only one side of an equation.
  • Reporting a numerical solution without interpreting units or context.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Return to the defined quantity, attach units/meaning, and check the value is feasible and actually answers the situation.

What to listen for: Students should generalize from the investigation evidence, not repeat a procedure. Teacher press:
  • What does each term mean?
  • Would a negative or fractional answer be allowed in this context?
