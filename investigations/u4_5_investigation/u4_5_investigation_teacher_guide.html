Polynomial Structure and Quadratic Graphs

Why This Fits

This bridge section connects polynomial structure to quadratic graph features before full factoring/solution work begins.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will connect polynomial expressions to quadratic graphs and key features.

Materials

quadratic graph, equation/table cards, feature organizer

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Inspect a quadratic graph and mark zeros, vertex, and opening direction.
  2. Match the graph to a table and to two equivalent polynomial forms.
  3. Identify which form makes zeros easiest to see and which makes other structure visible.
  4. Use the leading term to predict end behavior.
  5. Create one changed coefficient and predict a graph feature that must change.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Positive leading coefficient means opens upward.
  • Zeros are 2 and 3 from factored form.
  • Axis of symmetry is halfway at x=2.5; vertex is below the x-axis.
  • As |x| grows, y grows positive.

Watch For

  • Assuming every expression form reveals every graph feature equally well.
  • Confusing the vertex with an intercept or the axis of symmetry with a solution.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Factored form highlights zeros, vertex form highlights vertex/transformations, and standard form highlights coefficients/y-intercept/leading-term structure. Choose based on the question.

  • Which feature is hardest to read from standard form?
