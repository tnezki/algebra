Complete the Square with Tiles

Completing the Square

Why This Fits

The geometric missing-corner idea explains why half-the-linear-coefficient is squared instead of making the procedure arbitrary.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupingpairs
Core ideaStudents will solve quadratic equations by completing the square and connect the process to vertex form.

Materials

algebra tiles or paper x²/x/unit pieces, square-grid recording page

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Build x²+6x using one x² tile and six x tiles.
  2. Arrange the x tiles equally along two sides of a square and identify the missing corner area.
  3. Add the same value algebraically to complete x²+6x+9=(x+3)².
  4. Apply the idea to x²-8x+5 by adding/subtracting the needed value within the expression.
  5. Use vertex form to identify the vertex and explain what completing the square revealed.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • x²+6x needs +9 to become (x+3)².
  • x²-8x+5 = (x-4)²-11.
  • Related function has vertex (4,-11).

Watch For

  • Adding a term to one side without preserving equality.
  • Using the coefficient of the linear term instead of half that coefficient when forming a square.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Half the linear coefficient becomes the side extension on each dimension of the square; squaring it fills the missing corner area.

Synthesis — student prompt

What to listen for: Students should generalize from the investigation evidence, not repeat a procedure. Teacher press:
  • Show the missing corner in the model.
  • How do you preserve equality while adding within an expression?
