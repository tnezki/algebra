How Fast Can You Write?

Students collect their own data, move it into tables and a graph, then use a linear regression model.

TimeAbout 40–50 minutes
GroupingIndividual data; partner/class discussion
MaterialsWorksheet, record sheets, pencil, Desmos, timer

Before Class

  • Print the student worksheet and record sheets from the student page.
  • Record sheets are built with 1/4-inch boxes. Print at 100% scale.
  • Have Desmos ready for the regression step.

Run the Timed Trials

Student rule: One letter per box. Write A to Z, then start again at A. Keep going until time is called.
HandTrialsAfter each trial
Dominant15 sec → 30 sec → 45 sec → 60 secCount letters and write the total.
Non-dominant15 sec → 30 sec → 45 sec → 60 secCount letters and write the total.

Give a short reset between trials. Students start each new trial at A.

Class Timer
0:15

Student Flow

  1. Collect: Complete all eight timed trials.
  2. Table: Move the totals into the two separate data tables.
  3. Graph: Plot both hands on the same graph.
  4. Desmos: Enter both tables. Use the Regression menu and choose Linear.
  5. Model: Record both equations and both rates.
  6. Discuss: Finish the prediction and discussion questions.

Desmos Step

  • Students enter one table for each hand.
  • Use Desmos's Regression menu and choose Linear. No regression syntax is needed.
  • Do not force the model through (0, 0).
  • Rate units are letters per second.

What to Expect

PatternBoth sets should usually show a positive, roughly linear relationship between time and letters written.
Hand comparisonThe dominant hand will often have the greater rate, but individual results can vary.
RegressionThe intercept may not be zero because the line is fitting imperfect real data.
Model limitA 5-minute prediction may be less reliable because fatigue or pace changes can matter.

Discussion Guide

  1. Which hand was faster? Look for data, graph, or rate evidence.
  2. What does the rate mean? Letters written per second.
  3. Why are the rates different? Hand control, practice, comfort, or natural variation.
  4. Perfect line? Usually no. Real measurements vary.
  5. Five-minute model? It may be less accurate because the writing pace may change.
  6. Graph vs. table? The graph makes the trend and hand comparison easier to see.

Watch For

  • Students switching the meanings of x and y.
  • Plotting time on the vertical axis.
  • Using the same symbol for both hands without a key.
  • Reporting a rate without units.
  • Assuming a regression model must pass through every point.
Finish: Ask two or three students to compare their dominant and non-dominant rates. Then ask whether a model from a short writing test should be trusted forever.
