Function Synthesis Capstone

Function Synthesis and Capstone Analysis

Why This Fits

The capstone requires choosing and coordinating representations rather than mechanically using every tool in the course.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupingteams of 4 with assigned analyst roles
Core ideaStudents will synthesize understanding of function families and representations to solve complex problems.

Materials

capstone evidence packet, calculators, chart paper or shared page

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Assign each teammate one representation to analyze first: graph, table, equation, or context.
  2. Share findings and identify which pieces describe the same relationship and which are competing models.
  3. Evaluate two claims using at least two representations each.
  4. Choose the most efficient representation to answer a new crossover/feature question and solve it.
  5. Receive one new piece of evidence, revise the team conclusion if needed, and document what changed.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Claim 1 is false: B overtakes A between x=2 and x=3 in the table.
  • Claim 2 is supported for long-run positive x in these models; B is exponential with factor 2.
  • C is quadratic with minimum at x=1, value 9.
  • Good synthesis uses graph/table/equation evidence and specifies the domain being discussed.

Watch For

  • Choosing a representation by habit rather than by the information needed.
  • Combining results from different representations without checking that they describe the same relationship.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Select the representation that exposes the needed information most directly, then cross-check with another view when the conclusion depends on equivalence, scale, or model validity.
