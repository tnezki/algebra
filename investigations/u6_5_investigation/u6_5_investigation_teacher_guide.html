Quadratic Optimization Studio

Quadratic Modeling and Optimization

Why This Fits

Optimization is meaningful only when graph features are interpreted as quantities and constrained by the situation.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will use quadratic functions to model real-world situations and interpret maximums, minimums, and intercepts.

Materials

modeling handout, calculator, graph panel

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Use a projectile-height data/model card to identify meaningful input and output quantities.
  2. Analyze a quadratic model to locate the maximum and intercepts using graph/table/equation evidence.
  3. Interpret the vertex and positive zero with units.
  4. Restrict the mathematical domain to times that make physical sense.
  5. Change one parameter and predict how the maximum or flight time changes before checking.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Axis/peak time t=-b/(2a)=1.5 s; h(1.5)=41 ft.
  • Positive landing time is about 3.10 s (negative root is not physically meaningful for time after launch).
  • Meaningful domain approximately 0≤t≤3.10.
  • Increasing the constant by 10 shifts the graph up 10, increasing maximum height and changing landing time.

Watch For

  • Interpreting the vertex without considering whether it is a maximum or minimum.
  • Keeping mathematically valid solutions that are not meaningful in context.

Strong Synthesis

Target: The situation restricts inputs and outputs. Times before launch or after landing may be mathematically valid points but physically irrelevant.

  • Distinguish a zero from the vertex.
