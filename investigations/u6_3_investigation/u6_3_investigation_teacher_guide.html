Discriminant and Formula Lab

Solution-type prediction + formula · designed for 20–25 minutes

Time20–25 minutes
GroupsPairs / groups of 3
Core toolSolution-type prediction + formula

Why This Investigation Fits Section 6.3

The investigation directly follows the current learning target: Students will solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula and interpret solutions.

Primary representations from the assessment plan: Standard form equations; Quadratic formula; Discriminant values; Exact and approximate solutions.

Materials Per Group

  • One student investigation per student (Notability or print)
  • Pencil; calculator when appropriate
  • Optional mini-whiteboard for group claims

Before Class

  1. Students should have completed the Section 6.3 notes.
  2. Open the student file and confirm any local figures render.
  3. Decide whether students work in pairs or groups of three.

Suggested Flow

MinutesStudent WorkTeacher Focus

Expected Evidence / Strong Responses

Positive discriminant gives two distinct real roots, zero gives one repeated real root, negative gives no real roots. Coefficients must be mapped from standard form including signs.

Watch for: Students may use the wrong sign for $b$ or $c$, omit parentheses in substitution, or interpret a negative discriminant as “no solutions” rather than “no real solutions.”
