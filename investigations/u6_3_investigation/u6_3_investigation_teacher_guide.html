Algebra 1 · Section 6.3 Investigation Teacher Guide

Quadratic Formula Error Lab

Quadratic Formula

Why This Fits

Critiquing realistic errors focuses attention on the structure of the formula and the meaning of the discriminant.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula and interpret solutions.

Materials

worked-solution cards, highlighters, formula reference, calculator optional

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Before calculating, use the discriminant to predict two, one, or no real solutions.
  2. Audit worked solutions for missing parentheses, sign errors, and denominator errors.
  3. Repair one solution line by line.
  4. Compare exact radical form with a decimal approximation and decide which is more informative for the task.
  5. Write a short checklist that would prevent the two most common errors you observed.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Case A: discriminant 25, roots 1/2 and -2.
  • Case B: discriminant 16-28=-12, so no real solutions.
  • Denominator must be the entire 2a, not divide by 2 then multiply by a.

Watch For

  • Substituting signs incorrectly for $a$, $b$, or $c$.
  • Ignoring the discriminant when interpreting the number or type of real solutions.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Positive discriminant → two distinct real roots; zero → one repeated real root; negative → no real roots (complex roots if studied).

  • Explain why parentheses are structural, not cosmetic.
