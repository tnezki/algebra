Algebra 1 · Section 6.4 Investigation Teacher Guide

Choose the Quadratic Method

Choosing and Defending Solution Methods

Why This Fits

A sort-and-defend structure turns method choice into strategic reasoning rather than a teacher-given command.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3 rotating through method stations
Core ideaStudents will compare factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula and select efficient solution methods.

Materials

quadratic station cards, method-choice matrix, calculators optional

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. At each station, choose factoring, square roots/completing the square, or quadratic formula before doing full work.
  2. Write the structural clue that drove the choice.
  3. Solve selected stations and compare effort with an alternative method.
  4. Reclassify one equation after a partner argues for a different efficient method.
  5. Write a decision guide that allows more than one valid choice when justified.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A: square roots or factoring difference of squares are efficient.
  • B: completing square or formula; factoring is not obvious over integers.
  • C: factoring works cleanly: (2x+1)(x+3)=0.
  • D: square root method is immediate.
  • Valid defenses can differ if the method is correct and reasonably efficient.

Watch For

  • Choosing a familiar method without considering equation structure or efficiency.
  • Claiming a method is best without comparing accuracy, complexity, or available structure.

Strong Synthesis

Target: It uses the equation’s visible structure, reduces unnecessary algebra and error risk, and produces exact/useful results efficiently.
