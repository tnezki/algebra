Feasible Region Floor Map

Linear Systems of Inequalities

Why This Fits

A system of inequalities is literally an overlap of conditions, and the floor model lets students experience intersection before formal shading.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupingwhole class + groups of 4
Core ideaStudents will analyze and interpret systems of linear inequalities.

Materials

floor coordinate grid, constraint signs, graph paper, colored pencils

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Represent the first inequality on the floor and have students move to points that satisfy it.
  2. Add the second constraint; students must remain only where both are true.
  3. Record several feasible and non-feasible points with reasons.
  4. Graph both inequalities on paper and shade the overlap.
  5. Choose one feasible point and interpret what both coordinates mean in a short context.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • The feasible region is the overlap above/on y=x-1 and below/on y=-x+5.
  • The boundaries intersect at (3,2).
  • Points such as (0,1) satisfy both; points above the second line or below the first fail one constraint.

Watch For

  • Treating any point satisfying one inequality as feasible for the system.
  • Overlooking that the feasible region is the overlap of all constraints.

Strong Synthesis

Target: It must satisfy every inequality in the system simultaneously, including any boundary conditions represented by solid or dashed lines.

What to listen for: Students should generalize from the investigation evidence, not repeat a procedure. Teacher press:

  • What does an empty feasible region mean in context?
