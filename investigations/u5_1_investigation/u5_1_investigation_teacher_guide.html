Algebra 1 · Section 5.1 Investigation Teacher Guide

Pull Out the Common Factor

GCF and Structure

Why This Fits

Factoring a GCF becomes reversing distribution through visible equal groups, not guessing a symbolic step.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupingpairs
Core ideaStudents will identify common factors and use structure to rewrite polynomial expressions.

Materials

algebra tiles or paper tile cutouts, recording page

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Build 6x²+9x with tiles.
  2. Arrange the tiles into equal groups that reveal the greatest common factor.
  3. Write the dimensions/product represented by the arrangement.
  4. Compare with a grouping that factors out only 3x and explain why it is not greatest.
  5. Create a new tile expression with a nontrivial GCF for another pair to factor.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • A factors as 3x(2x+3).
  • B factors as 4x²(2x-3).
  • A factor can be common without being greatest; multiplying factors back must reproduce the original expression.

Watch For

  • Factoring out a quantity that is not common to every term.
  • Failing to verify the factored form by multiplication.

Strong Synthesis

Target: Its numerical part is the greatest common divisor and its variable part uses the smallest exponent present in every term; no larger factor divides all terms.

Synthesis — student prompt

How can you tell that a proposed common factor is truly the greatest common factor?

Suggested / acceptable response: Its numerical part is the greatest common divisor and its variable part uses the smallest exponent present in every term; no larger factor divides all terms.
What to listen for: Students should generalize from the investigation evidence, not repeat a procedure. Teacher press:
  • What common factor remains if you factor out only 3?
  • How does distribution check your answer?
