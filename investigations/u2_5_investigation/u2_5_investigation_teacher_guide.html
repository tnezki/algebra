Index-Card Stack Modeling Lab

Linear Modeling with Data

Why This Fits

Authentic measurement creates small noise, making linear modeling and model limitations visible instead of artificially perfect.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will use linear models to represent and interpret real-world data.

Materials

20-40 identical index cards, ruler in millimeters, graph grid, calculator optional

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Measure the height of stacks of 5, 10, 15, 20, and 30 identical cards.
  2. Record measurements and make a scatter plot.
  3. Draw a reasonable line of fit rather than forcing every point onto the line.
  4. Estimate slope and intercept and interpret both in this measurement context.
  5. Predict the height of 50 cards and state one reason the prediction could differ from an actual measurement.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Expected pattern is approximately linear because each added card contributes about the same thickness.
  • Slope has units millimeters per card and estimates card thickness.
  • A small nonzero intercept can arise from measurement/fit error; it should be interpreted cautiously.
  • A 50-card prediction is reasonable only if the same card type and stacking method continue.

Watch For

  • Treating every data set as perfectly linear.
  • Using a prediction far outside the meaningful data range without considering model limits.

Strong Synthesis

Target: A model can capture the overall constant-rate trend while real measurements include small error. Fit quality and residual direction matter more than exact coincidence.

Synthesis — student prompt

What makes a line a reasonable model even if none of the measured points lies exactly on it?

Suggested / acceptable response: A model can capture the overall constant-rate trend while real measurements include small error. Fit quality and residual direction matter more than exact coincidence.
What to listen for: Students should generalize from the investigation evidence, not repeat a procedure. Teacher press:
  • Which measurement has the largest influence on your estimated rate?
  • Would extrapolating to 10,000 cards be equally defensible?
