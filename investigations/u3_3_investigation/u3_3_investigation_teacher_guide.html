Algebra 1 · Section 3.3 Investigation Teacher Guide

Bundle-Price System Builder

Writing and Solving Systems from Situations

Why This Fits

Concrete bundles make two simultaneous constraints visible and keep the ordered-pair solution tied to quantities.

TimeAbout 20–30 minutes
Groupinggroups of 3
Core ideaStudents will create and solve systems that model real-world situations.

Materials

two-color counters or paper tokens, bundle cards, recording sheet

Before Class

Prepare the listed materials and, when a generated representation is used, verify the local figure opens. Keep the task optional: students should still receive required core instruction elsewhere.

Suggested Flow

  1. Build two purchase bundles from colored tokens representing two item types.
  2. Translate each bundle total into an equation.
  3. Use the physical model to combine/remove equal groups and predict an elimination or substitution move.
  4. Solve the system and label both quantities with units.
  5. Change one bundle total and predict how the solution should shift before recalculating.

Answer / Evidence Guide

  • Equations: 2d+s=13 and d+2s=17.
  • Solution d=3, s=7.
  • If only Bundle B total rises while A stays fixed, at least one price must change; solving new system gives d=2, s=9.

Watch For

  • Writing two equations that describe the same constraint instead of two distinct constraints.
  • Defining variables inconsistently with the equations or final interpretation.

Strong Synthesis

Target: One equation describes infinitely many possible pairs. A second non-equivalent constraint can narrow the possibilities to a unique pair when the lines intersect once.

  • What would dependent bundle equations look like?
