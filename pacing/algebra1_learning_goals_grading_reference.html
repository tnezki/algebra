Warm-Up Pacing

Every non-test day · five numbered sets per cycle

The warm-up sequence is a separate spiral from the chapter lesson calendar. Use a numbered 1–5 cycle, rather than treating the set number as a weekday. Keep four questions: one equation, one diamond/factoring/graph fluency task, and two retrieval items drawn only from previously taught work.

Q1Solve

One equation; move from one-step to variables on both sides, distribution, fractions and checking. Keep old skills alive.

Q2Diamond → factoring → graphing

One visually clear diamond problem; progress into factors, zeros and their graph relationships.

Q3Spiral A

Previously taught representation or concept, with table / equation / graph variation.

Q4Spiral B

Independent retrieval or explanation from a different earlier section.

On days when the Monday Four Views task is the main lesson, the warm-up still follows this same four-question pattern. Assessment days can replace the routine. Do not introduce a brand-new topic as spiral review before it has been taught.

Before or after a summative

The governing framework

CPM curriculum → section Learning Goals → assessment evidence and progress reports → long-term Essential Standards and learning ladders. The section is the everyday reporting unit.

01Keep the textbook in charge

Use CPM concepts and its instructional progression. Do not invent a parallel sequence to satisfy a standards map.

02One Learning Goal per taught section

Each goal has one evolving PowerSchool grade, one five-square growth bar, and expandable supporting skills.

03Evidence and trend, not percentage bands

Plus/minus grades reflect how securely and independently students are progressing. Gather multiple pieces of evidence.

04Long-term mastery is distinct

Essential Standards and ladder rungs are derived later from underlying evidence, rather than assigning each goal's grade directly to a rung.

05Performance tasks support transfer

Authentic application helps document upper rungs. It is not a mandatory prerequisite for an A in a section goal.

06Preserve instructional routines

Keep Modeling Mondays and daily warm-ups. Skills may continue maturing across multiple CPM courses.

What the grades mean

Until the last planned meaningful exposure at the expected depth, use plus/minus grades to show progress. A− represents exceptional current performance; an A becomes available when final proficiency evidence exists. A+ is not currently used.

GradeInterpretation in the current learning cycle
AStrong, independent proficiency at expected depth, with final evidence available.
A−Nearly at A; exceptionally strong independent understanding of material taught so far. Highest pre-final designation.
B+Solid B-level understanding, progressing toward A.
BMeeting current expectations once final evidence allows a plain letter.
B−B-level understanding, trending toward C.
C+C-level understanding, progressing toward B.
CDeveloping, with meaningful gaps once final evidence allows a plain letter.
C−C-level understanding, trending toward D.
For pre-final grades, use A−, B+, B−, C+, C− (and any further locally approved trend symbols); do not treat plain A/B/C/D as interim placeholders. D-range definitions are not specified in this working source.
Grade ceiling follows meaningful exposure, not the calendar.

A full A is possible when a section's intended content and depth have been independently demonstrated in final evidence. Do not require the highest transfer rung or a separate major performance task. Earlier outstanding work can earn A−.
