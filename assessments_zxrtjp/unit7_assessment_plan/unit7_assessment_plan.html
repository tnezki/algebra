Algebra 1 — Unit 7: Exponential Functions and Modeling

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Students will identify exponential relationships from patterns, tables, graphs, and situations.

Unit Source Alignment

Framework Alignment
  • Framework Topics / Standards: 7.1: Recognizing Growth and Decay; 7.2: Writing Exponential Equations; 7.3: Graphing and Analyzing Exponential Functions; 7.4: Comparing Function Families; 7.5: Exponential Modeling with Data
  • Course-Specific Emphasis: Spiraled retrieval, delayed mastery, mathematical reasoning, communication, modeling, and connections among tables, graphs, equations, and contexts.
  • Pacing Guidance: The framework establishes the unit sequence but no fixed day-by-day calendar. Instruction and evidence opportunities remain flexible, with prior learning intentionally revisited over time.

Section Outline

Section 7.1: Recognizing Growth and Decay

Learning Target: Students will identify exponential relationships from patterns, tables, graphs, and situations.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct evidence from patterns, tables, graphs, and situations to support an exponential growth or decay relationship.
  • I can apply repeated multiplication and constant ratios to exponential patterns.
  • I can determine whether a relationship is exponential by checking how values change across representations.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • exponential function
  • growth
  • decay
  • common ratio
  • growth factor
  • decay factor

Key Representations

  • Tables with constant ratios
  • Graphs showing growth or decay curves
  • Verbal situations involving repeated percent change
  • Sequences of values or patterns
  • Equations in the form $y=ab^x$

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Classifies any increasing relationship as exponential.
  • Uses constant difference when exponential evidence requires a constant ratio.
  • Confuses a decay factor with the percent decrease.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Model population growth from a table of values
  • Model depreciation of an object over time
  • Connect repeated percent change to growth or decay factors
  • Sort evidence from tables, graphs, and contexts by type of change

Spiral Planning

Intro: Students will identify exponential relationships from patterns, tables, graphs, and situations.

Review: Keep active: students will use quadratic functions to model real-world situations and interpret maximums, minimums, and intercepts.

Mastery: Gather repeated evidence that students can identify common factors and use structure to rewrite polynomial expressions, with increasing independence and accurate interpretation.

Section 7.2: Writing Exponential Equations

Learning Target: Students will write exponential equations from tables, graphs, and situations.

I Can Statements

  • I can build exponential equations using an initial value and a growth or decay factor.
  • I can use exponential equations to predict values from tables, graphs, and situations.
  • I can formulate exponential equations that represent repeated percent increase or decrease.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • initial value
  • growth factor
  • decay factor
  • exponential equation
  • exponent

Key Representations

  • Initial values and growth factors
  • Tables of input-output values
  • Graphs with visible intercepts and multiplicative change
  • Context statements with percent growth or decay
  • Equations such as $f(x)=a(1+r)^x$ and $f(x)=a(1-r)^x$

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Uses the growth/decay rate directly as the multiplicative factor.
  • Places the initial value in the exponent.
  • Writes an equation from a table without checking that equal input changes produce a constant ratio.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Create savings or investment models with repeated percent growth
  • Create depreciation models for cars, technology, or equipment
  • Translate table values into exponential equations
  • Use an equation to forecast future values within a context

Spiral Planning

Intro: Students will write exponential equations from tables, graphs, and situations.

Review: Keep active: students will compare factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula and select efficient solution methods.

Mastery: Gather repeated evidence that students can connect factors, zeros, intercepts, and graphs of quadratic functions, with increasing independence and accurate interpretation.

Section 7.3: Graphing and Analyzing Exponential Functions

Learning Target: Students will graph exponential functions and analyze key features.

I Can Statements

  • I can design graphs of exponential functions using key points, growth or decay factors, and asymptote behavior.
  • I can apply key features of exponential graphs to interpret domain, range, intercepts, and end behavior.
  • I can assess whether a graph is reasonable for an exponential equation or situation.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • exponential graph
  • initial value
  • growth
  • decay
  • intercept
  • asymptotic behavior

Key Representations

  • Coordinate graphs of exponential functions
  • Tables generated from exponential equations
  • Key points such as the y-intercept
  • Horizontal asymptote behavior
  • Domain and range statements

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Treats exponential graphs as having a constant slope.
  • Misreads the initial value when the input begins at zero.
  • Assumes decay reaches zero in a finite number of repeated steps.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Graph growth and decay models from equations
  • Connect an equation's parameters to graph behavior
  • Interpret asymptote behavior in real-world settings
  • Use key points to sketch and check exponential graphs

Spiral Planning

Intro: Students will graph exponential functions and analyze key features.

Review: Keep active: students will use quadratic functions to model real-world situations and interpret maximums, minimums, and intercepts.

Mastery: Gather repeated evidence that students can compare standard, vertex, and factored forms of quadratic functions and determine which form best reveals key features, with increasing independence and accurate interpretation.

Section 7.4: Comparing Function Families

Learning Target: Students will compare linear, quadratic, and exponential functions using multiple representations.

I Can Statements

  • I can formulate comparisons among linear, quadratic, and exponential functions using rates, differences, ratios, and graph features.
  • I can use tables, graphs, equations, and situations to distinguish additive, quadratic, and multiplicative patterns.
  • I can critique claims about function families using evidence from multiple representations.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • linear
  • quadratic
  • exponential
  • difference
  • ratio
  • function family

Key Representations

  • Tables showing first differences, second differences, and ratios
  • Linear, quadratic, and exponential graphs
  • Equations for multiple function families
  • Context descriptions of change over time
  • Feature comparisons such as intercepts, growth patterns, and turning points

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Uses graph shape alone to classify a function family.
  • Confuses constant second differences with constant ratios.
  • Compares rates of change as if linear, quadratic, and exponential rates behave the same way.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Choose a function family for a data pattern using evidence
  • Contrast additive growth with multiplicative growth
  • Analyze when an exponential model eventually exceeds a linear or quadratic model
  • Revise a model choice after checking multiple representations

Spiral Planning

Intro: Students will compare linear, quadratic, and exponential functions using multiple representations.

Review: Keep active: students will compare factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula and select efficient solution methods.

Mastery: Gather repeated evidence that students can connect factors, zeros, intercepts, and graphs of quadratic functions, with increasing independence and accurate interpretation.

Section 7.5: Exponential Modeling with Data

Learning Target: Students will create and analyze exponential models using real-world data.

I Can Statements

  • I can construct exponential models from real-world data using an initial value and a growth or decay factor.
  • I can extend exponential models to make predictions and interpret results in context.
  • I can assess model fit and limitations when exponential data do not follow a perfect pattern.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • exponential model
  • data
  • growth factor
  • decay factor
  • prediction
  • initial value

Key Representations

  • Real-world data tables
  • Scatter plots with exponential trends
  • Exponential equations built from data
  • Predictions and residual reasoning
  • Contextual interpretations of parameters and outputs

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Chooses an exponential model without evidence of multiplicative change.
  • Uses a fitted model outside a reasonable input range without qualification.
  • Makes a prediction but does not connect the growth factor, units, and context.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Model viral sharing, population change, or compound growth
  • Model decay situations such as medicine concentration or depreciation
  • Use data to estimate a growth or decay factor
  • Check whether a model prediction is reasonable for the context

Spiral Planning

Intro: Students will create and analyze exponential models using real-world data.

Review: Keep active: students will use quadratic functions to model real-world situations and interpret maximums, minimums, and intercepts.

Mastery: Gather repeated evidence that students can compare standard, vertex, and factored forms of quadratic functions and determine which form best reveals key features, with increasing independence and accurate interpretation.

Unit Themes / Throughlines

  • Represent repeated multiplication with tables, graphs, equations, and contexts
  • Connect growth and decay factors to percent change
  • Interpret exponential parameters as contextual starting values and multipliers
  • Use multiple representations to distinguish exponential functions from linear and quadratic functions
  • Assess whether exponential models and predictions are reasonable for real data

Mastery Goals

U7-MG01: Factoring polynomial structure

Mastery Goal: Factor polynomial expressions using GCF, trinomial structure, and special patterns while verifying equivalence.

Supporting learning / prerequisites:

  • Students will identify common factors and use structure to rewrite polynomial expressions.
  • Students will factor quadratic trinomials using patterns, products, sums, and structure.
  • Students will recognize and factor special polynomial patterns.

Key representations:

  • Polynomial expressions in expanded form
  • Factored expressions with greatest common factors
  • Area models for common-factor structure
  • Tables of term coefficients and variable powers
  • Verbal contexts involving shared quantities
  • Standard-form quadratic trinomials
  • Factored binomial products
  • Product-sum tables

Recurring practices: Use polynomial structure; verify equivalence; connect algebraic factors to graph features; justify form choice.

What Meeting evidence should demonstrate: Meeting evidence chooses a valid factoring structure, factors completely, and verifies the result by multiplication or structural reasoning.

Common evidence weaknesses / misconceptions:

  • Factors out a common factor that is not greatest and stops too early.
  • Drops a term or sign when dividing each term by the GCF.
  • Treats factoring as changing an expression rather than rewriting it equivalently.
  • Chooses factors that multiply correctly but do not produce the correct middle term.
  • Forgets sign combinations when the constant term is negative.
U7-MG02: Factors, zeros, and quadratic graphs

Mastery Goal: Connect factored form to zeros, intercepts, and graph behavior of quadratic functions.

Supporting learning / prerequisites:

  • Students will connect factors, zeros, intercepts, and graphs of quadratic functions.

Key representations:

  • Factored quadratic functions
  • Zeros and x-intercepts
  • Quadratic graphs
  • Input-output tables near intercepts
  • Context statements involving break-even or height reaching zero

Recurring practices: Use polynomial structure; verify equivalence; connect algebraic factors to graph features; justify form choice.

What Meeting evidence should demonstrate: Meeting evidence obtains zeros from factors accurately and links those solutions to $x$-intercepts and relevant graph features.

Common evidence weaknesses / misconceptions:

  • Treats factors themselves as zeros instead of setting each factor equal to zero.
  • Confuses zeros with $y$-intercepts.
  • Does not connect repeated factors to repeated zeros or graph behavior.
U7-MG03: Choosing among quadratic forms

Mastery Goal: Compare standard, vertex, and factored forms and choose the form that most directly reveals needed features.

Supporting learning / prerequisites:

  • Students will compare standard, vertex, and factored forms of quadratic functions and determine which form best reveals key features.

Key representations:

  • Standard form
  • Vertex form
  • Factored form
  • Quadratic graphs with key features labeled
  • Contexts involving maximums, minimums, intercepts, and starting values

Recurring practices: Use polynomial structure; verify equivalence; connect algebraic factors to graph features; justify form choice.

What Meeting evidence should demonstrate: Meeting evidence identifies what each form reveals, rewrites when needed, and justifies the form selected for the task or interpretation.

Common evidence weaknesses / misconceptions:

  • Assumes one quadratic form is always best.
  • Reads a feature from a form that does not reveal it directly.
  • Rewrites between forms inaccurately and then compares mismatched functions.

Unit 7 Summative Assessment

This summative gathers secure evidence on the Mastery Goals and Learning Ladder defined in this Assessment Plan.

Important representations / practices / contexts: Use a purposeful mix of Polynomial expressions in expanded form, Factored expressions with greatest common factors, Area models for common-factor structure, Tables of term coefficients and variable powers, Verbal contexts involving shared quantities, Standard-form quadratic trinomials, Factored binomial products, Product-sum tables, Factored quadratic functions, Zeros and x-intercepts. Across the assessment, preserve the recurring emphasis on reasoning, precision, interpretation, and connections among representations.

Recommended balance: Use a conventional secure summative with selected-response or brief-response items for efficient breadth, constructed response for reasoning and representation evidence, and—when natural—a separate performance task or investigation that can contribute richer evidence. Do not force extended performance work into every paper assessment.

Materials / references: The framework does not specify an additional calculator or reference restriction for these unit plans; apply established coursewide norms consistently.

Assessment time: The existing plan reference used approximately 45 minutes for the conventional paper summative. Keep timing flexible for accommodations and for any separate performance evidence.

Reassessment / alternate forms: Reassessment should provide a new opportunity to demonstrate the same Mastery Goals and ladder without repeating the exact prompts. Changed numbers, contexts, methods, and representations should preserve the intended evidence.

Meeting and Exceeding: Meeting means sufficient evidence of mastery, not perfection. Exceeding may emerge through flexible transfer, stronger synthesis, or application beyond the expected challenge; it does not require every student to complete a special “Exceeding question.”

DOK note: Depth of Knowledge is descriptive, not a quota. Use the level of thinking naturally required by the evidence; do not force a fixed DOK distribution or DOK 4 into a conventional paper test.
