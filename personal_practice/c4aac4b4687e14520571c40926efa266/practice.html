Lesson 3.1.1

1. Direct Skills

Fill in the missing exponent.

\(y^{4}\cdot y^{2}=y^{\square}\)

Answer: \(y^{6}\)

The base is the same; add the exponents: 4+2=6.

\(y^{6}\)

2. Direct Skills

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{4p^{4}}{2p^{2}}\)

Answer: \(2 p^{2}\)

Divide the coefficients: 4/2=2.

Subtract exponents: 4−2=2.

\(2 p^{2}\)

Lesson 3.1.2

5. Direct Skills

Simplify using positive exponents.

\(\frac{2x^{-7}y^{2}}{2y^{-2}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{y^{4}}{x^{7}}\)

Combine coefficients, send x with its negative exponent to the denominator, and bring the denominator’s negative y power to the numerator.

\(\frac{y^{4}}{x^{7}}\)

6. Direct Skills

Rewrite with positive exponents. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(4a^{-2}\)

Answer: \(\frac{4}{a^{2}}\)

The coefficient 4 stays in the numerator; move only the base with exponent −2.

\(\frac{4}{a^{2}}\)

7. Review & Preview variation

Samantha has $2100 in the bank and spends $20 per week. In how many weeks will her balance be $1780? Define a variable and solve.

Answer: \(16\text{ weeks}\)

\(2100-20w=1780\)

Subtract: 20w=320.

Divide: w=16.
