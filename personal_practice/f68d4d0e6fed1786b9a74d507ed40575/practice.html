Lesson 3.1.1

1.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{16x^{6}y^{7}}{4x^{4}y^{3}}\)

Answer: \(4 x^{2} y^{4}\)

Divide coefficients: 16/4=4.

Subtract exponents for each base: x^(6−4) and y^(7−3).

\(4 x^{2} y^{4}\)

2.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify.

\((3x^{6}y^{2})^{3}\)

Answer: \(27 x^{18} y^{6}\)

Apply the outer power to each factor: 3^3, x^(6×3), y^(2×3).

\(27 x^{18} y^{6}\)

Lesson 3.1.2

5.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Rewrite with positive exponents. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(4p^{-3}\)

Answer: \(\frac{4}{p^{3}}\)

The coefficient 4 stays in the numerator; move only the base with exponent −3.

\(\frac{4}{p^{3}}\)

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Write an equivalent expression with positive exponents.

\(\frac{p^{-7}}{y^{-2}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{y^{2}}{p^{7}}\)

A negative exponent means reciprocal; simplify the factors in the numerator and denominator.

\(\frac{y^{2}}{p^{7}}\)
