Lesson 3.1.1

1.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Multiply and write one monomial.

\((2p^{7}y^{4})(-2p^{6}y^{6})\)

Answer: \(- 4 p^{13} y^{10}\)

Multiply coefficients: 2×-2=-4.

Add each variable’s exponents: 7+6=13 and 4+6=10.

\(- 4 p^{13} y^{10}\)

5.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Rewrite with positive exponents. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(5y^{-7}\)

Answer: \(\frac{5}{y^{7}}\)

The coefficient 5 stays in the numerator; move only the base with exponent −7.

\(\frac{5}{y^{7}}\)

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Evaluate. Assume any variable is nonzero.

\(6^0\)

Answer: \(1\)

Any nonzero base raised to the zero power equals 1.

7.Original CPM mixed/spaced homework problem (all original subparts).

Determine the equation of the line containing the points in the table.

x-2-123
y-4-5-8-9

Answer: \(y=-x-6\)

Differences in y divided by differences in x give slope -1.

The intercept is -6.

\(y=-x-6\)

8.Original CPM mixed/spaced homework problem (all original subparts).

Samantha has $2350 in the bank and spends $15 per week. In how many weeks will her balance be $2200? Define a variable and solve.

Answer: \(10\text{ weeks}\)

\(2350-15w=2200\)

Subtract: 15w=150.

Divide: w=10.
