3.Original CPM mixed/spaced homework problem (all original subparts).

Rewrite each expression using exponent properties.

a. \(\frac{h^{3}}{h^{8}}\)b. \(x^{5}\cdot x^{2}\)c. \((2k^{4})^{2}\)d. \(n^{4}\cdot n\)e. \(\frac{18x^{4}y^{5}}{3x^{4}}\)f. \(2xy^{2}\cdot 4x^{3}y^{5}\)

Answer: <p class="rp-parts"><span><strong>a.</strong> \(\frac{1}{h^{5}}\)</span><span><strong>b.</strong> \(x^{7}\)</span><span><strong>c.</strong> \(4k^{8}\)</span><span><strong>d.</strong> \(n^{5}\)</span><span><strong>e.</strong> \(6y^{5}\)</span><span><strong>f.</strong> \(8x^{4}y^{7}\)</span></p>

Lesson 3.1.2

5.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Simplify using positive exponents.

\(\frac{3x^{-3}y^{5}}{3y^{-2}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{y^{7}}{x^{3}}\)

Combine coefficients, send x with its negative exponent to the denominator, and bring the denominator’s negative y power to the numerator.

\(\frac{y^{7}}{x^{3}}\)

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{(3a^{4})^0+(2a)^0}{(2a^2)^0}\)

Answer: \(2\)

Each nonzero expression to power 0 equals 1.

Then (1+1)/1=2.
