Lesson 3.1.1

1.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{6y^{8}}{2y^{5}}\)

Answer: \(3 y^{3}\)

Divide the coefficients: 6/2=3.

Subtract exponents: 8−5=3.

\(3 y^{3}\)

2.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Multiply.

\((3y^{3})(5y^{2})\)

Answer: \(15 y^{5}\)

Multiply coefficients: (3)(5)=15.

Add exponents of y: 3+2=5.

\(15 y^{5}\)

Lesson 3.1.2

5.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Rewrite with positive exponents. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(7x^{-6}\)

Answer: \(\frac{7}{x^{6}}\)

The coefficient 7 stays in the numerator; move only the base with exponent −6.

\(\frac{7}{x^{6}}\)

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Simplify using positive exponents.

\(\frac{6x^{-3}y^{2}}{6y^{-1}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{y^{3}}{x^{3}}\)

Combine coefficients, send x with its negative exponent to the denominator, and bring the denominator’s negative y power to the numerator.

\(\frac{y^{3}}{x^{3}}\)
