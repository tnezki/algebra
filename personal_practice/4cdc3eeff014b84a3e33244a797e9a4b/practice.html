Lesson 3.1.1

1.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{8p^{13}}{4p^{8}}\)

Answer: \(2 p^{5}\)

Divide the coefficients: 8/4=2.

Subtract exponents: 13−8=5.

\(2 p^{5}\)

2.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Write as one power.

\((y^{5})^{3}\)

Answer: \(y^{15}\)

Multiply the exponents: 5×3=15.

\(y^{15}\)

3.Original CPM mixed/spaced homework problem (all original subparts).

Use slope and y-intercept to graph the line.

\(y = - 2 x - 5\)

Answer: Slope \(-2\); y-intercept -5

The slope is the rise divided by the run; the y-intercept is where x=0.

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Evaluate. Assume any variable is nonzero.

\((y^{5})^0\)

Answer: \(1\)

The entire nonzero expression is the base; anything nonzero to power 0 equals 1.
