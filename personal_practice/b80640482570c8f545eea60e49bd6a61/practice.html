Lesson 3.1.1

1. Direct Skills

Multiply the expressions below.

\(y^{6}\)
\(y\)

Answer: \(y^{7}\)

The base is the same; add the exponents: 6+1=7.

\(y^{7}\)

2. Direct Skills

Simplify. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(\frac{4x^{6}y^{4}}{2x^{4}y^{3}}\)

Answer: \(2 x^{2} y\)

Divide coefficients: 4/2=2.

Subtract exponents for each base: x^(6−4) and y^(4−3).

\(2 x^{2} y\)

4. Review & Preview variation

An account has $117. A student withdraws $8 per week. Write an expression for the balance after w weeks, and state when the model makes sense.

Answer: \(117-8w\)

Start with the initial amount and add or subtract the amount per time period.

\(117-8w\)

Lesson 3.1.2

5. Direct Skills

Write an equivalent expression with positive exponents.

\(\frac{p^{-2}}{y^{-2}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{y^{2}}{p^{2}}\)

A negative exponent means reciprocal; simplify the factors in the numerator and denominator.

\(\frac{y^{2}}{p^{2}}\)

6. Direct Skills

Evaluate. Assume any variable is nonzero.

\((8x^{7})^0\)

Answer: \(1\)

The entire nonzero expression is the base; anything nonzero to power 0 equals 1.
