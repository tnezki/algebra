Lesson 3.1.1

1.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify.

\((a^{2})(a)\)

Answer: \(a^{3}\)

The base is the same; add the exponents: 2+1=3.

\(a^{3}\)

2.I can simplify expressions using exponent properties.

Simplify.

\((a^{5})^{2}\)

Answer: \(a^{10}\)

Multiply the exponents: 5×2=10.

\(a^{10}\)

5.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Rewrite with positive exponents. Assume variables are nonzero.

\(2x^{-4}y^{5}\)

Answer: \(\frac{2y^{5}}{x^{4}}\)

Move only factors with negative powers across the fraction bar.

\(\frac{2y^{5}}{x^{4}}\)

6.I can explain zero and negative exponents and rewrite expressions.

Simplify using positive exponents.

\(\frac{8x^{-1}y}{2y^{-1}}\)

Answer: \(\frac{4 y^{2}}{x}\)

Combine coefficients, send x with its negative exponent to the denominator, and bring the denominator’s negative y power to the numerator.

\(\frac{4 y^{2}}{x}\)

7.Original CPM mixed/spaced homework problem (all original subparts).

Samantha has $1350 in the bank and spends $40 per week. In how many weeks will her balance be $950? Define a variable and solve.

Answer: \(10\text{ weeks}\)

\(1350-40w=950\)

Subtract: 40w=400.

Divide: w=10.
