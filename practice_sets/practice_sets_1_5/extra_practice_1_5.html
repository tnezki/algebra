Algebra 1 · 1.5 Equations as Models · Extra Practice

Intro

1. A gym charges a $24 joining fee plus $8 per visit. Write an equation for total cost C after v visits, then use it to find the cost after 5 visits.

2. A rectangle has perimeter 50 cm and length 16 cm. Write an equation using width w, solve it, and interpret w with units.

3. A student models “four less than three times a number is 20” as 4 - 3x = 20. Diagnose the translation and write the correct equation.

4. The equation 7x + 5 = 47 models seven identical items plus a $5 fee. Solve for x and explain what x means in the model.

5. A tank starts with 62 L and drains 5 L per minute. Write a rule for volume V after t minutes and use it to determine when the tank reaches 32 L.

6. A student solves 3(x + 2) = 27 and gets x = 11. Substitute that value into the original equation to show why it fails, then find the correct solution.

7. Create a short context that could be modeled by 12 + 4x = 40. Define x and interpret the solution in your context.

8. A model gives x = -6 for the number of tickets sold. Explain why algebraic correctness alone may not make a solution reasonable in context and what you would check.

10. Plan A starts at $40 and rises $2 per month. Plan B starts at $10 and rises $7 per month. Which plan is larger at month 0, and which has the faster rate?

11. A table changes by +15 in y when x changes by +5. Convert that to a per-1 rate, then compare it with the slope 4 of another relationship.

12. Two linear relationships have the same rate 6 but different y-intercepts, -2 and 9. Explain why one remains exactly 11 units above the other for every x.

13. Relationship A begins higher but decreases; Relationship B begins lower but increases. Explain what additional information or calculation is needed to know when B becomes larger.

14. Use the points (-1, 4) and (3, -8) to find A’s rate. B has rate -2. Which relationship decreases more rapidly?

15. A context says “starts with 15 and adds 4 each step.” An equation is y = 4x + 12. Identify which comparison feature already proves these are different relationships.

16. One relationship is shown by a graph and another by a table. Describe a common set of inputs you could use to make a fair numerical comparison.
