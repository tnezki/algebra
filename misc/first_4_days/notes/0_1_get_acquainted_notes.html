Consider the function

$$f(x)=-x^2+6x-5$$

  1. Does this function have a maximum or a minimum? What do you think that word is asking you to find?

  3. State the domain and range, or write what you think those words might describe.
  4. Where is the function increasing? Where is it decreasing?

Idea 1 - Get the File Ready to Use

Your goal is not to memorize a long sequence of taps. Your goal is to get today's file, open a working copy in Notability, and place it where you can find it again. A useful routine is one you can explain: where the original came from, where your working copy lives, and how you would reopen it tomorrow.

Stop and Discuss
  1. What is one sign that you opened the correct file?
  2. What system will help you find the same file again tomorrow?

Idea 2 - Show the Thinking, Then Check It

A workspace should leave enough of your thinking visible that you can understand it later. When solving an equation, make a legal move, keep both sides balanced, and then substitute your answer back into the original equation to check it.

Example 1

Solve $4x=28$. Show the inverse operation and check your solution in the original equation.

You Try It 1

Solve $x-9=14$. Show the inverse operation and check your solution in the original equation.

Idea 3 - Ordered Pairs Tell a Location

An ordered pair $(x,y)$ gives a location on the coordinate plane. Move horizontally using the $x$-coordinate first, then vertically using the $y$-coordinate. The signs of the coordinates determine the quadrant.

Stop and Discuss
  1. Why does the order in $(x,y)$ matter?
  2. How can the signs of $x$ and $y$ help you predict the quadrant before you plot?

Example 2

Plot $A=(-3,2)$. State the $x$-coordinate, the $y$-coordinate, and the quadrant.

You Try It 2

Plot $B=(4,-2)$. State the $x$-coordinate, the $y$-coordinate, and the quadrant.

Idea 4 - Build from One Move to Two

When an equation needs more than one move, undo operations in reverse order. Keep your work lined up so each step is easy to inspect, and check the final value in the original equation.

Example 3

Solve $2x+5=17$. Show each inverse operation and check your solution.

You Try It 3

Solve $3x-4=20$. Show each inverse operation and check your solution.

Use the Agenda to Know What Comes Next

Each class resource has a purpose. Use the agenda to locate the named material and recognize whether you are learning, practicing, investigating, or showing evidence. The file type changes, but the routine for finding, opening, and organizing it should become predictable.

Stop and Discuss
  1. What information on the agenda helps you decide which file to open?
  2. What should you do first if you are unsure where today's material is located?

Summary

  • Get the correct file, open a working copy, and organize it so you can find it again.
  • Leave enough work visible that another person - or future you - can follow the mathematics.
  • Check equation solutions in the original equation.
  • For an ordered pair $(x,y)$, move in the $x$ direction first and the $y$ direction second.
  • Use the agenda to identify the purpose of today's resource and what comes next.

Common Mistakes

  • Opening the correct file but not putting the working copy where you can find it again.
  • Writing only an answer when the reasoning or steps are needed.
  • Doing an inverse operation on only one side of an equation.
  • Skipping the check after solving an equation.
  • Reversing the $x$- and $y$-coordinates when plotting an ordered pair.
