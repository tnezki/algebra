Learning Target

Learn how to access, organize, and use Algebra 1 course materials while practicing the complete class routine.

I Can…

  • download a class file and open it in Notability.
  • organize and use course materials in Notability.
  • find the materials I need and identify where I am in the three-day learning cycle.
  • use familiar Algebra skills, including solving one-step equations and plotting points, to demonstrate the complete class routine.

What's to Come

DO NOT SOLVE IT YET. This is supposed to look harder than today's work. Circle words or notation that seem familiar, underline anything you think matters, put a ? beside unfamiliar language, and make reasonable attempts. You are not expected to know how to finish every part yet.

Consider the function

$$f(x)=-x^2+6x-5$$

  1. Does this function have a maximum or a minimum? What do you think that word is asking you to find?
  2. Find the x-intercepts and the y-intercept, or describe what you think those terms mean if you are not sure how to calculate them.
  3. State the domain and range, or write what you think those words might describe.
  4. Where is the function increasing? Where is it decreasing?
  5. Solve $f(x)=3$. What do you think the notation $f(x)$ means?

Getting a File Ready to Use

Your first job is not to memorize a sequence of taps. Your job is to be able to get today’s file, open it in Notability, and put it where you can find it again. When you are done, you should be able to explain where the original came from, where your working copy lives, and how you would reopen it tomorrow.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What is one sign that you opened the correct file?
  2. What system will help you find this file again later?

Using the Workspace

Notability is most useful when the page becomes a record of your thinking. Write enough steps that you can understand your work later. If you make a mistake, correct it in a way that lets you see what changed instead of erasing every trace of your reasoning.

Stop and Discuss

  1. Why might a teacher want to see the steps between the question and the answer?
  2. What makes digital handwriting readable enough to review later?

Example 2: Solve a One-Step Equation

Solve $4x=28$. Check your solution.

You Try It 2: Solve a One-Step Equation

Solve $x-9=14$. Check your solution.

Finding and Using Today’s Materials

Each day has a purpose. You should be able to locate the materials named on the agenda, open the correct file, and recognize whether you are learning, practicing, investigating, or showing evidence. The file type changes, but the routine for finding and organizing it should feel familiar.

Stop and Discuss

  1. What information on the agenda helps you decide which file to open?
  2. What will you do first if you are unsure where today’s material is located?

Example 3: Read Coordinates

Point $A$ is located at $(-3,2)$. State the $x$-coordinate, the $y$-coordinate, and the quadrant containing the point.

You Try It 3: Plot a Point

Plot $B=(4,-2)$ on a coordinate plane and label the point.

Summary

  • Get the correct file, open it in Notability, and organize it so you can find it again.
  • Use the workspace to leave a readable record of your mathematical thinking.
  • Use the agenda and the three-day cycle to recognize what kind of work you are doing.
  • Familiar Algebra skills let us practice the class routine without needing new content first.
Summary notes / questions / connections

Common Mistakes

  • Opening a file but not putting the working copy in the expected Notability location.
  • Writing only an answer when the work is needed to show reasoning.
  • Reversing the $x$- and $y$-coordinates of an ordered pair.
  • Undoing only one side of an equation instead of keeping the equation balanced.
