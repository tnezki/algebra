Problem 13

Choose the most efficient first method for each equation and give a one-phrase reason.

EquationFirst method
$(x-3)(x+6)=0$?
$x^2+10x=11$?
$3x^2+2x-6=0$?
Answer:

Factoring (already factored); completing the square (leading coefficient 1 and even linear term); quadratic formula (dependable when no simple factorization is apparent).

Problem 14

Compare solving $x^2-36=0$ by factoring and by the quadratic formula. Which is more efficient and why?

Answer:

Factoring is more efficient because $x^2-36=(x-6)(x+6)$ immediately gives $x=\pm6$ without formula substitution.

Problem 15

A measurement answer must be reported to the nearest tenth. Why is it useful to keep an exact radical form until the final rounding step?

Answer:

The exact radical preserves precision; rounding only at the end avoids unnecessary accumulated error.

Problem 16

A student says, “I always use the quadratic formula because it always works.” Critique this strategy for $(x-7)(x+2)=0$.

Answer:

The formula could work after expanding, but the zero product property solves the already-factored equation directly and with less work. Reliability alone does not make a method efficient.

Problem 17

Factor $x^2-121$.

Answer:

$(x-11)(x+11)$.

Problem 18

Factor $x^2+20x+100$.

Answer:

$(x+10)^2$.

Problem 19

Before factoring $9x^2-64$, identify the special pattern.

Answer:

Difference of squares: $(3x)^2-8^2$.

Problem 20

Explain why $x^2+12x+36$ fits a perfect-square-trinomial pattern.

Answer:

The first and last terms are squares and the middle term is $2(x)(6)=12x$, so it is $(x+6)^2$.

Problem 21

For equally spaced inputs, a table has constant first differences of $4$. Which familiar family is supported most strongly?

Answer:

Linear.

Problem 22

For equally spaced inputs, each table output is multiplied by $2$. Which familiar family is supported?

Answer:

Exponential.

Problem 23

Use the graph shown. Which familiar function family is the best match? Justify with one graph feature.

Answer:

Absolute value; the graph is V-shaped with a sharp vertex.

Problem 24

For equally spaced inputs, a table has constant second differences. Which familiar family is supported, and why is “the graph looks curved” weaker evidence?

Answer:

Quadratic. Constant second differences are diagnostic for equally spaced inputs, while many nonquadratic functions can also appear curved.
