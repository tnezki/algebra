Question 1

Which method fits $3x^2+10x+8=0$ best?

Solution:

Factoring fits well because $a c=3\cdot 8=24$ and $6+4=10$.

$3x^2+10x+8=(3x+4)(x+2)$, so zero product property would solve it efficiently.

Question 2

Explain one clue that an equation is ready for zero product property.

Solution:

A clue is that the equation is already set equal to zero and written as a product of factors.

Example: $(x-3)(x+5)=0$ is ready because each factor can be set equal to zero.

Question 3

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Choosing and Defending Solution Methods. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

The graph shows a quadratic. Estimate the zeros from the graph, then solve $y=x^2-10x+24$ algebraically and decide which representation is more precise.

Solution:

From the graph, the zeros are about $4$ and $6$.

Algebraically, $x^2-10x+24=(x-4)(x-6)$, so the exact zeros are $x=4$ and $x=6$.

The algebraic representation is more precise because it gives exact intercepts.

Question 10

A context describes a bouncing ball whose maximum heights are multiplied by about $0.6$ after each bounce. Compare what evidence a table, graph, and equation would each show if the relationship were exponential decay.

Solution:

A table would show each height multiplied by about $0.6$.

A graph would decrease quickly at first and then level toward zero.

An equation would have a form such as $h=a(0.6)^n$, showing exponential decay.

Question 11

A classmate says $y=(x-4)^2+6$ moves $y=x^2$ left $4$ and up $6$ because the equation contains $x-4$. Explain why that translation is not reasonable and give the correct movement.

Solution:

In $y=(x-4)^2+6$, the expression $x-4$ means the input must be $4$ to make the squared part zero.

So the graph moves right $4$ and up $6$, not left $4$.
