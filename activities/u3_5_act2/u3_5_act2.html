What is the first check you can do to decide if $(2,5)$ is feasible for a system?

First substitute $(2,5)$ into each inequality in the system. The point is feasible only if it makes every inequality true.

2

Use two points on each boundary to write the boundary equations, then use the shaded overlap to write the system of inequalities represented by the graph.

The boundaries are $y=2x+3$ with shading below and a dashed line, and $y=-x-1$ with shading above and a solid line. The system is $y\lt2x+3$ and $y\ge -x-1$.

3

Construct a strategy for graphing a system with constraints $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$, $x+y\le 12$, and $y\ge x-2$.

Your strategy must explain the order you would graph boundaries, how you would handle the nonnegative constraints, and how you would identify the final feasible region.

Graph $x=0$ and $y=0$ first to restrict the region to the first quadrant. Then graph $x+y=12$ as a solid line and shade below it. Graph $y=x-2$ as a solid line and shade above it. The feasible region is the overlap of all four shaded regions.

4

A student council has \$200 for prizes. Small prizes cost \$8 and large prizes cost \$15. They want at least 18 total prizes. Let $s$ be small prizes and $l$ be large prizes.

  1. Write a system of inequalities for the constraints.
  2. Recommend either $(10,8)$ or $(14,5)$ as the better feasible order, or explain if neither works.
  3. Justify your recommendation using both total prizes and total cost.

The constraints are $8s+15l\le200$, $s+l\ge18$, $s\ge0$, and $l\ge0$. For $(10,8)$: prizes $18$ and cost $80+120=200$, so it is feasible. For $(14,5)$: prizes $19$ and cost $112+75=187$, so it is feasible. $(14,5)$ is better if saving money matters because it meets the prize minimum at lower cost.

5

Evaluate $2x+5$ when $x=6$.

$2(6)+5=17$.

6

Use the distributive property to rewrite $3(x+2)$.

$3(x+2)=3x+6$.

7

Factor the common factor from $6x+9$.

$6x+9=3(2x+3)$.

8

A gym membership costs \$25 to join and \$15 each month. Write an equation and use it to find the cost after $6$ months.

Let $m$ be months. $C=25+15m$. For $6$ months, $C=25+15(6)=115$, so the cost is \$115.

Check $y=4x-1$. For $x=0$, $y=-1$; for $x=1$, $y=3$; for $x=2$, $y=7$. These match the table, so the representations match.

10

The graph shows a cost relationship. Use the graph to estimate the cost for $8$ tickets and explain how you used the axes.

The graph follows $y=5+4x$. At $x=8$, $y=5+4(8)=37$, so the cost is about \$37.

11

Analyze the structure of $4(x+3)+2(x+3)$. Rewrite it in two equivalent ways and explain which form makes the repeated group easiest to see.

Combine common groups: $4(x+3)+2(x+3)=(4+2)(x+3)=6(x+3)$. Expanded form is $6x+18$. The factored form $6(x+3)$ makes the repeated group easiest to see.

12

A table for an input-output rule is flawed: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(2,12),(3,12)$. Revise the relation in two different ways: one revision that makes it a function with a constant increase, and one revision that makes it a function but not a constant-increase rule. Explain both choices.

Constant-increase revision: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,12)$. Function but not constant-increase revision: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,15). The first has one output per input and constant differences; the second has one output per input but nonconstant differences.

13

State why $x=5$ cannot be written as $y=mx+b$.

$x=5$ is vertical and gives one $x$ value with many possible $y$ values. It cannot be written as $y=mx+b$ because it is not a function of $x$.

14

Write the equation of the line through $(4,7)$ and $(8,15)$. Show how you found the intercept.

Slope is $\frac{15-7}{8-4}=\frac{8}{4}=2$. Use $y=2x+b$ and point $(4,7)$: $7=8+b$, so $b=-1$. Equation: $y=2x-1$.

15

One linear model could be reasonable as an approximation for the early/middle data, but the scatter suggests the rate may increase at higher $x$ values.

16

Synthesize one relationship in four ways: equation, table, graphing points, and context. The slope must be $-2$ and the relationship must contain $(0,12)$.

Equation: $y=-2x+12$. Table: $(0,12),(1,10),(2,8),(3,6). Graphing points are the same coordinate pairs plotted and connected. Context: a tank starts with $12$ gallons and drains $2$ gallons per minute.

17

Describe the overlap for $y\le 4$ and $x>1$.

The overlap is all points at or below the horizontal line $y=4$ and to the right of the dashed vertical line $x=1$.

18

Why can a system of inequalities have infinitely many solution points?

A system of inequalities can have infinitely many solutions because shaded regions usually cover areas, not just one point.
