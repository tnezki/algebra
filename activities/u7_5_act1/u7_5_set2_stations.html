Station 2 Solutions

A medication-concentration model uses time in hours after a dose. Distinguish the mathematical domain from the inputs that make sense in this situation.

Solution:

The exponential function can accept all real inputs mathematically, but the context uses nonnegative times after the dose and only while the model remains medically realistic.

A real-world quantity is modeled by $A(t)=200(\frac{3}{2})^t$. Interpret both 200 and the factor $\frac{3}{2}$ in context-independent language.

Solution:

200 is the initial value at $t=0$. The factor $\frac{3}{2}$ means a 50% increase for each one-unit increase in $t$.

Station 5 Solutions

The height of an object is modeled by $H(t)=-(t-5)^2+35$. Interpret the vertex in the context.

Solution:

The vertex is $(5,35)$, so the object reaches a maximum height of 35 units at $t=5$.

A quadratic situation is modeled by $Q(t)=-2(t-1)(t-6)$. Find the zeros and explain what the positive zeros could represent in a context where $Q(t)=0$ marks an event.

Solution:

The zeros are $t=1$ and $t=6$. Positive zeros mark the times when the modeled quantity reaches 0.

Station 6 Solutions

A quadratic profit model has a positive value at input 0, rises to a maximum, then drops to 0 at a positive input. Name the graph features that represent the initial profit, greatest profit, and break-even input.

Solution:

The $y$-intercept represents the initial profit, the vertex represents the greatest profit, and the positive $x$-intercept represents the break-even input.

Factor $x^2-5x+6$ completely.

Solution:

$(x-2)(x-3)$.

Station 6 Solutions

Factor $\displaystyle 2x^2-x-6$ completely.

Solution:

$(2x+3)(x-2)$.

Find two integers whose product is $\displaystyle 20$ and whose sum is $\displaystyle 9$. Explain how this pair would help factor a quadratic trinomial.

Solution:

$\displaystyle 4$ and $\displaystyle 5$. The pair supplies the two numbers needed to split or construct the middle term in product-sum factoring.
