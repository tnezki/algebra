Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Write the next predicted value if the same multiplier continues.

  1. 50, 75, 112.5, ...
  2. 1200, 960, 768, ...
  3. 12, 15, 18.75, ...

Solution: a) Multiplier $1.5$, next value $168.75$. b) Multiplier $0.8$, next value $614.4$. c) Multiplier $1.25$, next value $23.4375$.

Question 2

In the model $P(t)=350(1.08)^t$, identify what 350 and 1.08 could mean in a real-world data situation.

Solution: $350$ is the starting amount when $t=0$. The multiplier $1.08$ means the amount is multiplied by $1.08$ each time period, which is an $8\%$ increase per period.

Question 3

The value of a vintage item changed from $\$180$ to $\$243$ over three years. Assuming exponential growth, estimate the annual multiplier and write a model.

Solution: Let the annual multiplier be $r$. Then $180r^3=243$, so $r^3=1.35$ and $r=\sqrt[3]{1.35}\approx1.105$. A model is $V(t)=180(1.105)^t$.

Question 4

The imperfect data set below is being modeled exponentially. Construct a modeling strategy for this data set. Your strategy must explain how to estimate a multiplier, how to make a week 6 prediction, and how to decide whether the prediction is reasonable.

Week01234
Amount100132174229306

Solution: Estimate ratios: $132/100=1.32$, $174/132\approx1.318$, $229/174\approx1.316$, and $306/229\approx1.336$. Use an average multiplier about $1.32$. Predict week $6$ by continuing from week $4$: $306(1.32)^2\approx533$. The prediction is reasonable for near-term use because the ratios are close, but it should be checked against future data.

Question 5

Identify the axis of symmetry halfway between the zeros $-4$ and $6$.

Solution: The axis of symmetry is the midpoint of the zeros: $\frac{-4+6}{2}=1$. So the axis is $x=1$.

Question 6

For $y=(x-2)^2$, how many distinct zeros does the function have? Explain the repeated factor in one sentence.

Solution: The function has one distinct zero, $x=2$. The repeated factor $(x-2)^2$ means the graph touches the x-axis at $x=2$ instead of crossing it.

Question 7

Use the graph to write two equivalent forms of the same quadratic: factored form and vertex form.

Solution: The graph matches $y=x^2-4x-5$. Factored form is $y=(x-5)(x+1)$, showing zeros $5$ and $-1$. Vertex form is $y=(x-2)^2-9$, showing vertex $(2,-9)$.

Question 9

Rewrite $y=2(x-1)(x-5)$ in standard form and use the factored form to identify the zeros.

Solution: Expand: $y=2(x-1)(x-5)=2(x^2-6x+5)=2x^2-12x+10$. The factored form shows zeros at $x=1$ and $x=5$.

Question 10

A classmate claims $x^2-12x+40$ is almost a perfect square because $40$ is close to $36$. Is that reasoning sufficient? Give a reasonableness argument based on structure, not closeness.

Solution: No. Structure matters: $(x-6)^2=x^2-12x+36$, so a perfect square with middle term $-12x$ must have constant $36$, not $40$. Being close does not make it a perfect square.

Question 11

Design a strategy for deciding whether any trinomial is a perfect square before factoring. Test your strategy on $9x^2+30x+25$ and $9x^2+30x+16$.

Solution: Check that the first and last terms are squares and that the middle term is $2ab$. For $9x^2+30x+25$, $a=3x$, $b=5$, and $2ab=30x$, so it is $(3x+5)^2$. For $9x^2+30x+16$, $a=3x$, $b=4$, and $2ab=24x$, not $30x$, so it is not a perfect square.

Question 12

Create a three-term polynomial with greatest common factor $6x$ such that one term is negative and the polynomial has degree $3$. Then factor it completely and explain how your example satisfies all constraints.

Solution: One valid example is $12x^3-18x^2+30x$. The GCF is $6x$, one term is negative, and the degree is $3$. Factoring gives $6x(2x^2-3x+5)$.

Question 15

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Quadratic Formula. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Use the discriminant to predict the number of real solutions for $2x^2+4x+7=0$.

Solution: For $2x^2+4x+7=0$, the discriminant is $b^2-4ac=4^2-4(2)(7)=16-56=-40$. Since it is negative, there are no real solutions.

Question 16

Strategy Construction. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Quadratic Modeling and Optimization. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

Design a step-by-step solving strategy for $2x^2+9x-5=0$ that avoids unnecessary work. Then solve it.

Solution: Use the quadratic formula: $x=\frac{-9\pm\sqrt{9^2-4(2)(-5)}}{2(2)}=\frac{-9\pm\sqrt{121}}{4}=\frac{-9\pm11}{4}$. So $x=\frac12$ or $x=-5$. This avoids forcing a factoring method when the formula is direct.
