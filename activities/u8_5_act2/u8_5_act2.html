Structure Matching puzzle (self-checking)
Setup Groups of 4. Copies of puzzle
Directions
  1. Students match expressions, answers, or steps.
  2. Pieces form a complete shape (triangle/square).
  3. If correct, puzzle fits perfectly.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Students justify each match before placing pieces.
  • The group uses the puzzle shape to self-check and revises mismatches.
  • All students can explain at least one question-answer pair.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Pieces are matched by guessing shapes only.
  • One student controls all pieces while others watch.
  • The group forces pieces to fit without checking the math.

Unit 8.5 · Activity 2 Exit Ticket Answer Key
Exit Ticket
1. Current · DOK 1–2

Compare $f(x)=x^2$ and $g(x)=2^x$. Which is larger at $x=4$?

2. Current · DOK 1–2

Name one reason a simpler model may be better than a more complex model.

3. Last · DOK 1–2

For $y=6(0.8)^x$, growth or decay?

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

Solve $(2x-6)(x+1)=0$.

Key:
  1. At $x=4$, $f(4)=16$ and $g(4)=16$, so they are equal.
  2. A simpler model may be better if it explains the pattern about as well with fewer assumptions, or if the data do not support the extra complexity.
  3. If the multiplier is greater than 1, it is growth; if it is between 0 and 1, it is decay. Here the named multiplier determines the answer.
  4. $2x-6=0$ gives $x=3$, and $x+1=0$ gives $x=-1$.
