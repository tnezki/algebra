Problem 13

Does $x^2+12x+36$ fit a perfect-square pattern? If so, factor it.

Yes; $(x+6)^2$.

Problem 14

Does $x^2+13x+36$ fit a perfect-square pattern? Explain.

No. With last term 36, the required middle term would be $12x$, not $13x$.

Problem 15

Compare $x^2-49$ and $x^2+49$. Which is a difference of squares?

$x^2-49$; it factors as $(x-7)(x+7)$.

Problem 16

A student claims $x^2+25$ factors as $(x-5)(x+5)$. Diagnose the claim.

That product gives a difference, not a sum: $x^2-25$. The original is not a difference of squares.
