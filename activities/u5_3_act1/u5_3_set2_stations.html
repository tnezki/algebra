Set 2 · Unit 5 · Section 5.3

Problem 13

Does $x^2+14x+49$ fit a perfect-square pattern? If so, factor it.

Answer:

Yes; $(x+7)^2$.

Problem 14

Does $x^2+15x+49$ fit a perfect-square pattern? Explain.

Answer:

No. With last term 49, the required middle term would be $14x$, not $15x$.

Problem 15

Compare $x^2-64$ and $x^2+64$. Which is a difference of squares?

Answer:

$x^2-64$; it factors as $(x-8)(x+8)$.

Problem 16

A student claims $x^2+36$ factors as $(x-6)(x+6)$. Diagnose the claim.

Answer:

That product gives a difference, not a sum: $x^2-36$. The original is not a difference of squares.

Problem 21

Find the missing constant so $x^2+22x+\square$ is a perfect-square trinomial.

Answer:

121

Problem 22

Find the missing middle term so $x^2+\square+49=(x+7)^2$.

Answer:

$14x$

Problem 23

Expand $(x-6)(x+6)$ and name the special pattern produced.

Answer:

$x^2-36$, a difference of squares.

Problem 24

Expand $(x+5)^2$ and identify the relationship between the middle coefficient and $n$.

Answer:

$x^2+10x+25$; the middle coefficient is $2n$.
