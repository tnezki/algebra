Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Use the square area model to write three equivalent expressions for the total area: a sum, a product, and a squared-binomial form.

Solution:

The model has side lengths $x+4$ and $x+4$.

Sum: $x^2+4x+4x+16=x^2+8x+16$. Product: $(x+4)(x+4)$. Squared-binomial form: $(x+4)^2$.

Question 2

Complete the table and use it to factor the trinomial.

ExpressionFirst squareLast squareMiddle check
$4x^2-20x+25$_______________

Use the signs to write the correct squared binomial.

Solution:

First square: $(2x)^2=4x^2$. Last square: $5^2=25$.

Middle check: $2(2x)(5)=20x$, and the sign is negative, so the middle term is $-20x$.

$4x^2-20x+25=(2x-5)^2$.

Question 3

A classmate claims $x^2-12x+40$ is almost a perfect square because $40$ is close to $36$. Is that reasoning sufficient? Give a reasonableness argument based on structure, not closeness.

Solution:

No. For a perfect square with middle term $-12x$, the form would be $(x-6)^2=x^2-12x+36$.

The constant must match $36$, not just be close to it. Since $40 e36$, the expression is not a perfect-square trinomial.

Question 4

Create three expressions: one difference of squares, one perfect-square trinomial, and one expression that looks similar but is neither. Explain the structural evidence for each classification.

Solution:

Difference of squares: $x^2-25=(x+5)(x-5)$ because both terms are squares and subtract.

Perfect-square trinomial: $x^2+10x+25=(x+5)^2$ because the middle term is $2(x)(5)$.

Neither: $x^2+10x+20$ because $20$ is not $5^2$ and it is not a difference of squares.

Question 5

For $a=b+9$, what expression can replace $a$ in a second equation?

Solution:

Replace $a$ with $b+9$ in the second equation.

Question 6

Which system is more substitution-ready: $y=2x+1$ with $x+y=10$, or $3x+2y=12$ with $5x-y=1$?

Solution:

$y=2x+1$ with $x+y=10$ is more substitution-ready because $y$ is already isolated.

Question 7

After finding $x=5$ in a substitution problem, what should you do next to complete the ordered pair?

Solution:

Substitute $x=5$ into one original equation to find the matching $y$-value, then write the ordered pair $(5,y)$.

Question 8

A school store sold 52 snacks. Granola bars cost \$1.50 and fruit cups cost \$2.25. Total sales were \$91.50. Write and solve a system.

Solution:

Let $g$ be granola bars and $f$ be fruit cups. Then $g+f=52$ and $1.50g+2.25f=91.50$.

Multiply by $100$: $150g+225f=9150$. With $g=52-f$, $150(52-f)+225f=9150$, so $7800+75f=9150$, $f=18$, and $g=34$.

Question 9

At a fundraiser, small candles cost \$4 and large candles cost \$7. The group sold 63 candles for \$330. Write and solve a system for small and large candles.

Solution:

Let $s$ be small candles and $l$ be large candles. Then $s+l=63$ and $4s+7l=330$.

Use $s=63-l$: $4(63-l)+7l=330$, so $252+3l=330$, $l=26$, and $s=37$.

Question 10

The table gives two gym membership plans. Write equations and solve for the month when the costs match.

PlanStart feeMonthly fee
A\$30\$18
B\$90\$10
Solution:

Plan A: $C=30+18m$. Plan B: $C=90+10m$.

Set equal: $30+18m=90+10m$, so $8m=60$ and $m=7.5$ months.

Question 11

Construct a strategy for graphing a system with constraints $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$, $x+y\le 12$, and $y\ge x-2$.

Your strategy must explain the order you would graph boundaries, how you would handle the nonnegative constraints, and how you would identify the final feasible region.

Solution:

Graph $x\ge0$ and $y\ge0$ first to restrict to Quadrant I. Then graph $x+y=12$ with shading below and $y=x-2$ with shading above.

The feasible region is the overlap of all four shaded regions, including solid boundary lines because the inequalities include equality.

Question 12

Create a system of two linear equations that has solution $(4,3)$ and is efficient to solve by substitution because one equation is already solved for $y$.

  1. Write the system.
  2. Show a substitution solution path.
  3. Explain why your structure makes substitution efficient.
Solution:

Example system: $y=x-1$ and $2x+y=11$.

Substitute $x-1$ for $y$: $2x+(x-1)=11$, so $3x-1=11$, $3x=12$, and $x=4$.

Then $y=4-1=3$. The solution is $(4,3)$, and substitution is efficient because one equation is already solved for $y$.

Station 4 Answers

Question 13

A graph of $y=x^2$ is shifted down $3$ units. Write the new equation.

Solution:

The new equation is $y=x^2-3$.

Question 14

Compare the parent graph and translated graph. Write the translation in words and identify the translated vertex.

Solution:

The translated absolute value graph has vertex $(3,2)$, so the translation is right $3$ and up $2$.

Question 15

A student predicts that $y=4(x-1)^2-2$ is wider than $y=x^2$ because it is multiplied by $4$. Make a reasonableness argument to support or reject the prediction.

Solution:

Reject the prediction. Multiplying by $4$ makes the graph vertically stretched, so it is narrower than $y=x^2$, not wider.

The graph also shifts right $1$ and down $2$.

Question 16

A student sees a curved graph and chooses quadratic, but the table gives $(0,3),(1,6),(2,12),(3,24)$. Revise the family choice and explain why the revised family is stronger.

Solution:

The table outputs $3,6,12,24$ multiply by $2$ each step, so exponential is stronger than quadratic.

A curved graph alone is not enough; the constant ratio provides clearer evidence for an exponential family.
