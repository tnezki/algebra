Whiteboard Indy

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Each student needs a whiteboard and marker. Project one problem at a time.

Student Directions

Work independently first. Show enough work that another person can follow your reasoning. Hold your board up when asked, then revise after discussion.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: Every student has a visible attempt before answers are compared.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Look for students waiting instead of starting; ask, “What representation or value can you use first?”
  • Select two different correct approaches to compare after the reveal.

Whiteboard Partners

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Partners share one whiteboard and one marker. Project one problem at a time.

Student Directions

Take turns writing. The non-writer explains or checks each step. Switch the writer every problem.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: Both partners can explain the solution, not only the person holding the marker.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Ask the non-writer to explain the current step.
  • If partners disagree, require a mathematical check before they erase either idea.

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Arrange two facing rows or another quick partner-rotation setup.

Student Directions

Solve the assigned problem, compare reasoning with your partner, then rotate when directed. Carry one useful idea forward to the next partner.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: Each round includes mathematical talk and a written solution or revision.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Listen for repeated misconceptions across partners.
  • Pause a rotation to spotlight a strong checking strategy when it appears.

Showdown

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Teams of 3–4 each need individual paper or boards. One problem is shown at a time.

Student Directions

Everyone solves privately. On the signal, reveal work at the same time. Compare methods and agree on a team explanation.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: Every person commits to a solution before the team discussion.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Check that boards stay hidden until the signal.
  • After reveal, ask teams to explain one difference between methods, not just announce an answer.

Think, Trade, Agree

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Students need individual work space and a nearby partner.

Student Directions

Think and solve alone. Trade explanations with a partner. Agree on a checked solution or record why you still disagree.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: The final response reflects evidence from both students’ reasoning.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Require students to point to the exact step or value that supports agreement.
  • When partners disagree, ask what check could settle the issue.

Round Table

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Groups of 3–4 share one recording sheet or board.

Student Directions

One student begins the problem, then passes the work so each person adds or checks one meaningful step. Continue until the group has a complete solution.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: Every student contributes a mathematical step, check, or explanation.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Watch for one student completing the whole problem before passing.
  • Ask the next student to explain the previous contribution before adding to it.

Mathematical Hot Seat

Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
← Activity Options
Setup

Pairs or small groups face the projected problem; one student is the explainer for each round.

Student Directions

The explainer talks through a solution without simply reading another person’s work. Teammates may ask clarifying questions but cannot take over. Rotate the hot seat.

Participation

Be ready to explain your own reasoning. Ask questions, compare evidence, and revise when a check shows something needs to change.

Looks Like Success: The student in the hot seat uses precise mathematical language and can defend the reasoning.
Question Set

Open the 12-question Activity set

Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Prompt with “What evidence supports that?” instead of supplying the next step.
  • Rotate quickly if one student begins dominating the explanation.
Unit 1 • Section 1.2 • Printable Directions

Stations

4 stations × 3 questions
Open Handout
Setup

Place one station page at each location. Groups begin at different stations and rotate on the teacher signal.

Student Directions

Work all three problems at your station. Everyone records work. Compare methods and resolve disagreements before rotating.

Participation

Each group member must contribute a step, check, or explanation at every station.

Looks Like Success: Groups leave readable reasoning and can explain how they checked at least one problem before rotating.
Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Track which station creates the most disagreement and revisit one representative problem.
  • Ask a student who did not write the current step to explain it.

Find Someone Who

12 partner-check problems
Open Handout
Setup

Give each student the handout. Students circulate and find a partner for each problem.

Student Directions

Do your own work first. Ask a partner to explain or check the reasoning. Record what was checked, then get the partner’s name or signature.

Participation

Use a different partner when possible. A signature only counts after a mathematical explanation or check.

Looks Like Success: Work areas show the student’s reasoning, and partner notes identify what was explained, checked, or revised.
Teacher Monitoring Moves
  • Listen for “looks right” signatures and require an actual mathematical check.
  • Ask students to name one revision they made because of a partner explanation.
