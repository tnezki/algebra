Solving Quadratics by Factoring
Source: practice_set_6_1_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: Whiteboard for each student
Structure Indy → Group comparison → Consensus
Setup Groups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard. Rotate the Captain each round.
Directions
  1. Each student solves the problem individually
  2. When prompted, students Showdown (reveal answers at the same time)
  3. Discuss differences and justify reasoning
  4. Group agrees on a correct final answer
  5. Repeat
Goal: Accountability + justification
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Every student has an answer ready before the reveal.
  • Groups discuss mismatches and cite algebra or graph evidence.
  • The captain records a consensus answer after justification.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students reveal before everyone has tried.
  • A group votes on an answer without explaining why.
  • One student becomes the permanent captain or solver.

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Solve $3x^2+12x=0$ and connect each solution to an intercept.

Solution:

Factor the left side: $3x^2+12x=3x(x+4)$.

Set each factor equal to zero: $3x=0$ or $x+4=0$.

The solutions are $x=0$ and $x=-4$; on the graph these are the $x$-intercepts.

Unit 6.1 · Activity 2 · ShowdownSolution 15
Solution 15
Last · DOK 3

Factor $12x^2+30x$ in two different first steps: one using a common factor of $6x$ and one using a common factor of $3x$. Compare the results and explain why one final answer is considered completely factored.

Solution:

Using $6x$: $12x^2+30x=6x(2x+5)$.

Using $3x$: $12x^2+30x=3x(4x+10)=3x\cdot 2(2x+5)=6x(2x+5)$.

The completely factored form is $6x(2x+5)$ because no common factor remains.
