Station 1 Answers

Question 1

For each phrase, identify the likely function family.

  1. Adds the same amount each step.
  2. Multiplies by the same factor each step.
  3. Has constant second differences.
  4. Has a turning point and symmetric output pattern.

Solution: Adds the same amount: linear. Multiplies by the same factor: exponential. Constant second differences: quadratic. Turning point and symmetric output pattern: quadratic.

Question 2

For each context, choose linear, quadratic, or exponential and justify with the change pattern.

  1. A subscription adds 12 songs every week.
  2. A rumor reaches 3 times as many people each hour.
  3. The height of a thrown object follows a path with a maximum.

Solution: a) Linear because it adds $12$ songs each week. b) Exponential because it triples each hour. c) Quadratic because projectile height has a maximum and a parabolic path.

Question 3

A student claims that $y=2x^2$ will always outgrow $y=2(2)^x$ because quadratics have an exponent of 2. Critique the claim using at least three input values and a conclusion about long-run behavior.

Solution: Test values. At $x=2$, $2x^2=8$ and $2(2)^x=8$. At $x=4$, $2x^2=32$ and $2(2)^x=32$. At $x=6$, $2x^2=72$ while $2(2)^x=128$. The exponential eventually grows faster, so the claim is false.

Question 4

A school club wants to predict membership for the next 6 weeks. The first three counts are 40, 52, and 67.6.

Recommend either a linear or exponential model. Support your recommendation with calculations and explain one limitation.

Solution: Ratios are $52/40=1.3$ and $67.6/52=1.3$, so an exponential model is reasonable: $M(w)=40(1.3)^w$. A limitation is that club growth may slow when the school population or interest becomes saturated.

Question 5

Choose the expression equivalent to $9x^2-36$.

  1. $9(x+2)(x-2)$
  2. $(3x+6)(3x-6)$
  3. $9(x^2-4)$
  4. All of these are equivalent.

Explain your choice briefly.

Solution: All of these are equivalent. $9x^2-36=9(x^2-4)=9(x+2)(x-2)=(3x+6)(3x-6)$.

Question 6

Use signs to determine whether the square is $(x+7)^2$ or $(x-7)^2$: $x^2-14x+49$.

Solution: $(x-7)^2=x^2-14x+49$, so the expression is $(x-7)^2$.

Question 7

For each factored function, list the zeros.

  1. $y=(x-3)(x+5)$
  2. $y=2(x+1)(x-4)$
  3. $y=-(x-6)(x+2)$

Solution: $y=(x-3)(x+5)$ has zeros $3$ and $-5$. $y=2(x+1)(x-4)$ has zeros $-1$ and $4$. $y=-(x-6)(x+2)$ has zeros $6$ and $-2$.

Question 8

Complete the table for $f(x)=(x-1)(x+3)$ and use it to identify the zeros.

$x$$-3$$-2$$-1$$0$$1$
$f(x)$_________________________

Solution: Evaluate $f(x)=(x-1)(x+3)$. At $x=-3$, $0$; $x=-2$, $-3$; $x=-1$, $-4$; $x=0$, $-3$; $x=1$, $0$. The zeros are $x=-3$ and $x=1$.

Station 3 Answers

Question 9

Write a factored equation for the graph shown, then expand it to standard form.

Solution: The graph is $y=(x-2)(x+4)$ because it has x-intercepts $2$ and $-4$. Expanding gives $y=x^2+2x-8$.

Question 10

Use the blank coordinate plane to sketch $y=x^2+6x+8$ from its x-intercepts and vertex.

Solution: Factor $y=x^2+6x+8=(x+2)(x+4)$, so the x-intercepts are $-2$ and $-4$. The vertex is halfway at $x=-3$, with $y=(-3)^2+6(-3)+8=-1$, so sketch an upward parabola through $(-4,0)$, $(-2,0)$ and vertex $(-3,-1)$.

Question 11

Analyze the structure of $kx^2+7x-6$. Find two possible integer values of $k$ that make the trinomial factorable over the integers, and explain how the product-sum structure changes.

Solution: To be factorable over integers, the product $k(-6)=-6k$ needs integer factors that add to $7$. Examples: if $k=-1$, the trinomial is $-x^2+7x-6=-(x-1)(x-6)$. If $k=2$, then $2x^2+7x-6=(2x-? )$ actually uses pair $8$ and $-1$: $2x^2+8x-x-6=(x+4)(2x-3)$. Changing $k$ changes the product target and therefore the split of the middle term.

Question 12

Recommend a form for each task using the same quadratic $y=x^2-6x+5$: finding zeros, finding the minimum, and finding the y-intercept. Justify each recommendation with an equivalent form when helpful.

Solution: For zeros, use factored form $y=(x-1)(x-5)$. For the minimum, use vertex form $y=(x-3)^2-4$, giving minimum $-4$ at $x=3$. For the y-intercept, standard form $y=x^2-6x+5$ shows $y=5$ when $x=0$.

Question 13

Solve $x^2-4x-1=0$ exactly.

Solution: Use the quadratic formula: $x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{(-4)^2-4(1)(-1)}}{2}=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{20}}{2}=2\pm\sqrt5$.

Question 14

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Completing the Square. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Rewrite $y=x^2+2x+6$ in vertex form, then interpret the minimum.

Solution: Complete the square: $y=x^2+2x+6=(x^2+2x+1)+5=(x+1)^2+5$. The minimum value is $5$ at $x=-1$.

Question 15

Critique Error. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Choosing and Defending Solution Methods. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

Identify the error, correct the solution, and explain how the structure shows the mistake.

Solution: The source prompt does not show the specific erroneous work sample, so a valid critique must identify a concrete incorrect step, correct it, and connect the correction to structure. For example, if someone treats a non-perfect-square trinomial as a square, check the middle term with $2ab$ and rewrite the correct factorization or solving method.

Question 16

Create With Constraints. Create or revise a quadratic task for Completing the Square using the constraints below.

  • Include at least two representations among equation, table, graph, or context.
  • Make the solution method visible from structure, not from a hint.
  • Interpret the solution in words.

Use the provided graph as one representation. Create a quadratic with vertex $(5,60)$ that opens downward, write it in vertex and standard form, and explain how completing the square connects the two forms.

Solution: Answers vary. A downward-opening quadratic with vertex $(5,60)$ is $y=-(x-5)^2+60$. Expanding gives $y=-(x^2-10x+25)+60=-x^2+10x+35$. Completing the square on $-x^2+10x+35$ factors out $-1$ from the quadratic terms and returns $-(x-5)^2+60$.
