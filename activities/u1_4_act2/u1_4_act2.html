Speed Dating Math
Expressions and Structure
Source: practice_set_1_4_3(1).html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Rotating pairs
Materials: Record sheet + pencil or whiteboard
StructureTimed partner rounds with quick solve, compare, and rotate.
SetupTwo facing rows or an inside/outside circle; students need individual recording space.
Directions
  1. Teacher displays one problem page.
  2. Partners solve independently for a short timed round.
  3. Partners compare answers and explain differences.
  4. Teacher shows the solution page.
  5. One row rotates before the next problem.
Goal: Students get repeated short cycles of solving, explaining, and peer checking.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Partners solve first, then compare during the timed round.
  • Students explain one difference before rotating.
  • Each rotation produces a recorded answer or revision.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students rotate before comparing reasoning.
  • Partners only announce answers without showing steps.
  • One student stays with the same partner each round.

Solution: In $4(3+x)^2$, order of operations says evaluate the parentheses, square that quantity, then multiply by $4$. To mean “multiply $3+x$ by $4$, then square,” write $[4(3+x)]^2$.

Solution: Solve $2h+1\le9$: $2h\le8$, so $h\le4$. Both rules are equivalent. The rule $h\le4$ is clearer for students because it states the maximum hours directly; $2h+1\le9$ may show a scoring or workload model.
