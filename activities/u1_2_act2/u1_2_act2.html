Think Trade Agree
Inputs, Outputs, and Functions
Source: practice_set_1_2_3(1).html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Pairs
Materials: Whiteboards or paper
StructureThink independently, trade reasoning with a partner, then agree on a final response.
SetupPairs; each student needs a whiteboard or paper for private thinking before discussion.
Directions
  1. Students think and write a first attempt independently.
  2. Partners trade boards or papers and read each other’s reasoning.
  3. Partners ask one clarifying question before revising.
  4. Partners agree on a final response and prepare to justify it.
  5. Teacher displays the solution page for comparison.
Goal: Students use peer reasoning to refine, not replace, their own thinking.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Both partners have an independent first attempt.
  • Students ask a clarifying question before revising.
  • The final answer reflects agreement and explanation.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One partner starts by copying the other.
  • Partners trade only final answers, not reasoning.
  • Disagreement is ignored instead of resolved.

Solution 4
Current · DOK 4

A table for an input-output rule is flawed: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(2,12),(3,12)$. Revise the relation in two different ways: one revision that makes it a function with a constant increase, and one revision that makes it a function but not a constant-increase rule. Explain both choices.

Solution: Constant-increase revision: replace $(2,12)$ with $(3,12)$ and replace the old $(3,12)$ with $(3,12)$? A cleaner set is $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,12)$, which follows $y=3x+3$. Function but not constant-increase revision: keep one output for each input, such as $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,15)$; each input has one output but the changes are $3,3,6$.
