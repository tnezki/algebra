Source: practice_set_6_5_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: Copies of puzzle
Structure Matching puzzle (self-checking)
Setup Groups of 4. Copies of puzzle
Directions
  1. Students match expressions, answers, or steps.
  2. Pieces form a complete shape (triangle/square).
  3. If correct, puzzle fits perfectly.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Groups read both sides of a match before placing a piece.
  • Students use algebra or context to defend uncertain matches.
  • The puzzle shape is used as a self-check after reasoning.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Pieces are matched by shape before considering the math.
  • One student builds while others watch silently.
  • Groups force pieces to fit and ignore mismatched answers.

1. Current · DOK 1–2

A ball's height is $h(t)=-16t^2+32t+5$. What does $h(0)$ mean?

2. Current · DOK 1–2

A quadratic opens downward. Does its vertex give a maximum or a minimum?

3. Last · DOK 1–2

Choose an efficient method for $x^2-5x+6=0$ and solve.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

A quadratic has zeros $-2$ and $4$. Write a factored expression.

Key:
  1. $h(0)$ is the height when time is $0$. Here $h(0)=5$, so it represents the ball’s initial height.
  2. A downward-opening quadratic has its vertex at the highest point. So the vertex gives a maximum.
  3. Factoring is efficient: $x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$. So the solutions are $x=2$ and $x=3$.
  4. Zeros $-2$ and $4$ give factors $(x+2)$ and $(x-4)$. A factored expression is $(x+2)(x-4)$.
