Equations as Models
Source: practice_set_1_5_3(1).html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Pairs or teams of 3
Materials: Printed Tarsia pieces + scissors if needed
StructureMatching puzzle using question-answer pairs.
SetupTeacher opens the embedded Tarsia generator, generates the puzzle, prints, cuts, and gives pieces to groups.
Directions
  1. Use the embedded generator to print the preloaded puzzle.
  2. Groups match each question side to its correct answer side.
  3. Students justify matches before placing pieces permanently.
  4. Groups check the completed puzzle for unmatched or contradictory edges.
  5. Students complete the exit ticket individually.
Goal: Students connect exact problem statements to correct answers through repeated comparison and justification.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Groups read the exact question before choosing an answer edge.
  • Students justify uncertain matches using math work.
  • Completed puzzles are checked for mismatched edges.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students match by shape only without solving.
  • One student controls all pieces while others watch.
  • Groups leave unmatched edges unexplained.

1In the equation $C=15+3m$, what could $m$ represent if $C$ is cost?$m$ could represent the number of miles, months, minutes, or other units being charged at \$3 each. The $15$ is the starting cost.
2The graph shows two cost models. Estimate the value of $x$ where the costs are equal, then write the equation that the intersection represents. | [Figure: Graph of two linear cost models with an intersection.]The costs are equal at about $x=5$, with cost about $25$. The intersection represents $15+2x=5+4x$.
3Construct a strategy for translating a context into an equation before solving. Apply your strategy to: a phone plan charges \$12 plus \$5 per gigabyte and the total bill is \$47.Define the variable first: let $g$ be gigabytes. Translate the fixed charge and rate: $12+5g=47$. Solve: $5g=35$, so $g=7$. The bill includes $7$ gigabytes.
4Design a one-variable equation model for a realistic school or club situation where the solution is $8$. Include the context, define the variable, write the equation, solve it, and interpret why $8$ makes sense.One valid model: A club has $4$ posters and prints $2$ posters per student helper. If the total is $20$, then $2x+4=20$. Solving gives $2x=16$, so $x=8$. The solution means $8$ helpers are needed.
5Solve $5-(2x-3)=-8+2x$. Show the distribution of the negative sign.Distribute the negative sign: $5-(2x-3)=5-2x+3=8-2x$. Then $8-2x=-8+2x$, so $16=4x$ and $x=4$.
6First simplify both sides of $10-2(2x+1)=4(x-2)$, then solve the resulting linear equation.Left side: $10-2(2x+1)=10-4x-2=8-4x$. Right side: $4(x-2)=4x-8$. Solve $8-4x=4x-8$, so $16=8x$ and $x=2$.
7Identify the first operation you would undo in $\frac{x-4}{3}=7$, then solve.Undo division by $3$ first: multiply both sides by $3$ to get $x-4=21$. Then add $4$, so $x=25$.
8A student says $-2\frac{1}{3}+5\frac{1}{6}=3\frac{2}{3}$. Recompute the sum and identify the step where the likely error occurred.$-2\frac{1}{3}+5\frac{1}{6}=-\frac{14}{6}+\frac{31}{6}=\frac{17}{6}=2\frac{5}{6}$. The likely error was combining fractional parts without correct common denominators.
9Start at $-2.5$ on the number line. Move right $3.75$ units and then left $1.25$ units. Mark the pathway and state the final location. | [Figure: Blank number line from negative ten to ten for modeling signed moves]Start at $-2.5$. Right $3.75$ gives $1.25$. Left $1.25$ gives $0$. The final location is $0$.
10Complete the diamond problem. The top is the product and the bottom is the sum. Explain which operation you used first. | [Figure: Diamond problem with product negative twenty four and sum two]The two numbers are $6$ and $-4$ because $6(-4)=-24$ and $6+(-4)=2$.
11A student solves $3x+5=20$ by subtracting $3$ first and then dividing by $5$, giving $x=\frac{17}{5}$. Critique the pathway and solve correctly.Subtracting $3$ first is incorrect because $3x$ is a term, not a number being added. Correct: $3x+5=20$, subtract $5$ to get $3x=15$, then divide by $3$ to get $x=5$.
12A lab report states: "The sample changed by $-1.2$, then by $-\frac{3}{5}$, so the total change is $-1.7$ because $\frac{3}{5}=0.5$." Revise the model and write the corrected total change with units.$\frac{3}{5}=0.6$, so the corrected total change is $-1.2-0.6=-1.8$ units. The report should revise $0.5$ to $0.6$.
13In the equation $y={5}x+2$, identify the rate of change.The rate of change is $5$.
14A pattern doubles the input and then adds ${3}$. Another pattern adds ${3}$ and then doubles. Compare the outputs when the input is ${5}$.Double then add $3$: $2(5)+3=13$. Add $3$ then double: $2(5+3)=16$. The outputs are different because the operations happen in a different order.
15A student says Plan A is always better because it starts higher. Plan A is $A(x)=x+20$ and Plan B is $B(x)={5}x+4$. Decide whether the claim is reasonable and justify your conclusion.The claim is not always reasonable. Compare $A(x)=x+20$ and $B(x)=5x+4$. They are equal when $x+20=5x+4$, so $16=4x$ and $x=4$. Plan A is better for $x\lt4$, they tie at $4$, and Plan B is better after that.
16Two clubs offer reward plans. Plan A has a larger starting bonus; Plan B earns points faster. Recommend a plan for a student who will participate for ${3}$ weeks and for a student who will participate for ${12}$ weeks. Create or choose equations, then justify using at least two representations.One valid comparison: Plan A $A(w)=20+2w$ and Plan B $B(w)=5+5w$. For $3$ weeks, $A(3)=26$ and $B(3)=20$, so choose A. For $12$ weeks, $A(12)=44$ and $B(12)=65$, so choose B. The equations and table values show the larger start versus faster rate.
17A taxi charges $5+2m$ dollars. Write an equation if the total is $21$.$5+2m=21$.
18Solve $4x-3=13$.$4x-3=13$ gives $4x=16$, so $x=4$.
