Choosing and Defending Solution Methods
Source: practice_set_6_4_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Movement + peer pairs
Materials: Record sheet or worksheet and pencil
Structure Movement + peer interaction
Setup You will need your record sheet or worksheet and a pencil. Keep it with you as you move around the room.
Directions
  1. Work Alone (5 min): Skim every problem you can, then start solving any problem you can. Leave anything you’re stuck on blank for now.
  2. Find a Partner: Walk around and find someone who is ready to share. Compare your completed answers — discuss any that don’t match.
  3. Work Together (5 min): Choose one problem that either of you left blank. Work through it together. Both partners record the solution on their own sheet.
  4. Find Someone New: Thank your partner and repeat steps 2–3 with a different person each time.
  5. Return to your seat with every problem completed and understood — not just filled in.
Goal: Exposure to multiple explanations
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Students begin with independent attempts before walking.
  • Partners choose a stuck problem and both record the shared solution.
  • Students find a new peer after each comparison cycle.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Students walk immediately without trying any problems alone.
  • One partner fills in another student’s sheet.
  • Students keep the same partner for the entire activity.

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Choosing and Defending Solution Methods. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Choose the most efficient method for $x^2-8x+5=0$ and solve.

Solution:

This does not factor quickly over integers because no integer pair multiplies to $5$ and adds to $-8$.

Completing the square is efficient: $x^2-8x=-5$, so $x^2-8x+16=11$.

$(x-4)^2=11$, so $x=4\pm\sqrt{11}$.

Current · DOK 3

Critique Error. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Choosing and Defending Solution Methods. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

A student says the solutions of $x^2-6x-16=0$ are $x=2$ and $x=8$ because the factors are $(x-2)(x-8)$.

Identify the error, correct the solution, and explain how the structure shows the mistake.

Solution:

The proposed factors multiply to $(x-2)(x-8)=x^2-10x+16$, which does not match $x^2-6x-16$.

Correct factors are $(x-8)(x+2)=0$.

So the solutions are $x=8$ and $x=-2$.

Revise A Flawed Model. Create or revise a quadratic task for Choosing and Defending Solution Methods using the constraints below.

  • Include at least two representations among equation, table, graph, or context.

A student claims the provided graph is modeled by $y=4(x-5)^2+60$ and recommends factoring. Revise the model so it matches the graph's maximum, choose an efficient solving method for the intercepts, and defend your choice.

Solution:

The graph has a maximum near $(5,60)$, so the coefficient should be negative, not positive.

A better model is $y=-4(x-5)^2+60$. To find intercepts, completing the square/vertex form is efficient: $0=-4(x-5)^2+60$, so $(x-5)^2=15$ and $x=5\pm\sqrt{15}$.

  1. $x^2-64$
  2. $y^2-6y+9$
  3. $4x^2+4x+1$
  4. $5x^2-45$
Unit 6.4 · Activity 2 · Find Someone WhoSolution 13
Solution 13
Last · DOK 1

Classify each expression as a difference of squares, a perfect-square trinomial, or neither.

  1. $x^2-64$
  2. $y^2-6y+9$
  3. $4x^2+4x+1$
  4. $5x^2-45$
Solution:

$x^2-64$ is a difference of squares.

$y^2-6y+9=(y-3)^2$ is a perfect-square trinomial.

$4x^2+4x+1=(2x+1)^2$ is a perfect-square trinomial.

$5x^2-45=5(x^2-9)$ is not itself a difference of squares as written, but after GCF removal it contains a difference of squares.

Solution 15
Last · DOK 3

A student says the zeros of $y=(x-4)(x+7)$ are $4$ and $7$ because those are the numbers in the factors.

Critique the reasoning and write a corrected explanation.

Solution:

The factor $x-4$ gives zero $x=4$.

The factor $x+7$ gives zero $x=-7$, not $7$.

Correct zeros are $4$ and $-7$.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

What graph feature represents a solution to $f(x)=0$?

Key:
  1. Factoring is efficient because $x^2-9$ is a difference of squares. $(x-3)(x+3)=0$, so $x=3$ or $x=-3$.
  2. The quadratic formula is most reliable when factoring is not quick. It works for any quadratic once $a$, $b$, and $c$ are identified.
  3. $b^2-4ac=4^2-4(1)(4)=16-16=0$. There is one real solution, a repeated root.
  4. A solution to $f(x)=0$ is an $x$-intercept, where the graph crosses or touches the $x$-axis.
