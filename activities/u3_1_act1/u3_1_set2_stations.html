Answer:

One solution. The lines have different slopes, so they intersect exactly once.

Problem 2

A student wants to multiply $3x-3y=4$ by $-3$ for elimination but writes $-9x-3y=4$. Identify the mistake and write the correct equivalent equation.

Answer:

The multiplier must apply to every term and to the right side. The correct equivalent equation is $-9x+9y=-12$.

Problem 14

Classify the system as one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. $\begin{cases}y=3x+2\\2y=6x+4\end{cases}$

Answer:

Infinitely many solutions. The second equation is an equivalent form of the first, so both represent the same line.

Problem 15

Classify the system as one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. $\begin{cases}2x-2y=7\\4x-4y=14\end{cases}$

Answer:

Infinitely many solutions because one equation is a scalar multiple of the other; they represent the same line.

Problem 16

A student tries to eliminate $y$ from $3x+y=8$ and $x-y=4$ by subtracting the equations. Identify the error and state the efficient operation.

Answer:

The $y$-terms are opposites, so the equations should be added. Adding leads to the solution $(3,-1)$.
