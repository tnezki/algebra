Problem 1

Solve by elimination the system.

$\begin{cases}x + 2y = 9\\2x - y = -2\end{cases}$

Answer:

Solution: $(1,4)$. Substitution into both original equations verifies the ordered pair.

Problem 2

Solve by elimination the system.

$\begin{cases}3x - y = -8\\x + y = 0\end{cases}$

Answer:

Solution: $(-2,2)$. Substitution into both original equations verifies the ordered pair.

Problem 3

Classify the system and justify. $\begin{cases}x+y=-2\\3x+3y=-6\end{cases}$

Answer:

Infinitely many solutions because the second equation is exactly $3$ times the first, so both equations represent the same line.

Problem 4

Classify the system as one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. $\begin{cases}y=-2x-4\\y=3x+2\end{cases}$

Answer:

One solution because the lines have different slopes and therefore intersect exactly once.

Problem 19

A student wants to multiply $2x+3y=4$ by $2$ for elimination but writes $4x+3y=4$. Identify the mistake and write the correct equivalent equation.

Answer:

The multiplier must apply to every term and to the right side. The correct equivalent equation is $4x+6y=8$.
