Question 1

Approximate the solutions of $x^2-2x-8=0$.

Solution:

Factor: $x^2-2x-8=(x-4)(x+2)$.

The solutions are $x=4$ and $x=-2$, so the approximations are also $4.0$ and $-2.0$.

Question 2

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Quadratic Formula. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Use the formula to solve $4x^2+4x+1=0$.

Solution:

Use $a=4$, $b=4$, $c=1$.

$x=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{4^2-4(4)(1)}}{2(4)}=\frac{-4\pm 0}{8}=-\frac12$.

There is one repeated real solution: $x=-\frac12$.

Question 5

In $y=\frac{1}{3} x^2$, identify whether the graph is wider or narrower than $y=x^2$.

Solution:

The coefficient $\frac13$ makes vertical values smaller than the parent for the same distance from the vertex.

The graph is wider than $y=x^2$.

Question 6

In $y=-x^2+4$, identify the reflection and vertical translation.

Solution:

The negative sign reflects the graph over the $x$-axis.

The $+4$ translates the graph up $4$ units.

Question 7

If $(2,4)$ is on $f$, what point is on $g(x)=2f(x)$?

Solution:

The input stays $2$ and the output doubles from $4$ to $8$.

The point is $(2,8)$.

Question 8

Use the area model to write the expanded form of $(x+3)(x+5)$. Then combine like terms.

Solution:

The four products are $x^2$, $5x$, $3x$, and $15$.

Combining like terms gives $x^2+8x+15$.

Question 9

Use the row and column labels in the area model to list the four products for $(x-2)(x+4)$, then combine like terms.

Solution:

Products: $x\cdot x=x^2$, $x\cdot 4=4x$, $-2\cdot x=-2x$, and $-2\cdot 4=-8$.

Combine like terms: $x^2+2x-8$.

Question 10

Connect the completed area model to a distributive expression for $(2x-1)(x+3)$, then simplify.

Solution:

Distribute: $(2x-1)(x+3)=2x(x+3)-1(x+3)$.

$=2x^2+6x-x-3=2x^2+5x-3$.

Question 11

Construct a strategy for choosing between factored form, vertex form, and expanded form when answering questions about zeros, maximum or minimum, and initial value.

Solution:

Use factored form for zeros, vertex form for maximum/minimum and axis of symmetry, and expanded form for initial value or standard coefficient comparisons.

The strategy is to identify the feature asked for first, then choose the form that exposes that feature directly.

Question 12

Synthesize an equation, key-point table, and graph description for a function made from $y=|x|$ by reflecting over the $x$-axis, stretching vertically by factor $2$, and translating right $1$ and up $3$. Explain how each representation shows every transformation.

Solution:

Equation: $y=-2|x-1|+3$.

Key points include vertex $(1,3)$ and points $(0,1)$ and $(2,1)$.

Graph description: reflect over the $x$-axis, stretch by $2$, shift right $1$, and up $3$.

Question 13

Identify the $a$, $b$, and $c$ values in each trinomial $ax^2+bx+c$.

  1. $2x^2+5x+3$
  2. $6x^2-x-2$
  3. $x^2+x-72$
Solution:

$2x^2+5x+3$: $a=2$, $b=5$, $c=3$.

$6x^2-x-2$: $a=6$, $b=-1$, $c=-2$.

$x^2+x-72$: $a=1$, $b=1$, $c=-72$.

Question 14

Factor each quadratic completely. If it is not factorable over the integers, explain why not.

  1. $2x^2+3x-5$
  2. $x^2-x-6$
  3. $3x^2+13x+4$
  4. $2x^2+5x+7$
Solution:

$2x^2+3x-5=(2x+5)(x-1)$.

$x^2-x-6=(x-3)(x+2)$.

$3x^2+13x+4=(3x+1)(x+4)$.

$2x^2+5x+7$ is not factorable over the integers because no integer split of $ac=14$ adds to $5$ with the needed signs.

Question 15

A classmate claims $x^2-12x+40$ is almost a perfect square because $40$ is close to $36$. Is that reasoning sufficient? Give a reasonableness argument based on structure, not closeness.

Solution:

Closeness is not sufficient. A perfect-square trinomial with middle term $-12x$ would have half coefficient $-6$ and constant $36$.

$x^2-12x+40=(x-6)^2+4$, so it is not a perfect square even though $40$ is close to $36$.

Question 16

Design a short diamond problem that leads to a quadratic trinomial $ax^2+bx+c$ with $a\ne 1$ and integer factors. Provide the diamond problem, the factored form, and the expanded form.

Solution:

Example diamond: product $-12$ and sum $1$, giving pair $4$ and $-3$.

This can lead to $x^2+x-12=(x+4)(x-3)$. The pair multiplies to the constant and adds to the middle coefficient.
