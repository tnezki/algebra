Question 1

Use the discriminant to tell whether $3x^2+2x+5=0$ has real solutions.

Solution:

Compute $b^2-4ac=2^2-4(3)(5)=4-60=-56$.

Because the discriminant is negative, the equation has no real solutions.

Question 2

Solve $x^2-4x-1=0$ exactly.

Solution:

Use the quadratic formula with $a=1$, $b=-4$, $c=-1$.

$x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{(-4)^2-4(1)(-1)}}{2}=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{20}}{2}=2\pm\sqrt5$.

Solution:

Approximate roots are $x\approx 2.70$ and $x\approx 6.30$, which match the graph estimates.

Question 4

Reasonableness/Estimation Argument. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Quadratic Formula. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

A model gives solutions $t=-0.2$ and $t=3.4$ seconds. Decide which solution(s) are meaningful and justify using the context and the equation form.

Solution:

In a time context, negative time usually represents a value before the model starts, so $t=-0.2$ seconds is not meaningful for the event after launch.

The meaningful solution is $t=3.4$ seconds. In factored form, the zeros tell when the modeled quantity is zero, but context restricts the domain to $t\ge 0$.

Question 7

Write a transformed absolute value equation with vertex $(1,4)$, reflected over the $x$-axis, and vertically stretched by factor $3$.

Solution:

Start with $y=|x|$. Use vertex form and include reflection/stretch.

A correct equation is $y=-3|x-1|+4$.

Question 8

Write a transformed quadratic equation with vertex $(-2,5)$ that opens down and is vertically compressed by factor $\frac{1}{2}$.

Solution:

Use quadratic vertex form $y=a(x-h)^2+k$.

Opening down and compressed by $\frac12$ gives $a=-\frac12$, so $y=-\frac12(x+2)^2+5$.

Question 9

Apply the transformation $g(x)=-2f(x)+1$ to the points $(-1,2)$, $(0,0)$, and $(1,2)$. List the transformed points.

Solution:

Transform each output by $y_{new}=-2y+1$.

$(-1,2)\to(-1,-3)$, $(0,0)\to(0,1)$, and $(1,2)\to(1,-3)$.

Question 10

Two methods can find the axis of symmetry for $y=(x+2)(x-6)$. Explain a method using the zeros and a method using expanded form, then compare the results.

Solution:

Using zeros, the axis is halfway between $-2$ and $6$: $x=\frac{-2+6}{2}=2$.

Expanded form is $y=x^2-4x-12$, so $x=-\frac{b}{2a}=\frac{4}{2}=2$. Both methods agree.

Question 11

A model predicts a ball height with vertex $(4,80)$ and zeros at $0$ and $8$. Explain why those features are reasonable together, then identify what would be unreasonable about zeros at $0$ and $6$ with the same vertex.

Solution:

Zeros at $0$ and $8$ are symmetric around $x=4$, so a vertex at $x=4$ is reasonable.

Zeros at $0$ and $6$ would have midpoint $3$, so the axis of symmetry would be $x=3$, not $x=4$; that would be unreasonable with vertex $(4,80)$.

Question 12

Synthesize a graph description, equation, and table for a translated quadratic with minimum at $(2,-3)$ and $y$-intercept $1$. Explain how each representation shows the minimum.

Solution:

Equation: $y=(x-2)^2-3$ has minimum $(2,-3)$.

To make the $y$-intercept $1$, check $x=0$: $(0-2)^2-3=1$.

Table: $(0,1),(1,-2),(2,-3),(3,-2),(4,1)$; the graph opens upward with lowest point at $(2,-3)$.

Question 13

Expand each product to check the middle term.

  1. $(x+4)(x+2)$
  2. $(x-5)(x+1)$
  3. $(2x+3)(x-1)$
Solution:

$(x+4)(x+2)=x^2+6x+8$.

$(x-5)(x+1)=x^2-4x-5$.

$(2x+3)(x-1)=2x^2+x-3$.

Question 14

Factor each trinomial with leading coefficient $1$.

  1. $x^2+5x+6$
  2. $x^2-6x-7$
  3. $x^2-11x+24$
  4. $x^2-4x-32$
Solution:

$x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$.

$x^2-6x-7=(x-7)(x+1)$.

$x^2-11x+24=(x-3)(x-8)$.

$x^2-4x-32=(x-8)(x+4)$.

Question 15

For $36a^4b^2-24a^3b+12a^2b$, find the greatest common monomial factor and write the factored form.

Solution:

The GCF is $12a^2b$.

$36a^4b^2-24a^3b+12a^2b=12a^2b(3a^2b-2a+1)$.

Question 16

Factor $36x^2-49$ using the difference-of-squares pattern. Then factor it again by thinking of it as a product of conjugates. Compare the two explanations.

Solution:

Use difference of squares: $36x^2-49=(6x)^2-7^2$.

So $36x^2-49=(6x-7)(6x+7)$. These are conjugates, whose middle terms cancel.
