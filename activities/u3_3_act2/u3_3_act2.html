Unit 3 · Section 3 · Activity 2
Mathematical Hot Seat
Writing and Solving Systems from Situations
Source: practice_set_3_3_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Teams of 3–4
Materials: Problem pages and whiteboards or paper
StructureQuestioning round with one student explaining and peers probing
SetupStudents work in teams of 3–4. One student is in the hot seat for each problem.
Directions
  1. The hot-seat student explains the problem setup or first step aloud.
  2. Teammates ask clarifying questions before anyone gives a final answer.
  3. The team solves together while the hot-seat student defends or revises the reasoning.
  4. Rotate the hot-seat role for the next problem, then check the solution page.
Goal: Strengthen mathematical vocabulary, justification, and responsive explanation.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • The hot-seat student speaks first using math vocabulary.
  • Teammates ask questions before giving answers.
  • The role rotates so each student explains.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One student stays in the hot seat all period.
  • Questions become guesses instead of reasoning probes.
  • The group jumps to the key before defending an answer.

Solution 2
Current · DOK 2

A streaming service charges \$8 per month plus a \$20 setup fee. Another charges \$12 per month with no setup fee. Write and solve a system to find the break-even month.

Solution:

Let $m$ be months and $C$ be cost. First service: $C=8m+20$. Second service: $C=12m$. Set equal: $8m+20=12m$, so $20=4m$ and $m=5$. The break-even month is $5$.

Solution 4
Current · DOK 4

A club is choosing between two bus companies. Company A charges \$180 plus \$6 per student. Company B charges \$90 plus \$9 per student. A draft graph labels the intersection as $(30,270)$ but the written conclusion says “Company A is always cheaper.”

  1. Revise the written conclusion so it matches the model.
  2. Explain what each coordinate of the intersection means.
  3. State which company is cheaper for 20 students and for 40 students.
Solution:

The intersection $(30,270)$ means at $30$ students, both companies cost \$270. Company A is not always cheaper. For $20$ students: A is $180+6(20)=300$ and B is $90+9(20)=270$, so B is cheaper. For $40$ students: A is $420$ and B is $450$, so A is cheaper.

You are given a graph with no labeled intercept but two clear points: $(-2,9)$ and $(4,-3)$. Construct a strategy to write the equation accurately and explain why your strategy avoids guessing from the picture.

Solution:

Use the two clear points instead of guessing the intercept. Slope is $\frac{-3-9}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-12}{6}=-2$. Substitute into $y=mx+b$ with $(4,-3)$: $-3=-2(4)+b$, so $b=5$. Equation: $y=-2x+5$.

Exit Ticket
1. Current · DOK 1–2

A class sold $2$ and $5$ raffle tickets for a total of $74$ from 25 tickets. Write a system.

2. Current · DOK 1–2

After solving a context system, what should you check about the solution?

3. Last · DOK 1–2

Find the value of $y$ in $y=x^2$ when $x=-3$.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

Solve $x/4=6$.

Key:

  1. Let $x$ be the number of \$2 tickets and $y$ be the number of \$5 tickets. The system is $x+y=25$ and $2x+5y=74$.

  2. Check that the numbers make sense in the context, including units, whole-number restrictions, and whether the solution satisfies both original conditions.

  3. If $y=x^2$ and $x=-3$, then $y=(-3)^2=9$.

  4. $x/4=6$ gives $x=24$.
