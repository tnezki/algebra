5. Create three input-output pairs for \(y=-3x+8\) using x=-2, 1, and 4. Then describe the direction of change you would expect in a matching table or graph.

Solution: The pairs are (-2,14), (1,5), (4,-4). Outputs decrease 3 for every increase of 1 in x.

6. Relationship A and Relationship B both change by -2 per +1 in x. A has output 9 at x=0; B has output 4 at x=0. Are they the same relationship? Explain with evidence.

Solution: No. They have the same rate but different starting values, so their outputs remain 5 units apart.

7. A student says two linear representations must match because they have the same y-intercept. Describe one additional value-based or rate-based check that could prove or disprove the claim.

Solution: Check their rates or compare outputs at a common nonzero input. Same intercept alone does not guarantee the same relationship.

Solution: Outputs are -7, -3, 1, 5. Pairs: (-1,-7), (1,-3), (3,1), (5,5).

Station 4 Solutions • Set 1
Answer Key
13. Write a short real-world context that could be represented by \(y=7x+2\). Identify what 7 and 2 mean in your context.

Solution: Example: a club starts with 2 points and earns 7 points per round; 2 is the initial amount and 7 is the rate per round.

14. Two linear relationships both start at -4. A has rate 2 and B has rate 5. Use x=3 to show with outputs that the relationships are different.

Solution: A(3)=2; B(3)=11. Different outputs at the same input prove the relationships differ.

15. Write a linear equation that includes the points (0,-6) and (4,10), then check both points.

Solution: Rate=(10-(-6))/4=4, so \(y=4x-6\). Both pairs satisfy the equation.

16. Explain why rearranging the rows of a table does not change the relationship, while reversing every ordered pair usually does.

Solution: Row order is only display order. Reversing pairs swaps input and output, which changes the correspondence.

Station 5 Solutions • Set 1
Answer Key
17. Start at the origin, move 7 units left and 5 units up. Write the ordered pair and name its quadrant.

Solution: (-7,5), Quadrant II.

18. The point is (3,-6). Describe the horizontal and vertical movements from the origin in the correct coordinate order.

Solution: Move 3 units right, then 6 units down.

19. A graph will show days as the input and pages read as the output. Which quantity belongs on each axis, and what does the point (4,65) mean?

Solution: Days belongs on the x-axis and pages on the y-axis. (4,65) means 65 pages have been read after 4 days.

20. A student treats the pair (2,17), where 2 is hours and 17 is cost, as if cost were the horizontal coordinate. Diagnose the axis mistake.

Solution: The input hours should be first/horizontal; cost is the output and belongs second/vertical.

Station 6 Solutions • Set 1
Answer Key
21. On a y-axis, the labeled values 0, 6, and 12 are separated by three equal small intervals each time. What does one small interval represent?

Solution: 2 units.

22. On an x-axis, 0 and 8 are separated by four equal small intervals. What is the coordinate three small intervals to the right of 0?

Solution: Each interval is 2, so the coordinate is 6.

23. A line crosses the x-axis at x=-5 and the y-axis at y=4. Write both intercepts as ordered pairs and identify which coordinate must be zero in each.

Solution: x-intercept (-5,0); y-intercept (0,4). At an x-intercept y=0; at a y-intercept x=0.

24. A tank graph uses time on x and liters remaining on y. The y-intercept is (0,32) and the x-intercept is (8,0). Interpret both intercepts in context.

Solution: The tank starts with 32 L at time 0 and reaches 0 L after 8 time units.
