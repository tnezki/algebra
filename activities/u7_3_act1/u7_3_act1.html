  • Groups crowd one station instead of rotating when signaled.
  • Students copy the answer page before attempting the station card.

Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Read the graph. Estimate the y-intercept, then use the y-values at two consecutive integer x-values to decide whether the multiplier is greater than 1 or between 0 and 1.

Solution: The graph has y-intercept about $2$. Consecutive y-values increase by a multiplier about $1.5$, so the multiplier is greater than $1$ and the graph shows growth.

Question 2

Read the graph. Estimate the horizontal asymptote, then use the y-values at two consecutive integer x-values to decide whether the multiplier is greater than 1 or between 0 and 1.

Solution: The graph approaches the horizontal asymptote $y=0$. Consecutive y-values are multiplied by about $0.5$, so the multiplier is between $0$ and $1$ and the graph shows decay.

Question 3

Compare the graphs of $f(x)=2(2)^x$ and $g(x)=5(2)^x$ using y-intercepts and steepness. Use the graph as evidence.

Solution: $f(0)=2$ and $g(0)=5$, so $g$ has the larger y-intercept. Both use multiplier $2$, so they have the same repeated growth factor, but $g$ stays $2.5$ times as large as $f$ and appears steeper/higher on the graph.

Question 4

Analyze $f(x)=8(0.5)^x$ using two methods: a table of values and graph features. Use both methods to explain the domain, range, and long-run behavior.

Solution: A table gives $f(0)=8$, $f(1)=4$, $f(2)=2$, $f(3)=1$. The domain is all real numbers, the range is $y\gt0$, and the graph decreases toward the horizontal asymptote $y=0$ without crossing it.

Unit 7.3 · Activity 1 · Stations Algebra 1
Station 2 Answers

Question 5

Factor completely: $2x^2-2x-4$.

Solution: Factor out $2$ first: $2x^2-2x-4=2(x^2-x-2)$. Then $x^2-x-2=(x-2)(x+1)$, so the complete factorization is $2(x-2)(x+1)$.

Question 6

Show the two steps for $4x^2-24x+36$: factor out the GCF first, then factor the remaining expression.

Solution: First factor the GCF: $4x^2-24x+36=4(x^2-6x+9)$. Then factor the perfect square: $4(x-3)^2$.

Question 7

Factor completely: $9x^4-4y^2$.

Solution: This is a difference of squares: $9x^4-4y^2=(3x^2)^2-(2y)^2=(3x^2-2y)(3x^2+2y)$.

Question 8

For each expression, decide the most efficient pattern and factor it. $25r^2-81$ $16p^2+40p+25$ $3x^2-27$

Solution: $25r^2-81$ is a difference of squares: $(5r-9)(5r+9)$. $16p^2+40p+25$ is a perfect-square trinomial: $(4p+5)^2$. $3x^2-27$ has a GCF and difference of squares: $3(x^2-9)=3(x-3)(x+3)$.

Station 3 Answers

Question 9

Complete the table and use it to factor the trinomial. Expression First square Last square Middle check $4x^2-20x+25$ _____ _____ _____ Use the signs to write the correct squared binomial.

Solution: First square: $(2x)^2=4x^2$. Last square: $5^2=25$. Middle check: $2(2x)(5)=20x$, and the sign is negative, so $4x^2-20x+25=(2x-5)^2$.

Question 10

Two students rewrite $x^2-4x-5$. Student A uses $(x-5)(x+1)$ to answer where the function has zeros. Student B uses $(x-2)^2-9$ to answer the same question. Compare the two methods and decide which is more direct for this question.

Solution: Student A is more direct for zeros because $(x-5)(x+1)=0$ immediately gives $x=5$ and $x=-1$. Student B can also find zeros from $(x-2)^2-9=0$, but it requires moving $9$ and taking square roots.

Question 11

A context asks for the maximum height of a quadratic model. A student chooses factored form because it shows the zeros. Is that a reasonable form choice? Give a reasoned argument and name a more useful form if needed.

Solution: Factored form is not the most useful for maximum height because it emphasizes zeros. Vertex form is more useful because the vertex gives the maximum or minimum directly. For a downward-opening quadratic, the vertex y-value is the maximum height.

Question 12

A student says $25a^2-20ab+16b^2=(5a-4b)^2$. Revise the expression or the factorization so the statement becomes true, and explain the exact term that caused the mismatch.

Solution: $(5a-4b)^2=25a^2-40ab+16b^2$, so the given middle term $-20ab$ is the mismatch. To make the factorization true, revise the expression to $25a^2-40ab+16b^2$. To keep the expression, the factorization is not a single squared binomial.

Station 4 Answers

Question 13

State why adding $25$ completes $x^2-10x$.

Solution: Half of $-10$ is $-5$, and $(-5)^2=25$. Adding $25$ makes $x^2-10x+25=(x-5)^2$.

Question 14

Convert $x^2-4x+1$ to vertex form.

Solution: Complete the square: $x^2-4x+1=(x^2-4x+4)-3=(x-2)^2-3$. Vertex form is $y=(x-2)^2-3$.

Question 15

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Completing the Square . Show the algebra that supports your answer. Rewrite $y=x^2-10x+18$ in vertex form and name the vertex.

Solution: Complete the square: $y=x^2-10x+18=(x^2-10x+25)-7=(x-5)^2-7$. The vertex is $(5,-7)$.

Question 16

Reasonableness/Estimation Argument. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Quadratic Formula . Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence. A model gives solutions $t=-0.2$ and $t=3.4$ seconds. Decide which solution(s...

Solution: In a time context, $t=-0.2$ seconds is usually not meaningful after the start. The solution $t=3.4$ seconds is meaningful because it occurs after time $0$. The equation can have two algebraic roots, but the context filters the reasonable one.
