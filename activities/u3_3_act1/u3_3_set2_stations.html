Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Which phrase usually suggests a rate in a linear model: “each,” “total,” or “both”?

Solution:

“Each” usually suggests a rate in a linear model.

Question 2

A streaming service charges \$8 per month plus a \$20 setup fee. Another charges \$12 per month with no setup fee. Write and solve a system to find the break-even month.

Solution:

Let $m$ be months and $C$ be cost. First service: $C=8m+20$. Second service: $C=12m$. Set equal: $8m+20=12m$, so $20=4m$ and $m=5$. The break-even month is $5$.

Question 3

A student models a coin problem with $n+q=44$ and $5n+25q=6.80$. The student says the system is ready to solve because the money equation uses cents and dollars together.

Assess the reasonableness of the model, revise it if needed, and explain how the units affect the solution.

Solution:

The model is not reasonable because the money equation mixes cents and dollars. Use either $5n+25q=680$ in cents or $0.05n+0.25q=6.80$ in dollars. Consistent units are needed so the solution counts coins correctly.

Question 4

A club is choosing between two bus companies. Company A charges \$180 plus \$6 per student. Company B charges \$90 plus \$9 per student. A draft graph labels the intersection as $(30,270)$ but the written conclusion says “Company A is always cheaper.”

  1. Revise the written conclusion so it matches the model.
  2. Explain what each coordinate of the intersection means.
  3. State which company is cheaper for 20 students and for 40 students.
Solution:

The intersection $(30,270)$ means at $30$ students, both companies cost \$270. Company A is not always cheaper. For $20$ students: A is $180+6(20)=300$ and B is $90+9(20)=270$, so B is cheaper. For $40$ students: A is $420$ and B is $450$, so A is cheaper.

Question 9

Rewrite $4(2x+3)+5x$ in an equivalent simplified form. Show the distribution and combining steps.

Solution:

Distribute first: $4(2x+3)+5x=8x+12+5x$. Combine like terms: $13x+12$.

Question 10

The rectangle model represents a product. Fill in the missing cells and write the product as a sum.

Solution:

The rectangle has side parts $2x$ and $3$ by $x$ and $5$. The cells are $2x^2$, $10x$, $3x$, and $15$. The product as a sum is $2x^2+13x+15$.

Question 11

Construct a strategy for translating a context into an equation before solving. Apply your strategy to: a phone plan charges \$12 plus \$5 per gigabyte and the total bill is \$47.

Solution:

Define the variable, identify the starting fee and rate, write an equation, then solve. Let $g$ be gigabytes. $12+5g=47$, so $5g=35$ and $g=7$.

Question 12

A table for an input-output rule is flawed: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(2,12),(3,12)$. Revise the relation in two different ways: one revision that makes it a function with a constant increase, and one revision that makes it a function but not a constant-increase rule. Explain both choices.

Solution:

Constant-increase revision: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,12)$, which is a function and linear. Function but not constant-increase revision: $(0,3),(1,6),(2,9),(3,15)$; each input appears once but the differences are not constant.

Question 15

You are given a graph with no labeled intercept but two clear points: $(-2,9)$ and $(4,-3)$. Construct a strategy to write the equation accurately and explain why your strategy avoids guessing from the picture.

Solution:

Use the two clear points instead of guessing the intercept. Slope is $\frac{-3-9}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-12}{6}=-2$. Substitute into $y=mx+b$ with $(4,-3)$: $-3=-2(4)+b$, so $b=5$. Equation: $y=-2x+5$.

Question 16

Design a real-world graphing situation with a positive slope and a positive starting value. Give the equation, axis labels, and four ordered pairs that would make a clear graph.

Solution:

Example: a runner starts $10$ meters from a marker and moves away at $3$ meters per second. Equation: $d=3t+10$. Axes: time in seconds and distance in meters. Points: $(0,10),(1,13),(2,16),(3,19)$.
