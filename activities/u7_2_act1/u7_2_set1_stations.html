Station 1 Solutions

A bacteria colony begins with 125 cells and decreases by 15% each hour. Write an exponential equation for the amount after $t$ hours.

Solution:

$A=125(\frac{17}{20})^t$.

For $f(x)=15(\frac{9}{10})^x$, identify the initial value and the growth or decay factor.

Solution:

Initial value $\displaystyle 15$; factor $\frac{9}{10}$ (decay).

Station 1 Solutions

A quantity begins at 200 and increases by 12% per period. Write a model for its value after $t$ periods.

Solution:

$A(t)=200(\frac{28}{25})^t$.

A quantity begins at 800 and decreases by 15% per period. Write a model for its value after $t$ periods.

Solution:

$A(t)=800(\frac{17}{20})^t$.

Station 2 Solutions

Use $f(x)=8(\frac{3}{2})^x$ to find $f(4)$.

Solution:

$f(4)=\frac{81}{2}$.

An exponential function has $f(0)=16$ and $f(2)=4$. The factor is positive. Determine the per-unit factor and write the function.

Solution:

Because two equal steps multiply by $\frac{1}{4}$, the positive per-step factor is $\frac{1}{2}$. Thus $f(x)=16(\frac{1}{2})^x$.

Station 2 Solutions

The graph below is consistent with a model whose $y$-intercept is 2.5. Compare the candidates $y=2.5(1.4)^x$ and $y=1.4(2.5)^x$. Which equation fits the graph more directly? Use the $y$-intercept first, then check the multiplicative growth.

Solution:

$y=2.5(1.4)^x$ fits: its initial value is 2.5, matching the $y$-intercept, and its base is greater than 1 so the graph grows.

Solution:

$f(5)=128$.

An exponential function has $f(0)=4$ and $f(2)=16$. The factor is positive. Determine the per-unit factor and write the function.

Solution:

Across 2 equal steps the outputs multiply by $4$, so the positive per-step factor is $2$. Thus $f(x)=4(2)^x$.

Solution:

A student claims $\displaystyle 9 x^{3} + 12 x^{2}=3x^{2}(3x+4)$. Determine whether the claim is correct. Verify by multiplication and, if needed, give the correct factored form.

Solution:

The claim is correct; distributing the factor reproduces $\displaystyle 9 x^{3} + 12 x^{2}$.
