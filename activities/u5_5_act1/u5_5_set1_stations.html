Question 1

Identify the zeros from each factored-form function.

  1. $y=(x-4)(x+5)$
  2. $y=-3(x+2)(x-1)$
  3. $y=2(x-6)^2$
Solution:
  1. Zeros: $4$ and $-5$.
  2. Zeros: $-2$ and $1$.
  3. Zero: $6$ with multiplicity $2$.

Question 2

Give the y-intercept of each function without rewriting it.

  1. $y=x^2-4x-5$
  2. $y=-2x^2+8x+10$
  3. $y=\frac{1}{2}x^2-3x+1$
Solution:
  1. $y$-intercept: $(0,-5)$.
  2. $y$-intercept: $(0,10)$.
  3. $y$-intercept: $(0,1)$.

Question 3

Complete the table to compare what each form reveals for $y=x^2-4x-5$.

FormExpressionFeature revealed
standard$x^2-4x-5$_____
factored$(x-5)(x+1)$_____
vertex$(x-2)^2-9$_____
Solution:

Standard form $x^2-4x-5$ reveals the $y$-intercept $-5$.

Factored form $(x-5)(x+1)$ reveals zeros $5$ and $-1$.

Vertex form $(x-2)^2-9$ reveals vertex $(2,-9)$.

Question 4

A context asks for the maximum height of a quadratic model. A student chooses factored form because it shows the zeros. Is that a reasonable form choice? Give a reasoned argument and name a more useful form if needed.

Solution:

Factored form is reasonable if the context asks when the height is $0$, but not most direct for maximum height.

Vertex form is more useful because it directly shows the vertex, which gives the maximum or minimum value.

Question 10

A student models a coin problem with $n+q=44$ and $5n+25q=6.80$. The student says the system is ready to solve because the money equation uses cents and dollars together.

Assess the reasonableness of the model, revise it if needed, and explain how the units affect the solution.

Solution:

The model mixes units: $5n+25q$ is in cents, but $6.80$ is dollars.

Revise to $n+q=44$ and $5n+25q=680$, or use dollars as $0.05n+0.25q=6.80$.

Using cents: $n=44-q$, so $5(44-q)+25q=680$. Then $220+20q=680$, $20q=460$, and $q=23$. Thus $n=21$.

Question 11

A school store sells pencils and pens. A student writes $p+n=60$ and $p+2n=95$ without defining variables.

Analyze the structure of the equations and write a precise interpretation for each variable and coefficient so the model makes sense.

Solution:

One interpretation is: $p$ = number of pencils and $n$ = number of pens. Then $p+n=60$ means 60 total items.

If pencils cost $1$ and pens cost $2$, then $p+2n=95$ means total cost is $95$.

The coefficients show the per-item cost, so variables must be defined before the equations have contextual meaning.

Question 14

Name two transformations shown by $y=-\frac{1}{2}(x+4)^2-3$.

Solution:

The negative sign reflects the graph across the $x$-axis, the factor $\frac{1}{2}$ vertically shrinks it, $x+4$ shifts it left $4$, and $-3$ shifts it down $3$.

Question 15

Use the blank coordinate plane to graph $y=-\frac{1}{2}(x-2)^2+4$. Plot the vertex and at least four transformed key points.

Solution:

The vertex is $(2,4)$. Parent points relative to the vertex transform to $(1,3.5)$, $(3,3.5)$, $(0,2)$, and $(4,2)$.

Plot those with the vertex on the blank coordinate plane.

Question 16

A model predicts a ball height with vertex $(4,80)$ and zeros at $0$ and $8$. Explain why those features are reasonable together, then identify what would be unreasonable about zeros at $0$ and $6$ with the same vertex.

Solution:

Zeros at $0$ and $8$ are symmetric around $x=4$, matching the vertex $x$-coordinate. A vertex height of $80$ is reasonable for the maximum halfway between the zeros.

Zeros at $0$ and $6$ would have axis of symmetry $x=3$, so they would not match a vertex at $x=4$.
