Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Choose the factor pair that factors $x^2+5x+6$.

  1. $1$ and $6$
  2. $2$ and $3$
  3. $-2$ and $-3$
  4. $5$ and $6$
Solution:

The correct pair is $2$ and $3$ because $2\cdot3=6$ and $2+3=5$.

So $x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$.

Question 2

Expand each product to check the middle term.

  1. $(x+4)(x+2)$
  2. $(x-5)(x+1)$
  3. $(2x+3)(x-1)$
Solution:
  1. $(x+4)(x+2)=x^2+6x+8$; middle term $6x$.
  2. $(x-5)(x+1)=x^2-4x-5$; middle term $-4x$.
  3. $(2x+3)(x-1)=2x^2+x-3$; middle term $x$.

Question 3

Factor $12x^2+11x-5$ by splitting the middle term. Then verify by multiplying your binomials.

Solution:

For $12x^2+11x-5$, $ac=12(-5)=-60$. Numbers with product $-60$ and sum $11$ are $15$ and $-4$.

$12x^2+15x-4x-5=3x(4x+5)-1(4x+5)=(3x-1)(4x+5)$.

Check: $(3x-1)(4x+5)=12x^2+15x-4x-5=12x^2+11x-5$.

Question 4

Analyze the structure of $kx^2+7x-6$. Find two possible integer values of $k$ that make the trinomial factorable over the integers, and explain how the product-sum structure changes.

Solution:

Two possible values are $k=3$ and $k=5$.

For $k=3$, $3x^2+7x-6$ has $ac=-18$; use $9$ and $-2$, so $3x^2+9x-2x-6=(3x-2)(x+3)$.

For $k=5$, $5x^2+7x-6$ has $ac=-30$; use $10$ and $-3$, so $5x^2+10x-3x-6=(5x-3)(x+2)$.

Question 10

For the taxi plans \$5 plus \$2 per mile and \$11 plus \$1.25 per mile, compare solving by graphing, substitution, and elimination. Which method gives the cleanest paper-only solution, and why?

Solution:

Set the costs equal: $5+2m=11+1.25m$.

$0.75m=6$, so $m=8$ miles. Substitution/equating expressions is cleanest on paper because both plans are already written as cost expressions.

Question 11

A student models a coin problem with $n+q=44$ and $5n+25q=6.80$. The student says the system is ready to solve because the money equation uses cents and dollars together.

Assess the reasonableness of the model, revise it if needed, and explain how the units affect the solution.

Solution:

The model mixes units: $5n+25q$ is in cents, but $6.80$ is dollars.

Revise to $n+q=44$ and $5n+25q=680$, or use dollars as $0.05n+0.25q=6.80$.

Using cents: $n=44-q$, so $5(44-q)+25q=680$. Then $220+20q=680$, $20q=460$, and $q=23$. Thus $n=21$.

Question 12

A poster claims to model “students need at least 20 total practice problems, with $x$ online problems and $y$ paper problems” using $x+y\lt 20$ and a dashed boundary.

  1. Revise the inequality so it matches the phrase “at least.”
  2. Describe the correct boundary and shading.
  3. Give one point that should be feasible and one point that should not.
Solution:

“At least 20” means $x+y\ge 20$.

The boundary is solid because equality is included, and the shading is on the side where the total is $20$ or more.

Example feasible point: $(10,10)$. Example non-feasible point: $(5,5)$.

Question 13

A graph is a straight line with negative slope. Name the family and describe the direction of change.

Solution:

The family is linear. With negative slope, the graph decreases from left to right.

Question 14

A table begins $2, 5, 10, 17, 26$ for consecutive inputs. Identify the repeated pattern that supports a family choice.

Solution:

First differences are $3,5,7,9$; second differences are $2,2,2$.

Constant second differences support a quadratic family.

Question 15

A student says both relationships in the figure are linear because they both increase. Estimate the outputs at three equally spaced $x$-values for each graph, compare the first differences, and identify one feature that separates the families.

Solution:

Increasing alone is not enough to prove linearity. Graph A is linear because equal $x$-steps give roughly equal output changes.

Graph B is exponential because equal $x$-steps show growing differences/ratios rather than constant first differences.

Question 16

You need to write a translated absolute value function with vertex $(2,-5)$. Construct a strategy that starts from key points instead of memorizing a rule, then use it to write the equation.

Solution:

Start from the parent absolute value key points around $(0,0)$, then shift every point right $2$ and down $5$ so the vertex becomes $(2,-5)$.

The equation is $y=|x-2|-5$.
