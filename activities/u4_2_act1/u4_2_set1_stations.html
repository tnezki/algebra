Station 1 Answers

Question 1

The parent vertex $(0,0)$ moves to $(5,-1)$. Write the translation in words.

Solution: The point moves right $5$ units and down $1$ unit.

Question 2

The point $(2,4)$ on $f$ moves to $(2,9)$ on $g$. What vertical translation occurred?

Solution: The $x$-coordinate stayed $2$ and the $y$-coordinate increased from $4$ to $9$, so the graph moved up $5$ units.

Question 3

Use the table to decide whether $g$ is a vertical translation of $f$. If so, name the shift.

$x$-1012
$f(x)$1014
$g(x)$4347

Solution: Compare $g(x)-f(x)$ for each row: $4-1=3$, $3-0=3$, $4-1=3$, and $7-4=3$. The difference is always $3$, so $g$ is a vertical translation of $f$ up $3$ units.

Question 4

You need to write a translated absolute value function with vertex $(2,-5)$. Construct a strategy that starts from key points instead of memorizing a rule, then use it to write the equation.

Solution: Start with the parent absolute value vertex $(0,0)$. To place the vertex at $(2,-5)$, move every key point right $2$ and down $5$. For example, $(0,0)$ becomes $(2,-5)$, $(1,1)$ becomes $(3,-4)$, and $(-1,1)$ becomes $(1,-4)$. The equation is $y=|x-2|-5$.

Question 5

For $y=2x-5$, identify the slope and $y$-intercept.

Solution: In $y=2x-5$, the slope is $2$ and the $y$-intercept is $-5$, or the point $(0,-5)$.

Question 6

Which point is the $y$-intercept of a line that crosses the $y$-axis at $6$?

Solution: The $y$-intercept is the point where $x=0$. If the line crosses the $y$-axis at $6$, the point is $(0,6)$.

Question 7

Complete a table for $y=-2x+7$ using $x=-1,0,1,2$, then graph the line on the blank coordinate plane.

$x$-1012
$y$9753

Plot $(-1,9)$, $(0,7)$, $(1,5)$, and $(2,3)$ and draw a line through them.

Question 8

A subscription begins with a \$15 setup charge and costs \$6 each month. Graph the first six months using month as $x$ and total cost as $y$.

Solution: The model is $y=15+6x$. For months $0$ through $6$, the points are $(0,15)$, $(1,21)$, $(2,27)$, $(3,33)$, $(4,39)$, $(5,45)$, and $(6,51)$. Plot these and draw the increasing line.

Question 9

A line goes through $(0,5)$ and $(3,-1)$. Graph the line, then use the graph to estimate where it crosses the $x$-axis.

Solution: The slope through $(0,5)$ and $(3,-1)$ is $\frac{-1-5}{3-0}=-2$. The equation is $y=-2x+5$. Set $y=0$: $0=-2x+5$, so $x=2.5$. The graph crosses the $x$-axis at about $(2.5,0)$.

Question 10

A model for a school fundraiser is $M=250+45d$, where $d$ is days. A student predicts \$4,750 after $100$ days. Is the prediction arithmetically correct and contextually reasonable for a short fundraiser? Justify both parts.

Solution: Compute $M=250+45(100)=250+4500=4750$, so the arithmetic is correct. Contextually, \$4,750 after $100$ days may not be reasonable for a short fundraiser because $100$ days is a long time compared with the phrase “short fundraiser.”

Question 11

You are given a graph with no labeled intercept but two clear points: $(-2,9)$ and $(4,-3)$. Construct a strategy to write the equation accurately and explain why your strategy avoids guessing from the picture.

Solution: Use the two clear points instead of guessing. Slope $m=\frac{-3-9}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-12}{6}=-2$. Substitute $(-2,9)$ into $y=mx+b$: $9=-2(-2)+b=4+b$, so $b=5$. The equation is $y=-2x+5$. This avoids guessing because it uses exact coordinates and slope.

Question 12

Design two lines that intersect at $(2,5)$ but have different slopes. Give equations and explain how the graph would show the intersection.

Solution: Example: $y=2x+1$ and $y=-x+7$. At $x=2$, both give $y=5$, so both pass through $(2,5)$. The slopes $2$ and $-1$ are different, so on the graph the lines cross at $(2,5)$ instead of overlapping.

Question 13

Which form is usually most convenient for graphing quickly: $y=mx+b$ or $Ax+By=C$? Explain in one sentence.

Solution: $y=mx+b$ is usually more convenient for quick graphing because it shows the slope and $y$-intercept directly.

Question 14

For $x=3$ and $y=-2$, state the point of intersection as an ordered pair.

Solution: A system has one solution when the two lines intersect at exactly one point.

Question 15

Use elimination to find the intersection point of the lines.

$$\begin{aligned}x=-2y-3\\4y-x=9\end{aligned}$$

Solution: Add the equations: $4x+y=18$ and $-4x+2y=-3$ give $3y=15$, so $y=5$. Substitute into $4x+y=18$: $4x+5=18$, so $4x=13$ and $x=\frac{13}{4}$. The solution is $(\frac{13}{4},5)$.

Question 16

Estimate the solution before solving: $y=0.5x+1$ and $y=-x+10$. Explain why your estimate should have $x$ between $5$ and $7$, then solve by substitution to verify.

Solution: Test each point in both inequalities. For $(4,6)$: $4+6=10\le 10$ and $6\ge 4-2$, so feasible. For $(8,1)$: $8+1=9\le 10$ but $1\not\ge 6$, so not feasible. For $(2,9)$: $2+9=11\not\le 10$, so not feasible.
