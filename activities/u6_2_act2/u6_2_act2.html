Completing the Square
Source: practice_set_6_2_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Partners
Materials: One whiteboard and marker per pair
Structure Fast partner practice
Setup Work with your partner. One whiteboard and one marker between you.
Directions
  1. Work with a partner.
  2. Both students stay engaged.
  3. Alternate who writes.
  4. Write clearly and large.
  5. Stay focused and collaborate.
  6. Fix mistakes and discuss.
Goal: Engagement + quick feedback
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Partners alternate who writes while both stay in the math conversation.
  • The non-writer predicts or checks each step before it is written.
  • Mistakes are fixed on the board with a quick explanation.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One partner holds the marker the entire time.
  • Students write tiny work that cannot be checked quickly.
  • Pairs erase errors without discussing the correction.

Identify the axis of symmetry of $y=(x+2)^2-5$.

Solution:

Vertex form $y=(x-h)^2+k$ has axis $x=h$.

Here $x+2=x-(-2)$, so the axis of symmetry is $x=-2$.

Solution 2
Current · DOK 2

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Completing the Square. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Complete the square to solve $x^2-4x-12=0$.

Solution:

Move the constant: $x^2-4x=12$.

Complete the square by adding $4$ to both sides: $x^2-4x+4=16$.

Then $(x-2)^2=16$, so $x-2=\pm4$ and $x=6$ or $x=-2$.

Solution 3
Current · DOK 3

Structure Analysis. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Completing the Square. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

Analyze $y=(x-4)^2+6$. Without expanding first, decide whether the equation $0=(x-4)^2+6$ has real solutions and justify.

Solution:

Because $(x-4)^2\ge 0$ for every real $x$, $(x-4)^2+6\ge 6$.

The equation $0=(x-4)^2+6$ would require a nonnegative square to equal $-6$, which is impossible over the reals.

So there are no real solutions.

Create With Constraints. Create or revise a quadratic task for Completing the Square using the constraints below.

  • Include at least two representations among equation, table, graph, or context.
  • Make the solution method visible from structure, not from a hint.
  • Interpret the solution in words.

Use the provided graph as one representation. Create a quadratic with vertex $(5,60)$ that opens downward, write it in vertex and standard form, and explain how completing the square connects the two forms.

Solution:

One model is $y=-4(x-5)^2+60$, which opens downward and has vertex $(5,60)$.

Expand to standard form: $y=-4(x^2-10x+25)+60=-4x^2+40x-40$.

Completing the square reverses this process: $-4x^2+40x-40=-4(x^2-10x+10)=-4((x-5)^2-15)=-4(x-5)^2+60$.

1. Current · DOK 1–2

What constant completes the square for $x^2+10x+\_\_\_$?

2. Current · DOK 1–2

Solve by completing the square: $x^2-8x+7=0$.

3. Last · DOK 1–2

Solve: $(x-4)(x+1)=0$.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

For $y=(x-3)^2+2$, name the vertex.

Key:
  1. Take half of $10$ and square it: $(10/2)^2=25$. The constant is $25$.
  2. Move $7$: $x^2-8x=-7$. Add $16$: $(x-4)^2=9$. So $x-4=\pm3$, giving $x=1$ and $x=7$.
  3. Set each factor equal to zero: $x=4$ or $x=-1$.
  4. The vertex form shows vertex $(3,2)$.
