Station 1 Answers

Question 1

A function has constant second differences in an equally spaced table. Name the function family that matches this clue and one graph feature you would expect.

Solution:

Constant second differences in an equally spaced table indicate a quadratic family. A matching graph would be a parabola with a turning point and changing first differences.

Question 2

A context says a starting value increases by $18$ each week. Name the likely model family and the role of $18$.

Solution:

The likely family is linear. The $18$ is the constant rate of change, so the output increases by 18 for every 1 week.

Question 3

For the table, decide whether the better model is linear or exponential. Then write a possible equation using $x=0$ as the starting input.

$x$$0$$1$$2$$3$
$y$$4$$10$$25$$62.5$
Solution:

The ratios are $10/4=2.5$, $25/10=2.5$, and $62.5/25=2.5$, so the relationship is exponential. With starting value 4 and multiplier 2.5, one equation is $y=4(2.5)^x$.

Question 4

The equation $y=4x+3$ and the table are supposed to match. Find the first mismatch, explain how you know, and revise the incorrect value.

$x$$0$$1$$2$$3$
$y$$3$$7$$10$$15$
Solution:

Check the equation: at $x=0$, $y=3$; at $x=1$, $y=7$; at $x=2$, $y=11$. The first mismatch is the table value 10 at $x=2$; revise it to 11.

Question 5

For $f(x)=(x-2)(x+8)$, name the $x$-intercepts.

Solution:

Set each factor equal to zero: $x-2=0$ or $x+8=0$. The $x$-intercepts are $(2,0)$ and $(-8,0)$.

Question 6

Factor $x^2+9x+20$.

Solution:

$4$ and $5$ multiply to 20 and add to 9, so $x^2+9x+20=(x+4)(x+5)$.

Question 7

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Write and solve a factored equation for zeros $-1$ and $8$.

Solution:

Zeros $-1$ and $8$ give factors $(x+1)$ and $(x-8)$. A factored equation is $(x+1)(x-8)=0$, so $x=-1$ or $x=8$.

Question 8

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

The graph shows a quadratic. Estimate the zeros, then write a possible factored equation.

Solution:

From the graph, the zeros are about $x=2$ and $x=6$. A possible factored equation is $y=(x-2)(x-6)$.

Question 9

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Solve $3x^2+12x=0$ and connect each solution to an intercept.

Solution:

Factor: $3x^2+12x=3x(x+4)=0$. Therefore $x=0$ or $x=-4$, giving intercepts $(0,0)$ and $(-4,0)$.

Question 10

Reasonableness/Estimation Argument. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

A model gives solutions $t=-0.2$ and $t=3.4$ seconds. Decide which solution(s) are meaningful and justify using the context and the equation form.

Solution:

In a time context, negative time usually occurs before the modeled start and is not meaningful. The meaningful solution is $t=3.4$ seconds, assuming it represents the event after launch/start.

Question 12

Recommend Between Two Approaches. Create or revise a quadratic task for Solving Quadratics by Factoring using the constraints below.

  • Include at least two representations among equation, table, graph, or context.
  • Make the solution method visible from structure, not from a hint.
  • Interpret the solution in words.

Student A wants to expand $(x-4)(x+9)=0$ first because the final report also needs standard form. Student B wants to use the zero product property first, then expand only after solving. Recommend an order of work, solve, write the standard form, and defend your choice for efficiency and accuracy.

Solution:

Use the zero product property first: $x-4=0$ or $x+9=0$, so $x=4$ or $x=-9$. Then expand for the report: $(x-4)(x+9)=x^2+5x-36$, so the standard form is $x^2+5x-36=0$.

This order is efficient because the factored form already shows the solutions.
