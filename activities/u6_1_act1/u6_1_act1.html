Speed Dating Math
Solving Quadratics by Factoring
Source: practice_set_6_1_2.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Partners, rotating
Materials: Whiteboard or blank paper for each student
Structure Paired practice → Timed rotation → Strategy sharing
Setup Partners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person
Directions
  1. Work with a partner on the projected problem — both students write on their own paper
  2. Compare strategies and methods with your partner before rotating
  3. After 3–4 minutes, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing
  4. Bring your previous work and share your strategy with your new partner
  5. Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings
Goal: Multiple representations + collaborative problem solving
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Both partners write their own attempt before comparing.
  • Rotating students bring their previous method and explain it to the new partner.
  • Pairs name where their answers match or differ before moving on.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One student waits for the other to solve before copying.
  • Students rotate without explaining the strategy they used.
  • Pairs only compare final answers and skip reasoning.

Solution 4
Current · DOK 3

Two-Method Analysis. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Solving Quadratics by Factoring. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

Solve $x^2-8x+7=0$ two ways. Then explain which method is more efficient and why.

Solution:

Factoring: $x^2-8x+7=(x-1)(x-7)$, so $x=1$ or $x=7$.

Quadratic formula: $x=\frac{8\pm\sqrt{64-28}}{2}=\frac{8\pm 6}{2}$, so $x=1$ or $x=7$.

Factoring is more efficient because the trinomial factors quickly into integer factors.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

A table has constant second differences. What function family does that suggest?

Key:
  1. Set each factor equal to zero: $x-5=0$ or $x+2=0$. So $x=5$ or $x=-2$.
  2. Zeros $3$ and $-7$ correspond to factors $(x-3)$ and $(x+7)$. One equation is $y=(x-3)(x+7)$, or $(x-3)(x+7)=0$.
  3. The GCF is $6x$. $12x^2+18x=6x(2x+3)$.
  4. Constant second differences suggest a quadratic function family.
