Graphing Linear Inequalities
Source: practice_set_3_4_3.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Stations (max 4 per station)
Materials: Printed station cards + record sheet
StructureFour station rotation with answer checks after each station
SetupPost four stations around the room. Each station has four questions and an answer page.
Directions
  1. Groups begin at an assigned station and solve the four station questions on a record sheet.
  2. Groups check the station answer page after completing the station.
  3. Groups revise work before rotating to the next station.
  4. Continue until each group has completed all four stations.
Goal: Give students distributed practice with immediate station-level feedback.
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Groups complete all four questions before using the station key.
  • Station records show revisions after checking.
  • Students rotate with materials organized for the next group.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • Groups look at the answer page first.
  • Only one student records while others wait.
  • Station cards are moved or mixed between stations.

Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Does the boundary line belong to the solution set for a strict inequality such as $y\lt x+1$?

Solution:

No. A strict inequality such as $y\lt x+1$ does not include points on the boundary line, so the boundary is dashed.

Question 2

Choose a test point that is not on the boundary of $y\gt -3x+2$. Substitute your point, report whether it satisfies the inequality, and use that result to choose the shaded side.

Solution:

Use $(0,0)$ because it is not on $y=-3x+2$. Substitute: $0\gt -3(0)+2$ gives $0\gt2$, false. Shade the side that does not contain $(0,0)$.

Question 3

Construct a graphing strategy for $4x-2y\le 10$.

Your strategy must include rewriting the boundary, deciding solid or dashed, selecting a test point, and deciding the shaded side.

Solution:

Rewrite $4x-2y\le10$ by subtracting $4x$: $-2y\le10-4x$. Divide by $-2$ and flip the inequality: $y\ge2x-5$. The boundary is solid because of $\ge$. Test $(0,0)$: $0\ge-5$ is true, so shade the side containing $(0,0)$, above the line.

Question 4

A poster claims to model “students need at least 20 total practice problems, with $x$ online problems and $y$ paper problems” using $x+y\lt 20$ and a dashed boundary.

  1. Revise the inequality so it matches the phrase “at least.”
  2. Describe the correct boundary and shading.
  3. Give one point that should be feasible and one point that should not.
Solution:

“At least 20” means $x+y\ge20$, not $x+y\lt20$. The boundary $x+y=20$ should be solid, and the shading should be on or above the line. A feasible point is $(10,10)$; a non-feasible point is $(5,5)$.

Question 5

What family often has a graph shaped like a U?

Solution:

A quadratic family often has a graph shaped like a U.

Question 6

Which family models repeated multiplication by the same factor?

Solution:

An exponential family models repeated multiplication by the same factor.

Question 7

Identify the family: $y=7(0.8)^x$.

Solution:

$y=7(0.8)^x$ is exponential because the input is in the exponent and values are repeatedly multiplied by $0.8$.

Question 8

Quinn has twice as many cards as Denali, then gives away $4$ cards. Quinn now has $18$ cards. Write and solve an equation for how many cards Denali started with.

Solution:

Let $d$ be Denali’s starting cards. Quinn has $2d$, then gives away $4$, so $2d-4=18$. Then $2d=22$ and $d=11$.

Station 3 Answers

Question 9

The equation $12+4s=44$ models a bus situation. Solve for $s$ and write a sentence explaining what the solution means in context.

Solution:

$12+4s=44$ gives $4s=32$, so $s=8$. In context, $8$ is the number of students/stops/units represented by $s$ in the bus situation.

Question 10

Solve $\frac{x}{5}+7=13$ and interpret what the first step does to the equation.

Solution:

$\frac{x}{5}+7=13$ gives $\frac{x}{5}=6$ after subtracting $7$ from both sides. Then multiply by $5$ to get $x=30$. The first step removes the added $7$.

Question 11

Build a strategy for deciding whether a list of ordered pairs represents a function without graphing it. Then apply your strategy to $(0,4),(1,6),(2,8),(1,10)$.

Solution:

Check whether any input repeats with different outputs. For $(0,4),(1,6),(2,8),(1,10)$, input $1$ has outputs $6$ and $10$, so the relation is not a function.

Question 12

A classmate writes the expression $12x+30$ for a rectangle model but cannot explain its structure. Revise the model by giving two equivalent factored forms, choosing the more useful one for finding dimensions, and explaining why.

Solution:

$12x+30$ can be factored as $6(2x+5)$ or $3(4x+10)$. The form $6(2x+5)$ is more useful for dimensions because it shows a greatest common factor of $6$ and a simpler other factor.

Station 4 Answers

Question 13

Identify $m$ and $b$ in $y=-4x+9$.

Solution:

In $y=-4x+9$, $m=-4$ and $b=9$.

Question 14

A line goes through $(0,5)$ and $(3,-1)$. Graph the line, then use the graph to estimate where it crosses the $x$-axis.

Solution:

Slope from $(0,5)$ to $(3,-1)$ is $\frac{-1-5}{3-0}=-2$, so the line is $y=-2x+5$. The $x$-intercept occurs when $0=-2x+5$, so $x=2.5$. It crosses the $x$-axis at about $(2.5,0)$.

Question 15

To compare $y=4x-2$ with the table below, one student converts the equation to a table and another finds the table’s slope and intercept. Compare the methods and choose the more efficient one.

$x$0369
$y$10223446
Solution:

The table has slope $\frac{22-10}{3-0}=4$ and intercept $10$, so its equation is $y=4x+10$. Comparing slope and intercept from the table is more efficient than making a full table for $y=4x-2$, because the intercept already shows a mismatch.

Question 16

Design two lines that intersect at $(2,5)$ but have different slopes. Give equations and explain how the graph would show the intersection.

Solution:

Example lines: $y=x+3$ and $y=2x+1$. Both contain $(2,5)$ because $2+3=5$ and $2(2)+1=5$. The graph would show the two lines crossing at $(2,5)$ with different slopes.
