Quadratic Modeling and Optimization
Source: practice_set_6_5_2.html
Time: 20–30 min
Groups: Groups of 4
Materials: One whiteboard and marker per group
Structure One step per student
Setup Groups of 4. One whiteboard and one marker per group.
Directions
  1. Groups of 4. One whiteboard and marker per group.
  2. Each student completes ONE step, then passes the board.
  3. Rotate until the problem is complete.
  4. Compare when the solution is shown.
  5. If everyone understands, celebrate.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility
✓ Looks Like Success
  • Each student adds one clear step before passing the board.
  • Groups compare their completed solution when the answer page is shown.
  • Students can explain the step they contributed and the next step.
✗ Doesn't Look Like
  • One student completes the whole problem for the group.
  • The board is passed with no explanation of the previous step.
  • Students skip checking because the final answer looks familiar.

Solution 4
Current · DOK 3

Strategy Construction. Analyze the quadratic evidence for Quadratic Modeling and Optimization. Write a conclusion and justify it with algebraic or graphical evidence.

Design a step-by-step solving strategy for $2x^2+9x-5=0$ that avoids unnecessary work. Then solve it.

Solution:

Use factoring because $a c=2(-5)=-10$ and the numbers $10$ and $-1$ add to $9$.

$2x^2+10x-x-5=0$, so $2x(x+5)-1(x+5)=0$.

$(2x-1)(x+5)=0$, so $x=\frac12$ or $x=-5$. This avoids completing the square or the formula.

Identify why $x^2+25$ is not a difference of squares over the integers, even though both terms are squares.

Solution:

$x^2+25$ is a sum of squares, so it does not factor over the integers using the difference-of-squares pattern.

Then factor the difference of squares: $8(x-3)(x+3)$.

4. Mastery · DOK 1–2

A quadratic has zeros $-2$ and $4$. Write a factored expression.

Key:
  1. $h(0)$ is the height when time is $0$. Here $h(0)=5$, so it represents the ball’s initial height.
  2. A downward-opening quadratic has its vertex at the highest point. So the vertex gives a maximum.
  3. Factoring is efficient: $x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$. So the solutions are $x=2$ and $x=3$.
  4. Zeros $-2$ and $4$ give factors $(x+2)$ and $(x-4)$. A factored expression is $(x+2)(x-4)$.
