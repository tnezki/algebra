Teacher & Student Moves Guide

Section 8.4

Use these moves across the activity options.
I-Can Moves

I Can 1: I can formulate a mathematical model from real-world data and state the assumptions used.

Teacher Moves

  • Press for evidence from the representation, not a guess.
  • Ask students to name the feature that makes the conclusion reasonable.

Student Moves

  • Point to specific graph/table/context evidence.
  • State why the chosen model or representation fits.

I Can 2: I can extend a model to make predictions within a reasonable domain.

Teacher Moves

  • Press for evidence from the representation, not a guess.
  • Ask students to name the feature that makes the conclusion reasonable.

Student Moves

  • Point to specific graph/table/context evidence.
  • State why the chosen model or representation fits.

I Can 3: I can critique how well a model fits data, constraints, and the situation being studied.

Teacher Moves

  • Press for evidence from the representation, not a guess.
  • Ask students to name the feature that makes the conclusion reasonable.

Student Moves

  • Point to specific graph/table/context evidence.
  • State why the chosen model or representation fits.
Participation-Structure Moves

Stations

Teacher Moves: Monitor for different strategies and misconceptions; select useful work to connect later.

Student Moves: Record reasoning on the separate record sheet and be ready to explain a strategy.

Find Someone Who

Teacher Moves: Require explanation/checking, not signatures alone.

Student Moves: Compare work, resolve one disagreement, and record reasoning before moving on.

Rally Coach / Showdown / Think, Trade, Agree / Speed Dating

Teacher Moves: Preserve independent think time, then press for comparison of methods and evidence.

Student Moves: Explain, question, compare, and revise rather than copy.

Activity Directions

Whiteboard Indy

Modeling Investigation
Set 1 · Set 2
Structure: Individual → Independent practice
Setup: Each student gets a whiteboard and one marker.
Directions:
  • Notes are welcome.
  • Try something before asking for help.
  • Write large and clearly.
  • Fix mistakes and discuss what changed.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry
Looks like success

Students show mathematical work, explain decisions, and use the partner/group structure without handing off the thinking.

Does not look like

Copying answers, skipping the reasoning, or treating movement/rotation as the goal.
