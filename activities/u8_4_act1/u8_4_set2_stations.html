Station 1 Answers

Question 1

A residual is negative. State whether the model overpredicted or underpredicted the actual value.

Solution:

A residual is actual minus predicted. If it is negative, the actual value is below the prediction, so the model overpredicted.

Question 2

Use the scatterplot and line of best fit to estimate the output when $x=7$. Then say whether the prediction is interpolation or extrapolation.

Solution:

Read the trend line at $x=7$; it is about 25 miles. Since $x=7$ is within the data range, the prediction is interpolation.

Question 3

A student fits a linear model to data whose residuals are negative, then positive, then negative as $x$ increases. Critique the model choice and name a model family that might be better.

Solution:

That curved residual pattern suggests a linear model is missing structure. A quadratic or other curved model may fit better because residuals should be more randomly scattered around 0.

Question 4

Use the attendance scatterplot to synthesize a short modeling response: choose a likely family, state one assumption, and predict whether attendance will keep rising above $95$ F.

Solution:

A reasonable response is that attendance rises then levels off or drops at very high temperatures, so a quadratic or curved model may fit. The assumption is that temperature is a meaningful predictor.

Above 95 F, attendance may not keep rising because heat can reduce attendance.

Question 5

Explain one clue that an equation is ready for zero product property.

Solution:

An equation is ready for the zero product property when one side is 0 and the other side is written as a product of factors.

Question 6

Explain one clue that completing the square will reveal a vertex quickly.

Solution:

Completing the square is useful when a quadratic is near vertex form or when the leading coefficient and middle term make a perfect-square trinomial easy to create.

Question 7

State one advantage of the quadratic formula.

Solution:

The quadratic formula works for any quadratic equation and can show whether the solutions are real through the discriminant.

Question 9

Solve or interpret the quadratic situation for Quadratic Modeling and Optimization. Show the algebra that supports your answer.

Write a model for revenue with zeros at prices $0$ and $12$ and revenue $72$ at $p=6$.

Solution:

Use $R(p)=a p(p-12)$. Since $R(6)=72$, $72=a(6)(-6)=-36a$, so $a=-2$.

Thus $R(p)=-2p(p-12)$.

Question 12

Synthesize Across Representations. Create or revise a quadratic task for Quadratic Formula using the constraints below.

  • Include at least two representations among equation, table, graph, or context.
  • Make the solution method visible from structure, not from a hint.
  • Interpret the solution in words.

Synthesize a coefficient map, a discriminant statement, and a graph-sketch description for $2x^2+4x+5=0$. Explain why all three indicate no real solutions.

Solution:

For $2x^2+4x+5=0$, $a=2,b=4,c=5$. The discriminant is $4^2-4(2)(5)=16-40=-24$, which is negative.

The graph opens upward and stays above the $x$-axis, so there are no real solutions.

Question 14

The table shows the value of a collectible.

Year0123
Value$\$80$$\$96$$\$115.20$$\$138.24$

Write an exponential equation and use it to predict the value in year 6.

Solution:

The multiplier is $96/80=1.2$, so $V(t)=80(1.2)^t$. Year 6 is $80(1.2)^6\approx\$238.88$.

Question 15

A student claims that $y=2x^2$ will always outgrow $y=2(2)^x$ because quadratics have an exponent of 2. Critique the claim using at least three input values and a conclusion about long-run behavior.

Solution:

The claim is false. At $x=2$, both are 8; at $x=4$, quadratic gives 32 and exponential gives 32; at $x=6$, quadratic gives 72 while exponential gives 128.

Eventually the exponential outgrows the quadratic.
