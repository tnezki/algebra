Rules & Structures

Structures that reveal features

  • Greatest common factor: factor the largest shared coefficient and variable power from every term, then verify by multiplication.
  • Product-sum reasoning: for a trinomial, choose factors whose product and sum rebuild the original coefficients.
  • Difference of squares: $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$.
  • Factored form reveals zeros and horizontal intercepts; vertex form reveals the vertex; standard form is useful for expansion and the vertical intercept.

Factor Out Common Factors

Back to top
Mastery Goal

Factor polynomial expressions by identifying a greatest common factor and use multiplication to verify equivalent forms.

I can...

  • I can construct equivalent polynomial expressions by factoring out a greatest common factor.
  • I can apply structure to rewrite polynomial expressions in a more useful form.
  • I can determine whether a rewritten expression is reasonable by checking common factors and equivalent products.

What to remember

  • Use structure before choosing a procedure.
  • Make the feature you need visible in the form you choose.
  • Verify equivalence or interpret the result back in the original representation.

Useful representations

  • Polynomial expressions in expanded form
  • Factored expressions with greatest common factors
  • Area models for common-factor structure
  • Tables of term coefficients and variable powers
  • Verbal contexts involving shared quantities

Common traps

Watch for

Factoring out a quantity that is not common to every term.

Watch for

Failing to verify the factored form by multiplication.

Final check

Before you call yourself ready

Factor accurately

  • Take out a GCF first when one exists.
  • Check products and sums.
  • Recognize special patterns.

Connect algebra and graphs

  • Set each factor equal to zero.
  • Interpret zeros as horizontal intercepts.
  • Use form to reveal the needed feature.

Explain your thinking

  • Verify equivalent forms.
  • Name the feature that supports your choice.
  • Use a graph, table, or context when it adds evidence.
